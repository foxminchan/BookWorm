## Context

BookWorm applications need a consistent way to authenticate customers,
administrators, and business users without coupling business services to
provider-managed credentials or identity records. The provider is an external
system, while BookWorm services own their domain data and operations. Allowing
the provider to own business records or application permissions would blur
that boundary and couple domain behavior to an external identity policy.

## Decision

The [[system|identity-provider]] owns user authentication, provider-side identity
records, authentication policy, and issuance of OAuth-compatible access
credentials for the requesting client and audience. BookWorm applications
delegate sign-in to the provider and consume the resulting identity evidence
when a client calls a protected API.

The provider is not the system of record for [[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], or
[[domain|reviews]] data. Its credentials and identity claims establish who is calling;
they do not transfer ownership of BookWorm data or decide what business action
the caller may perform.

## Alternatives considered

- **Store and verify application credentials in each BookWorm service.** This
  would duplicate credential handling and authentication policy across
  services, increasing the number of systems that must protect credentials
  and implement sign-in consistently.
- **Make the provider the owner of BookWorm business accounts and permissions.**
  This would couple domain data and application-specific policy to an external
  identity boundary. The provider remains authoritative only for its own
  identity records and authentication policy.

## Consequences

BookWorm can delegate authentication without transferring domain ownership to
the provider, and application services can evolve their business models
independently of provider-side account storage. Clients and APIs depend on the
provider's availability and token contract, so the integration boundary must
be explicit and its failures observable. Each consuming API remains
responsible for validating identity evidence before processing a protected
request. Provider-side account changes do not by themselves define the
lifecycle or ownership of BookWorm business records; applications must keep
those concerns within their own domains.
