This chapter defines the strategic baseline for the complete BookWorm online bookstore platform—its business purpose, eight essential system requirements, five prioritized quality goals, and key stakeholder roles. Use it to align product, architecture, engineering, and operations decisions before diving into individual bounded contexts or deployment details.

## Scope and architectural baseline

- **System under documentation:** The full BookWorm platform—three Next.js 16 web applications (**Storefront**, **Commerce**, and **Backoffice**) behind a YARP **API Gateway**, backed by ten .NET 11 services across six bounded contexts (**Catalog**, **Shopping**, **Ordering**, **Reviews**, **Conversational**, and **Platform**) orchestrated with **Aspire** 17.
- **Architectural priority order:** When trade-offs arise, BookWorm prioritizes qualities in descending order: (1) **Correctness**, (2) **Interoperability**, (3) **Deployability**, (4) **Modifiability**, and (5) **Operability**.
- **Baseline boundaries:** This chapter synthesizes requirements and quality priorities from the live codebase and architecture baseline. It does not replace a stakeholder-approved product backlog or formal production service-level agreements (SLAs).

## Key principles established in this chapter

- **Single authoritative owner per domain record:** **Catalog** owns product facts and pricing; **Shopping** owns mutable pre-order baskets; **Ordering** owns accepted order lifecycles; **Reviews** owns individual `Feedback` records; **Conversational** provides grounded AI discovery; **Platform** supplies enabling infrastructure without owning business state.
- **Explicit gaps over implied commitments:** Where numerical latency, availability, or recovery budgets and named approver contacts are not yet formally signed off, this chapter labels them as proposed rather than claiming operational guarantees.
