## 1. Context & Problem

BookWorm is an online bookstore and commerce platform supporting book discovery, basket building, checkout, order lifecycle management, customer reviews, conversational assistance, and shared platform capabilities.

Because the platform spans multiple autonomous capabilities, teams need a single starting point that answers foundational questions before diving into individual services: _Why does the platform exist? What must it do at a system level? Which architectural qualities win when trade-offs arise? Who relies on this architecture?_ Without that shared baseline, local service decisions can drift away from end-to-end business goals.

This chapter establishes that platform-wide baseline. It describes the complete BookWorm platform rather than an individual service or deployment unit, leaving detailed responsibilities and contracts in the relevant catalog entries.

## 2. Business Value

- **Coherent customer journey**: Aligns every capability around a seamless path from discovering books to completing checkout and managing accepted orders.
- **Trustworthy business data**: Protects the accuracy and clear ownership of product facts and order records across capability handoffs.
- **Clear domain autonomy**: Enables Catalog, Shopping, Ordering, Reviews, Conversational, and Platform to evolve independently without blurring responsibilities.
- **Safer cross-team collaboration**: Encourages changes through explicit, compatible agreements rather than entangled shared state.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Architecture synthesis vs. formal backlog**: The requirements and quality goals in this chapter synthesize the current catalog at a system level; they do not replace an approved product backlog or service-level acceptance criteria.
- **Explicit gaps over implied commitments**: The repository does not currently define stakeholder-approved, system-wide numerical service-level targets or named stakeholder contacts. Calling out those gaps explicitly avoids treating architectural goals as formal operational commitments before stakeholders agree on them.
- **Separation of concerns in documentation**: Keeping strategic intent in Chapter 1 while placing external boundaries, constraints, and solution strategies in dedicated chapters keeps each document concise and easier to maintain.
