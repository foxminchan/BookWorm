BookWorm ranks its top five architectural quality goals in strict priority order: **Correctness**, **Interoperability**, **Deployability**, **Modifiability**, and **Operability**. These scenario-based goals guide design trade-offs across all six bounded contexts; detailed measurable criteria and additional qualities (reliability, security, performance, usability) live in [[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements]].

:::note
These five priorities represent the architectural baseline derived from BookWorm's domain invariants and codebase practices. Numerical latency, throughput, availability, and recovery budgets remain proposed pending formal stakeholder sign-off.
:::

## Governing trade-offs

- **Correctness over convenience:** Checkout never trusts client-supplied book prices or bypasses **Catalog** validation to reduce latency.
- **Explicit contracts over shared state:** Separate persistence across **Catalog**, **Shopping**, **Ordering**, **Reviews**, **Conversational**, and **Platform** adds contract maintenance overhead, but keeps dependencies visible and independently testable.
- **Delivery state is not business completion:** Background job failures in **Scheduler**, retry exhaustion in **Finance**, or SMTP errors in **Notification** (a stateless Kafka worker with Redis idempotency and a native DLQ) are operational telemetry—they do not silently rewrite an accepted `Order`'s business state.
- **Current verification gap:** Automated unit tests (9 projects), Pact messaging contract tests (7 projects), architecture tests (`BookWorm.ArchTests`), and frontend unit/Playwright BDD tests exist in the repository, while backend `.IntegrationTests` projects are not yet implemented in `BookWorm.slnx`.
