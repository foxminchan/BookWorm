The following five goals are a proposed priority order derived from BookWorm's business invariants and documented architecture practices. They are architecture goals, not project milestones. Scenarios make the intent testable, but the repository does not yet define stakeholder-approved numerical targets or service-level objectives; those remain to be agreed.

## Prioritized quality scenarios

| Priority | Quality goal         | Scenario and expected response                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Acceptance evidence                                                                                                              |
| -------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | **Correctness**      | Given a customer checks out, the system reads the persisted [[entity\|basket]] and current [[domain\|catalog]] facts. A missing book prevents order creation; an accepted checkout creates one [[entity\|order]], whose lifecycle is then owned by [[domain\|ordering]]. | Checkout tests verify persisted quantities, Catalog-derived pricing, rejection of missing books, and a single accepted order.    |
| 2        | **Interoperability** | Given a capability needs information or behavior from another context, it uses an explicit contract with a clear owner. A contract change is reviewed for compatibility before dependent consumers adopt it.                                                             | Contract documentation and compatibility checks expose the producer, consumer, semantics, and breaking changes.                  |
| 3        | **Deployability**    | Given a change is confined to one context and its contracts remain compatible, that context can be tested and released without requiring unrelated contexts to release in lockstep.                                                                                      | Deployment and integration checks demonstrate that consumers continue to work across independently released compatible versions. |
| 4        | **Modifiability**    | Given a business rule changes, the responsible context can change that rule without modifying another context's database or moving ownership of its data.                                                                                                                | Domain ownership, service boundaries, and architecture fitness checks make the change location and affected contracts explicit.  |
| 5        | **Operability**      | Given a scheduled job or email delivery attempt fails, operators can identify the responsible capability and inspect relevant execution or delivery state without treating delivery status as business completion.                                                       | Operational documentation and observable job/outbox state distinguish processing failures from business outcomes.                |

## Trade-offs and measurable targets

Explicit contracts and separate ownership can add coordination and integration work compared with direct shared-state access. That cost is intentional: it makes dependencies visible and protects the meaning of [[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], [[domain|reviews]], [[domain|conversational]], and [[domain|platform]] data.

The scenarios above define what should be verified, not a guarantee of a particular latency, throughput, availability, recovery time, or retention period. Those quantitative budgets and their measurement conditions must be agreed with the relevant stakeholders before they can be treated as acceptance targets.
