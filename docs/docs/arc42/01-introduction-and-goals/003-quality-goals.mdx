## 1. Context & Problem

In an evolving commerce platform, engineering and product teams regularly face trade-offs between shipping quickly, isolating failures, changing business rules, and maintaining integrations. Without an agreed priority order for system qualities, teams can make conflicting local decisions—such as bypassing checkout validation for convenience or directly reaching into another capability's records.

These five quality goals establish a proposed priority order derived from BookWorm's business invariants and documented architecture practices. They act as testable architectural goals rather than project milestones. Note that the repository does not yet define stakeholder-approved numerical targets or service-level objectives; those quantitative budgets remain to be agreed.

## 2. Business Value

- **Customer and revenue protection (Priority 1 — Correctness)**: Ensures checkout always relies on saved basket intent and current book availability, preventing invalid or duplicate orders.
- **Safe collaboration across teams (Priority 2 — Interoperability)**: Requires clear ownership and compatibility review whenever capabilities exchange information, reducing surprise integration breakages.
- **Independent delivery speed (Priority 3 — Deployability)**: Lets one capability ship compatible improvements without forcing unrelated capabilities into lockstep releases.
- **Controlled cost of change (Priority 4 — Modifiability)**: Keeps business-rule updates localized to the owning capability rather than sprawling across multiple teams' data.
- **Faster incident resolution (Priority 5 — Operability)**: Enables operations teams to pinpoint which capability owns a delayed job or notification failure without misreading delivery delays as failed purchases.

## 3. How It Works

Each quality goal is expressed as a concrete scenario describing a stimulus and the expected system behavior, ordered from highest to lowest priority:

1. **Correctness**: Checkout accuracy and single-order ownership take precedence over all other qualities.
2. **Interoperability**: Capabilities communicate only through documented, reviewed agreements.
3. **Deployability**: Compatible changes inside one capability release independently.
4. **Modifiability**: Business rule changes remain confined to the responsible capability.
5. **Operability**: Operational delays or failures are observable and attributable without distorting business outcomes.

### Prioritized quality scenarios

| Priority | Quality goal         | Scenario and expected response                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | What it protects                                                                                               |
| -------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | **Correctness**      | Given a customer checks out, the system reads the persisted [[entity\|basket]] and current [[domain\|catalog]] facts. A missing book prevents order creation; an accepted checkout creates one [[entity\|order]], whose lifecycle is then owned by [[domain\|ordering]]. | Purchase accuracy, valid pricing, and unambiguous single ownership of accepted orders.                         |
| 2        | **Interoperability** | Given a capability needs information or behavior from another context, it uses an explicit agreement with a clear owner. A change is reviewed for compatibility before dependent consumers adopt it.                                                                     | Clear producer/consumer accountability and safe evolution of cross-capability interactions.                    |
| 3        | **Deployability**    | Given a change is confined to one context and its agreements remain compatible, that context can be tested and released without requiring unrelated contexts to release in lockstep.                                                                                     | Release independence and reduced cross-team deployment coordination.                                           |
| 4        | **Modifiability**    | Given a business rule changes, the responsible context can change that rule without modifying another context's internal records or moving ownership of its data.                                                                                                        | Bounded-context autonomy and localized impact of business rule changes.                                        |
| 5        | **Operability**      | Given a scheduled task or email delivery attempt fails, operators can identify the responsible capability and inspect relevant processing or delivery status without treating delivery status as business completion.                                                    | Fast root-cause attribution and clear separation between technical delivery and business transaction outcomes. |

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Coordination effort vs. data integrity**: Using explicit contracts and separate ownership across [[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], [[domain|reviews]], [[domain|conversational]], and [[domain|platform]] adds design and review overhead compared with direct shared-state access. BookWorm accepts that cost intentionally so dependencies stay visible and business data retains clear meaning.
- **Delivery state is not business completion**: Treating background job or email delivery failures as operational telemetry rather than redefining business outcomes prevents confusion during incidents.
- **Missing quantitative budgets**: These scenarios define _what_ must be verified, not specific latency, throughput, availability, recovery time, or data retention numbers. Stakeholders must agree on those numerical budgets and measurement conditions before treating them as formal acceptance commitments.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Acceptance evidence by quality goal

| Priority | Quality goal         | Acceptance evidence and verification mechanisms                                                                                  |
| -------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | **Correctness**      | Checkout tests verify persisted quantities, Catalog-derived pricing, rejection of missing books, and a single accepted order.    |
| 2        | **Interoperability** | Contract documentation and compatibility checks expose the producer, consumer, semantics, and breaking changes.                  |
| 3        | **Deployability**    | Deployment and integration checks demonstrate that consumers continue to work across independently released compatible versions. |
| 4        | **Modifiability**    | Domain ownership, service boundaries, and architecture fitness checks make the change location and affected contracts explicit.  |
| 5        | **Operability**      | Operational documentation and observable job/outbox state distinguish processing failures from business outcomes.                |
