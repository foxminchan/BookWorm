## 1. Context & Problem

BookWorm brings together several distinct business capabilities—book discovery, shopping, ordering, reviews, conversational assistance, and shared platform operations. Without a clear, system-level summary of what the platform must deliver and how those capabilities relate, teams risk making conflicting assumptions about data authority, checkout rules, or cross-capability dependencies.

This page synthesizes BookWorm's essential customer capabilities and architectural drivers from the current domain model and checkout flow. The `BW-R` identifiers (`BW-R1` through `BW-R8`) serve as local reference labels for this overview; they are not items from an approved product backlog, and detailed behavior and acceptance criteria live in the owning domain, service, and flow documentation.

## 2. Business Value

- **Purchase integrity**: Ensures customers only check out against current, authoritative book details and availability, preventing invalid orders and clarifying exactly when an accepted order becomes Ordering's responsibility.
- **Reliable supporting experiences**: Connects customer feedback to books without duplicating product ownership, and grounds conversational assistance in accurate Catalog facts without overpromising access to live basket or order data.
- **Safer, faster change delivery**: Independent ownership and explicit integration agreements let capabilities evolve without forcing unrelated teams to coordinate releases or share mutable state.
- **End-to-end traceability**: Gives product managers, business analysts, solution architects, and developers a shared map connecting customer needs to architectural boundaries.

## 3. How It Works

BookWorm's essential requirements fall into two complementary groups: customer-facing and supporting capabilities (`BW-R1` to `BW-R6`), and architectural rules that protect capability boundaries (`BW-R7` to `BW-R8`).

A central seam in the customer journey is **checkout**: it combines the customer's saved [[entity|basket]] with current [[domain|catalog]] facts, blocks order creation if a book is unavailable, and transfers lifecycle ownership to [[domain|ordering]] only after acceptance. Supporting capabilities—[[domain|reviews]], [[domain|conversational]], and [[domain|platform]]—enhance that journey while preserving their own boundaries.

### Essential requirements

| ID    | Capability or driver             | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                           | Why it matters                                                                                          |
| ----- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| BW-R1 | Book discovery                   | Customers can find books and receive current product information from [[domain\|catalog]], the owner of book facts, pricing, and availability.                                        | Discovery and purchase decisions rely on authoritative product information.                             |
| BW-R2 | Basket and checkout              | [[domain\|shopping]] maintains a customer-scoped [[entity\|basket]]. Checkout uses the persisted basket and current Catalog information; an unavailable book prevents order creation. | The submitted purchase must reflect saved customer intent and valid product data.                       |
| BW-R3 | Order lifecycle                  | A successful checkout creates one accepted [[entity\|order]], after which [[domain\|ordering]] owns its lifecycle and fulfillment state.                                              | Order state must have a clear owner beyond the point of purchase.                                       |
| BW-R4 | Customer feedback                | Customers can submit [[entity\|feedback]] associated with a book, while [[domain\|reviews]] does not take ownership of Catalog's book data.                                           | Feedback must remain connected to products without blurring context ownership.                          |
| BW-R5 | Conversational assistance        | Customers can receive Catalog-grounded book discovery and store-policy assistance. The experience must not imply live Basket or Order access where no such integration exists.        | Assistance should be useful without inventing product facts or overstating its access to customer data. |
| BW-R6 | Shared platform capabilities     | Scheduled processing and email notification delivery support business capabilities without changing their business state or taking ownership of their data.                           | Shared infrastructure must remain enabling rather than becoming an implicit business owner.             |
| BW-R7 | Independent capability evolution | Each bounded context keeps ownership of its business behavior and data; compatible changes should not require unrelated contexts to share mutable state or release together.          | Explicit boundaries limit hidden dependencies and reduce the impact of change.                          |
| BW-R8 | Explicit integration             | Cross-context communication uses documented, owned contracts, with compatibility checked when contracts evolve.                                                                       | Teams need to understand the meaning, ownership, and change impact of each integration.                 |

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Explicit contracts vs. ad-hoc coupling**: Enforcing independent ownership (`BW-R7`) and explicit, compatibility-checked integrations (`BW-R8`) introduces more coordination when designing cross-context interactions, but avoids fragile shared state and makes the impact of changes visible upfront.
- **Scoped AI assistance over unverified claims**: Limiting conversational assistance (`BW-R5`) to Catalog-grounded product facts and store policy prevents misleading customers about live basket or order state where integrations do not exist.
- **Identified documentation gaps**: No system-wide product backlog, formal acceptance criteria for every row, or approved performance, availability, recovery, and data retention targets are currently established in the architecture documentation. Product and service owners should refine these requirements and record measurable targets where needed.
