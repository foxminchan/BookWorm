## 1. Context & Problem

As a multi-capability commerce platform grows, stakeholders often hold different mental models of how the system is put together. Without a clear structural map, architects, project managers, analysts, and developers struggle to see where one capability ends and another begins, which teams own which data, and where complex coordination lives.

The Building Block View solves this by presenting BookWorm's static structure as a progressive hierarchy—moving from the whole platform down to specific capabilities and complex coordination components—before diving into runtime sequences or deployment details.

## 2. Business Value

- **Shared language across roles:** Gives architects, project managers, business analysts, and engineers a single structural map for scoping features and estimating impact.
- **Clear ownership boundaries:** Makes it obvious which capability owns each business concept and data store, reducing duplicate logic and cross-team friction.
- **Faster onboarding and review:** Helps new contributors and reviewers locate responsibilities quickly without having to reverse-engineer the entire codebase first.

## 3. How It Works

The Building Block View uses three zoom levels so readers can inspect only the level of detail they need:

1. **Level 1 — Platform Overview:** Opens the entire BookWorm platform as a single white box in [[doc|arc42/05-building-block-view/001-whitebox-overall-system]], showing the six business capabilities, the shared foundation, their responsibilities, and their main interactions.
2. **Level 2 — Capability Internals:** Zooms into Catalog, Shopping, Ordering, Reviews, Conversational, and Platform in [[doc|arc42/05-building-block-view/002-level-2]] to reveal their internal services and dedicated data stores.
3. **Level 3 — Deep Dive on Checkout Coordination:** Zooms into the multi-step checkout coordination component inside Ordering in [[doc|arc42/05-building-block-view/003-level-3]], showing how it tracks progress and coordinates outcomes across Ordering, Shopping, and Platform Notification.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Selective refinement over exhaustive diagrams:** Not every capability is expanded to Level 3. Level 2 refines capabilities where multiple modules, distinct data ownership, or asynchronous coordination matter, and Level 3 zooms into only the single coordination block whose cross-capability workflow warrants extra clarity.
- **Structure separated from runtime and deployment:** This chapter deliberately captures static building blocks rather than deployment topologies or step-by-step runtime sequences, keeping ownership boundaries easy to read. Neighboring systems and technical mechanisms are covered separately in [[doc|arc42/03-context-and-scope/003-technical-context]], and guiding design strategies live in [[doc|arc42/04-solution-strategy]].
