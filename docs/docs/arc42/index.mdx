## 1. Context & Problem

As a multi-capability platform grows, architectural knowledge easily becomes fragmented across individual services, codebases, and team conventions. Solution architects, project managers, business analysts, and developers need a single system-wide entry point that explains how BookWorm fits together as a whole—from business goals and boundaries down to runtime flows, operational assumptions, and open risks—without having to piece together every individual service specification first.

This overview frames BookWorm at the whole-system level rather than focusing on a single business domain, service, or deployment unit.

## 2. Business Value

Organizing whole-system architecture documentation around a standardized structure provides immediate practical value:

- **Faster cross-team alignment (SA, PM, BA)**: Gives stakeholders a consistent map of business capabilities, ownership boundaries, and quality goals for roadmap planning and impact assessment.
- **Clarity between fact and intent**: Explicitly distinguishes confirmed system behavior from provisional targets or open questions, preventing unfounded assumptions in delivery estimates or operational commitments.
- **Predictable evolution (Engineering)**: Grounds individual service changes in system-wide constraints, engineering standards, and cross-cutting rules so independent capabilities evolve without breaking one another.

## 3. How It Works

BookWorm is structured around clear business capabilities rather than a single shared application model. At the system level, the operating model connects six complementary areas:

- **Product truth and discovery**: Maintains authoritative book details, pricing, and availability.
- **Pre-purchase shopping**: Captures mutable customer basket selections and turns them into checkout requests.
- **Order management**: Accepts checkout requests and owns the order lifecycle after a purchase is confirmed.
- **Customer feedback**: Captures individual book ratings and reviews while referencing catalog book identities without duplicating book ownership.
- **Conversational assistance**: Provides AI-assisted customer guidance and discovery using explicit, controlled access to product and order information.
- **Shared platform capabilities**: Handles cross-cutting needs such as notifications and scheduled background work without owning any business area's core records.

The 12 architecture chapters guide readers progressively—from business intent and constraints, through system scope, solution strategy, structural building blocks, runtime flows, and deployment considerations, to cross-cutting concepts, key architectural decisions, quality requirements, risks, and shared terminology.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Capability boundaries over a single shared model**: Separating BookWorm into distinct business contexts keeps ownership clear and prevents unintended coupling, at the cost of explicit coordination across boundaries.
- **Transparency over false completeness**: Where the repository does not yet state a formal requirement, target, or external interface, the architecture documentation marks it as an architectural intent or an item to confirm rather than presenting it as settled fact.
- **Whole-system map vs. service-level depth**: This overview summarizes system-wide relationships and narratives; detailed service contracts, schemas, and implementation specifics remain in their respective catalog entries.

### Architectural Framing

BookWorm is intentionally modeled as a bounded-context system rather than a monolith with a single shared model. The primary decomposition is by business capability and ownership:

| Context        | Responsibility                                               | Primary relationship                                       |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Catalog        | Discoverability, pricing, and availability                   | Source of truth for product facts                          |
| Shopping       | Basket and pre-order purchase intent                         | Produces accepted checkout requests                        |
| Ordering       | Accepted order lifecycle                                     | Owns order state after purchase                            |
| Reviews        | Feedback and ratings                                         | Consumes product identities without owning them            |
| Conversational | AI-assisted guidance and discovery                           | Reads product and order state through explicit contracts   |
| Platform       | Notifications, scheduled processing, and shared capabilities | Supports other contexts without owning their business data |

This narrative is intentionally supported by the catalog's domain and service metadata. Where the repository does not state a formal requirement, target, or external interface, the chapters identify it as an architectural intent or an item to confirm rather than treating it as settled fact.
