## 1. Context & Problem

In a modular platform spanning shopping, ordering, catalog management, customer reviews, conversational AI, and shared infrastructure, everyday words like "order," "basket," "rating," or "checkout" can easily take on conflicting meanings across teams. Without a shared vocabulary, business analysts may assume a basket reserves inventory, product managers may expect checkout acceptance to mean payment is settled, or engineering teams may duplicate authoritative records across boundaries.

This glossary establishes a single, platform-wide set of definitions that clarifies both what key terms mean and which business area owns the underlying truth.

## 2. Business Value

A shared glossary reduces costly misunderstandings between business, architecture, and delivery teams:

- **Clear ownership (SA & PM)**: Makes it unmistakable which capability owns each business record—such as separating pre-purchase shopping intent from an accepted order, or individual customer reviews from an aggregate book rating.
- **Accurate requirements and expectations (BA)**: Prevents flawed assumptions around real-time consistency, inventory reservation, payment settlement, or workflow guarantees.
- **Faster onboarding and safer integration (Engineering)**: Gives teams consistent terminology when designing boundaries, interpreting system behavior, and translating between business areas.

## 3. How It Works

BookWorm's shared vocabulary is structured around two foundational principles:

1. **Clear Business Ownership**: Each core business concept belongs to one primary area—its **source of truth**. For example, the shopping area manages a customer's mutable basket before purchase, while the ordering area takes over once a checkout request is accepted as an order. Similarly, the reviews area owns individual customer feedback, while the catalog area maintains canonical book details and displays a summarized rating view derived from that feedback. Supporting areas—such as conversational AI assistance and shared platform utilities—access or coordinate work without taking ownership of those core business records.
2. **Explicit Boundaries Over Shared State**: Independent business areas do not reach directly into each other's internal records. Instead, they communicate through agreed boundaries, translate external concepts into their own language when needed, and publish facts about completed actions so other areas can update derived views asynchronously. Because derived views update in the background, they may briefly trail the authoritative source.

Each individual business area may also define more detailed, context-specific terms in its own documentation; within that area, those local definitions remain authoritative.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **System-wide consistency vs. local precision**: A global glossary prevents cross-team ambiguity, while allowing each business area to maintain specialized local terminology avoids forcing an unnatural one-size-fits-all vocabulary.
- **Explicit conceptual separation vs. conversational shorthand**: Distinguishing between a basket, checkout acceptance, order status, payment settlement, and fulfillment adds rigor to requirements and specifications, but avoids expensive rework when edge cases occur.
- **Definitions must reflect real boundaries**: Terms such as "idempotency" and "eventual consistency" are intentionally scoped in plain caveats so stakeholders understand what guarantees exist—and what is not guaranteed—without overpromising.

## 5. Technical Reference

This glossary defines terms used across the BookWorm architecture and clarifies distinctions that affect ownership and system behavior. Each bounded context may use additional terms or more specific meanings in its own documentation; those local definitions remain authoritative within that context.

| Term                             | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Anti-corruption layer**        | An adapter that translates another context's model into the terms used by its consumer. [[domain\|conversational]] uses one when presenting [[domain\|ordering]] data, rather than adopting Ordering's internal model.                                              |
| **Basket**                       | [[domain\|shopping]]-owned, mutable customer shopping intent ([[entity\|basket]]). A Basket is not an [[entity\|order]] and does not reserve [[domain\|catalog]] availability.                                                                                      |
| **Bounded context**              | A boundary within which a domain model and its language have defined meaning and ownership. Contexts exchange information through explicit contracts rather than sharing business state.                                                                            |
| **Catalog**                      | The context ([[domain\|catalog]]) that owns canonical [[entity\|book]] information, pricing, and availability. Search data and the book rating summary are derived views, not independent sources of truth.                                                         |
| **Checkout**                     | The request to turn a persisted [[entity\|basket]] into a purchase. [[domain\|ordering]] validates it using current [[domain\|catalog]] facts and, on acceptance, persists one [[entity\|order]]; this does not mean payment settlement or fulfillment is complete. |
| **Conversational**               | The context ([[domain\|conversational]]) providing AI-assisted customer interaction. It accesses business information through explicit tools and contracts but does not own the underlying [[domain\|catalog]] or [[entity\|order]] records.                        |
| **Eventual consistency**         | A state in which an asynchronously updated view may temporarily lag its source and converge after processing. It does not imply a fixed update time.                                                                                                                |
| **Feedback**                     | An individual customer review or rating record ([[entity\|feedback]]) owned by [[domain\|reviews]]. It is distinct from the aggregate rating summary displayed for a book.                                                                                          |
| **Finance orchestration**        | BookWorm's post-checkout coordination service ([[service\|order-orchestration]]). It coordinates work such as [[entity\|basket]] cleanup and notifications; it is not payment processing and does not own [[entity\|order]] status.                                 |
| **Idempotency**                  | Handling repeated requests or messages so retries do not unintentionally repeat an operation's effects. The guarantee depends on the specific operation and implementation; it does not mean the whole workflow executes exactly once.                              |
| **Integration event**            | A published fact about something that happened in its producer's context. Consumers can react asynchronously, but the event does not transfer ownership of the underlying state.                                                                                    |
| **MCP (Model Context Protocol)** | The protocol used by BookWorm's conversational workflow to access scoped tools ([[system\|mcp-tools]]), including [[domain\|catalog]] tools. Tool access does not make the conversational context the owner of the data.                                            |
| **Order**                        | The accepted purchase record ([[entity\|order]]) whose lifecycle and status are owned by [[domain\|ordering]]. It is separate from the [[entity\|basket]] and from payment settlement or external fulfillment.                                                      |
| **Ordering**                     | The context ([[domain\|ordering]]) that creates an [[entity\|order]] at checkout acceptance and owns its lifecycle and status afterward.                                                                                                                            |
| **Platform**                     | The generic context ([[domain\|platform]]) that supplies capabilities such as scheduled processing and notifications. It supports business contexts without owning their business state.                                                                            |
| **Projection**                   | A derived view built from source data for a particular read or processing need. A projection may be rebuilt from its source and is not authoritative for that source's data.                                                                                        |
| **Rating summary**               | The aggregate average rating and review count shown for a book. [[domain\|reviews]] owns individual [[entity\|feedback]]; [[domain\|catalog]] maintains this summary as a derived projection.                                                                       |
| **Reviews**                      | The context ([[domain\|reviews]]) that owns individual customer [[entity\|feedback]] and rating records. It references [[domain\|catalog]] book identities but does not own canonical book information.                                                             |
| **Saga**                         | A persisted coordination workflow that advances through work across services and can retry or time out. It is not one atomic transaction and does not itself determine the [[entity\|order]]'s business status.                                                     |
| **Service contract**             | An explicit API or message definition through which services communicate. It describes a boundary and its semantics, not the producer's internal persistence model.                                                                                                 |
| **Shopping**                     | The context ([[domain\|shopping]]) that owns [[entity\|basket]] state and pre-order shopping intent. It does not own an accepted [[entity\|order]].                                                                                                                 |
| **Source of truth**              | The authoritative state for a business concept, owned by its responsible context. Copies, caches, and projections in other contexts do not replace it.                                                                                                              |
