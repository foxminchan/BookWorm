This chapter defines the BookWorm system boundary: which people and external systems interact with it and what information or value crosses the boundary. It treats the complete platform as one black box.

BookWorm is responsible for the complete customer and administration experience of an online bookstore. External providers supply identity, AI, email transport, and managed infrastructure capabilities; they do not become part of the BookWorm system boundary.

## Overview

The subject system is the whole BookWorm platform, including all capabilities exposed to customers and store staff. It owns the domain information and business outcomes needed to operate the bookstore, while the following kinds of actors and neighbors remain outside:

- Customers and store administrators who use the platform.
- Identity services that authenticate users and establish roles.
- AI services that provide language, routing, and embedding capabilities.
- Email delivery services that transport transactional messages.
- Managed infrastructure services that support messaging and search.

Internal applications, bounded contexts, databases, queues, and modules are outside the scope of this chapter. They are described in the [[doc|arc42/05-building-block-view]] and the [[doc|arc42/07-deployment-view]].

## Business Context

The Business Context describes the domain-level relationships between BookWorm and its communication partners. It covers:

- The actors and external neighbors that exchange information or receive business outcomes.
- The purpose and value of each exchange.
- The information sent into and out of the BookWorm boundary.
- Any relevant business assumptions or interface exclusions.

The business view must not contain protocols, API routes, deployment components, or infrastructure product names.

See [[doc|arc42/03-context-and-scope/002-business-context]].

## Technical Context

The Technical Context describes how each business relationship is realized across the boundary. It identifies:

- Human and machine communication channels.
- Protocols, formats, authentication mechanisms, and interaction directions.
- External systems and the interfaces through which they are reached.
- Open network or infrastructure assumptions that are not yet formally resolved.

The technical view must map every communication partner in the Business Context to one or more concrete technical channels. Internal implementation detail remains in the deployment and building-block views.

See [[doc|arc42/03-context-and-scope/003-technical-context]].
