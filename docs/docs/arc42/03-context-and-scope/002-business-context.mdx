## Business value flow

BookWorm's primary commerce journey progresses through six connected business stages:

1. **Discovery:** A customer browses, searches, or asks conversational questions about books.
2. **Authoritative product facts:** **Catalog** provides current book details, pricing, availability, and derived rating summaries.
3. **Purchase intent:** **Shopping** captures and persists the customer's pre-order `Basket`.
4. **Order acceptance and lifecycle:** **Ordering** validates the basket against current **Catalog** facts, accepts the `Order`, and manages lifecycle transitions (`New`, `Cancelled`, `Completed`).
5. **Operational follow-up:** **Platform** sends transactional order emails (**Notification**) and refreshes search embeddings on schedule (**Scheduler**).
6. **Ongoing engagement:** **Reviews** captures customer `Feedback` and **Conversational** guides future discovery, while store administrators manage catalog records, orders, and reviews through **Backoffice**.

## Stakeholder alignment and scope limits

- **Black-box clarity vs. contract precision:** Treating BookWorm as a black box helps product managers, business analysts, and architects agree on business scope quickly, while exact wire formats, retry semantics, and authentication flows live in [[doc|arc42/03-context-and-scope/003-technical-context]] and service contract references.
- **SMTP acceptance is not inbox delivery:** Outbound notification exchanges confirm only whether the SMTP relay accepted a message; bounce tracking or end-user inbox confirmation requires external provider webhooks not currently configured.
- **Operating stakeholders:** Product stakeholders prioritize journeys and quality goals; domain teams own bounded-context rules and contracts; platform and operations teams sustain shared infrastructure and incident triage (see [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/004-stakeholders]]).
