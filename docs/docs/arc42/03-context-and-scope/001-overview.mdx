The system under consideration is the complete BookWorm platform. This section intentionally stays at the platform boundary: it describes users, neighboring capabilities, and technical interaction styles without assigning detailed responsibilities to a particular domain or service.

## System boundary

The system in scope is the BookWorm commerce platform as a whole, not an individual service. It includes the customer-facing commerce capabilities and the shared platform capabilities that support them. The diagram shows the main capabilities and external neighbors at a high level; it is not a deployment view or a complete inventory of providers.

## In-scope behavior

| Capability                   | Included behavior                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Catalog and shopping         | Product discovery, search, browsing, basket creation, and checkout intent |
| Ordering                     | Acceptance of purchases and management of order state                     |
| Reviews                      | Customer review and rating journeys                                       |
| Conversational assistance    | AI-assisted product discovery and customer support                        |
| Shared platform capabilities | Notifications, scheduling, and operational support                        |

## Outside the boundary

Identity, payment processing, fulfillment, email delivery, and AI model hosting are neighboring systems, not BookWorm-owned business capabilities. The platform may depend on them, but their internal behavior and operation are outside this system boundary.

## Context assumptions and open points

- The exact inventory of external providers, their owners, and their interfaces is not established by the repository-level architecture sources.
- This chapter identifies the known interaction categories without implying specific endpoints, protocols, payload schemas, or production topology.
- Detailed service responsibilities and contracts belong in the relevant service and system documentation.
