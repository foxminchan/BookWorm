## Scope and Rationale

The Azure Container Apps environment hosts the public gateway, the internal application tier, and event-triggered workers, making its ingress and scaling boundaries significant to availability and security. This view distinguishes external endpoints from internal app routing and managed dependencies without implying private network isolation that is not configured.

## Resilience and Operational Notes

- **Autoscaling:** The published baseline specifies a minimum of one replica but does not define workload-specific maximum replicas or scaling triggers. Confirm deployed values and establish limits and scale signals from HTTP load, CPU, and broker lag before production capacity commitments.
- **Health checks:** Workloads expose health or liveness endpoints where applicable. Verify readiness probes and promotion criteria per app; endpoint availability alone does not establish deployment health.
- **Rollouts:** The published baseline describes single-revision mode and defines no canary or blue/green traffic-splitting strategy. Verify deployed revision behavior; do not assume zero-downtime releases without validated rollout and rollback procedures.
- **Failure isolation:** Separate apps isolate process failures, but they share an environment and Azure region. Managed databases, messaging, AI, and external providers remain independent availability dependencies.
- **Network boundary:** Gateway and admin UI are externally reachable; business workloads use internal ingress. Internal ingress is not a private subnet, and private endpoints or a VNet perimeter are not currently declared.
