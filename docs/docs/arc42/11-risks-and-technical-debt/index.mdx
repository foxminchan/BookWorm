This chapter records architecture-level exposures that need stakeholder attention. A **risk** is an uncertain event or condition that could affect BookWorm; **technical debt** is a known limitation, missing decision, or verification gap that already constrains confidence or future change.

This is a working assessment of the documented architecture, not an incident history, penetration test, or approved risk acceptance. Priorities are provisional qualitative triage based on potential impact and the evidence linked below. **Open** means this chapter contains no evidence that the exposure has been closed or formally accepted; it does not assert that no mitigation exists in implementation.

## 11.1 Risks

The risks are ordered by provisional priority. Treatments are recommendations for the responsible stakeholders, not claims that the work is scheduled or approved.

| ID       | Priority   | Risk and potential consequence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Treatment and evidence to review                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Proposed accountability                                             |
| -------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **R-01** | **High**   | **Repeated asynchronous work can repeat business or external effects.** Finance may reissue checkout work after a timeout, while durable message delivery can still repeat processing. Basket cleanup or email submission may therefore happen more than once, and a timeout does not establish whether the effect completed.                    | Exercise duplicate delivery and timeout-after-side-effect cases across checkout and notification. Verify idempotency and reconciliation at each effect-owning service; distinguish Order state from saga and email state. Track the observed effects against QR-02 and QR-08. | Ordering, Finance, Basket, and Notification teams                   |
| **R-02** | **High**   | **Production recovery and availability may not meet stakeholder expectations.** The documented deployment view does not establish production topology, redundancy, backup arrangements, or recovery objectives. A service or dependency failure could exceed an intended recovery window or leave queued work unresolved.                        | Agree environment-specific topology, probes, dependency configuration, backup/retention, and recovery objectives. Run isolated restore and failure exercises; record actual results for QR-06, QR-13, and QR-14 before treating targets as commitments.                       | Platform and operations stakeholders; service owners                |
| **R-03** | **High**   | **AI privacy and tool controls may not cover every execution path.** Governance, guardrails, and PII handling are documented for Chat and Rating paths, not as universal protection for every model integration. An unreviewed or misconfigured path could expose unnecessary data or permit unintended tool actions.                            | Inventory model and tool paths. For each, verify effective policy, least-privilege tool registration, PII handling, provider data practices, and failure behavior in its deployed configuration; test the relevant QR-09, QR-10, and QR-15 cases.                             | Conversational and Rating teams; security and platform stakeholders |
| **R-04** | **Medium** | **Derived Catalog search and rating views can be stale during backlog or dependency failure.** Search and rating projections are updated asynchronously; a successful source write or scheduled invocation does not prove that the correct projection is current. Stale discovery information can confuse customers if treated as authoritative. | Preserve Catalog as the source of truth for purchase decisions. Monitor pending revision count and age, exercise failure and recovery paths, and agree a freshness budget using QR-07; make projection state visible to operators.                                            | Catalog and Scheduler teams                                         |
| **R-05** | **Medium** | **Undiscovered consumers can make an apparently compatible contract change unsafe.** Existing schema and consumer/provider checks provide evidence for known consumers, but cannot prove compatibility with an unrecorded consumer or detect every semantic change.                                                                              | Maintain producer/consumer ownership and inventories, review semantic changes as well as schema shape, and coordinate breaking migrations. Use the QR-03 compatibility evidence before release.                                                                               | Producer and consumer service teams                                 |

### Risk review

- Review risks with product, engineering, platform, and operations stakeholders, especially when an interface, data flow, deployment configuration, or operational process changes.
- Keep priorities provisional until likelihood, impact, and accountable risk acceptance are agreed by the stakeholders who own the consequences.
- Record any accepted residual risk with its decision owner, rationale, review condition, and date. Reopen it if the assumptions or mitigations change.
- A configured mechanism is not closure evidence by itself: verify its registration and behavior on the relevant deployed path.

## 11.2 Technical debt and evidence gaps

These are known limits in the documented design or in the evidence available in this architecture baseline. They are not all implementation defects: some require a decision, environment-specific configuration, or an observed test result.

| ID        | Affected area             | Known debt or gap                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Recommended next step                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TD-01** | Ordering idempotency      | The documented Redis duplicate-request guard performs its existence check and write separately and writes the key before the handler runs. It is not an atomic exactly-once transaction; concurrent requests and handler failure after key creation need explicit treatment. | Decide whether stronger atomicity or recovery is required. Add focused concurrent-request and post-key handler-failure verification; keep the guarantee accurately scoped in the API documentation. |
| **TD-02** | Deployment and operations | The logical deployment view does not establish physical topology, production dependency configuration, redundancy, backup/restore procedures, or the production telemetry backend and probe mapping.                                                                         | Complete and verify an environment-specific deployment and recovery profile. Do not infer production behavior from local orchestration or shared defaults.                                          |
| **TD-03** | Quality targets           | Latency, availability, throughput, projection freshness, recovery time, and recovery point targets in the quality scenarios are proposals, not stakeholder-approved service levels.                                                                                          | Assign decision owners, agree measurement conditions and thresholds, and promote only approved targets to acceptance criteria. Until then, label them proposed.                                     |
| **TD-04** | Verification evidence     | The quality-scenario chapter defines measurable checks but does not record execution results. In particular, its duplicate-effect, projection-recovery, production-recovery, and customer-path criteria cannot be treated as passed from the architecture description alone. | Keep test and exercise results in the owning team's acceptance or release record, then link the evidence and date when reviewing the relevant scenarios and risk items.                             |
