## 1. Context & Problem

Distributed architectures introduce uncertainties that do not exist in a single, centralized application. In BookWorm, work flows across independent business capabilities—such as shopping, ordering, catalog discovery, notifications, and AI-assisted guidance—where network delays, message retries, background schedules, and evolving data contracts can create subtle failure modes.

At the same time, architecture documentation often captures intended designs before production infrastructure, formal service-level agreements (SLAs), or full verification evidence are finalized. Without a shared register separating **known architectural risks** from **technical debt and missing evidence**, product, architecture, and delivery teams risk treating unverified assumptions as production guarantees.

This chapter serves as a working assessment of the documented BookWorm architecture. It is not an incident history, a penetration test, or a formal risk acceptance record.

## 2. Business Value

Maintaining an explicit, prioritized risk and technical debt register delivers clear value across stakeholders:

- **Informed investment decisions (PM & Business)**: Highlights high-impact exposures—such as duplicate customer notifications, unclear recovery windows, or unguarded AI paths—so remediation and testing effort goes where customer and operational impact is highest.
- **Expectation alignment (BA & SA)**: Distinguishes between proposed architectural targets and stakeholder-approved commitments, preventing premature promises around uptime, recovery speed, or data freshness.
- **Safer delivery and operations (Engineering & Ops)**: Identifies exact gaps in verification evidence and environment configuration before release, reducing surprise outages and data inconsistencies in production.

## 3. How It Works

BookWorm evaluates architectural exposures across two complementary dimensions:

1. **Architecture Risks (`R-01`–`R-05`)**: Uncertain conditions that could harm customer experience, business integrity, security, or service reliability if left unaddressed:
   - **Repeated side effects**: When work is retried across independent services after a delay or timeout, customer-facing actions—like clearing a shopping basket or sending an email—could happen more than once unless each owning area safely absorbs repeats.
   - **Unproven production recovery**: Local development setups do not establish how production infrastructure handles outages, backups, or queued work, creating a risk that real recovery times miss stakeholder expectations.
   - **Uneven AI safety coverage**: Privacy protections and tool restrictions documented for specific conversational or review flows might not automatically apply to every AI integration path unless verified per path.
   - **Stale discovery views**: Search indexes and summarized book ratings update asynchronously in the background; temporary backlogs can cause customers to see outdated discovery information while the core catalog remains authoritative.
   - **Unnoticed contract drift**: Changes to shared messages or interfaces can break undocumented consumers or alter business meaning even when the data structure appears compatible.

2. **Technical Debt & Evidence Gaps (`TD-01`–`TD-04`)**: Known limitations in the documented design or areas where operational proof is still missing—spanning concurrency edge cases in duplicate-request protection, incomplete production deployment specifications, unapproved quality targets, and missing test execution records.

### Governance and Review Cadence

- Priorities (`High`, `Medium`) reflect provisional qualitative triage based on potential impact and currently documented evidence.
- **Open** status means the architecture catalog does not yet contain evidence that the exposure is closed or formally accepted—it does not mean no safeguard exists in code.
- Risks are reviewed with product, engineering, platform, and operations stakeholders whenever data flows, interfaces, deployment setups, or operational processes change.
- Any accepted residual risk must record its decision owner, rationale, review trigger, and date, and be reopened if underlying assumptions change.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **A configured mechanism is not closure evidence**: Having a retry guard, privacy policy, or health check documented in design does not prove it behaves correctly under failure in a deployed environment. Verification requires observed evidence on the actual runtime path.
- **Proposed targets vs. approved commitments**: Quality numbers documented as architectural proposals must stay clearly labeled as provisional until business and operations owners agree on measurement rules and accept them as service levels.
- **Local orchestration cannot predict production resilience**: Running services together locally is valuable for development, but production redundancy, retention, backups, and failure recovery must be designed and exercised separately.
- **Provisional triage enables iteration without false certainty**: Ranking risks qualitatively lets teams act early on high-impact gaps while keeping priorities flexible until likelihood, impact, and ownership are formally confirmed.

### 11.2 Technical Debt and Evidence Gaps

These are known limits in the documented design or in the evidence available in this architecture baseline. They are not all implementation defects: some require a decision, environment-specific configuration, or an observed test result.

| ID        | Affected area             | Known debt or gap                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Recommended next step                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TD-01** | Ordering idempotency      | The documented Redis duplicate-request guard performs its existence check and write separately and writes the key before the handler runs. It is not an atomic exactly-once transaction; concurrent requests and handler failure after key creation need explicit treatment. | Decide whether stronger atomicity or recovery is required. Add focused concurrent-request and post-key handler-failure verification; keep the guarantee accurately scoped in the API documentation. |
| **TD-02** | Deployment and operations | The logical deployment view does not establish physical topology, production dependency configuration, redundancy, backup/restore procedures, or the production telemetry backend and probe mapping.                                                                         | Complete and verify an environment-specific deployment and recovery profile. Do not infer production behavior from local orchestration or shared defaults.                                          |
| **TD-03** | Quality targets           | Latency, availability, throughput, projection freshness, recovery time, and recovery point targets in the quality scenarios are proposals, not stakeholder-approved service levels.                                                                                          | Assign decision owners, agree measurement conditions and thresholds, and promote only approved targets to acceptance criteria. Until then, label them proposed.                                     |
| **TD-04** | Verification evidence     | The quality-scenario chapter defines measurable checks but does not record execution results. In particular, its duplicate-effect, projection-recovery, production-recovery, and customer-path criteria cannot be treated as passed from the architecture description alone. | Keep test and exercise results in the owning team's acceptance or release record, then link the evidence and date when reviewing the relevant scenarios and risk items.                             |
