This chapter translates BookWorm's five prioritized quality goals from [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/003-quality-goals]]—**Correctness**, **Interoperability**, **Deployability**, **Modifiability**, and **Operability**—into a structured quality tree and 17 concrete, measurable scenarios (`QR-01`-`QR-17`) spanning commerce correctness, reliability, security, privacy, AI governance, performance, and accessibility.

:::note
All 17 scenarios begin as **proposed / not evaluated**. Existing codebase mechanisms (such as Pact contract tests, Presidio PII middleware, or Azure HA provisioning) provide design evidence, not proof of end-to-end SLA compliance until evaluated under frozen test conditions with recorded evidence.
:::

## Baseline and governance rules

- **Authoritative priority order:** [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/003-quality-goals]] remains authoritative when qualities compete: **Correctness > Interoperability > Deployability > Modifiability > Operability**.
- **Behavioral criteria vs. proposed numerical budgets:** Behavioral assertions derive directly from BookWorm's domain invariants and service contracts. Numerical budgets (latency, availability, RTO/RPO, freshness lag, rollout time) are concrete starting proposals awaiting formal stakeholder sign-off.
- **Visible limits over hidden assumptions:** Duplicate side effects under retries, eventual consistency on derived search and rating views, partial safeguard coverage, the absence of backend `.IntegrationTests` projects, and unverified production restore drills remain explicitly documented rather than assumed away.
- **Proposed reviewers vs. named approvers:** Reviewer roles listed in [[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements/001-quality-requirements-overview]] are suggested review groups; formal production adoption requires named decision-makers.
