## 1. Context & Problem

BookWorm's introduction establishes five leading architectural goals in priority order in [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/003-quality-goals]]: **correctness, interoperability, deployability, modifiability, and operability**. However, high-level goals alone do not tell teams how to test a checkout flow, evaluate background search updates, verify access controls, or assess AI-assisted discovery.

This chapter makes BookWorm's architectural qualities concrete across the complete platform—covering commerce, derived catalog views, background workflows, and AI-assisted discovery—while expanding to reliability, security, privacy, efficiency, and usability.

## 2. Business Value

Turning quality goals into an explicit baseline creates alignment across business and technical stakeholders:

- **Shared priorities:** Connects executive goals to concrete, testable expectations before production commitments are made.
- **Honest baselines:** Distinguishes what the current design supports from unapproved service-level targets, preventing costly misunderstandings with stakeholders.
- **Clear accountability:** Establishes proposed reviewer roles and measurable criteria so teams can surface risks and technical debt early.

## 3. How It Works

The chapter is organized into two pages that move from strategy to verification:

1. **Overview and priority tiers** ([[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements/001-quality-requirements-overview]]): Defines priority tiers, a visual quality tree, stakeholder concern areas, and traceability back to BookWorm's architectural strategy.
2. **Measurable scenarios** ([[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements/002-quality-scenarios]]): Translates business invariants and ownership rules into 17 concrete usage and change scenarios (`QR-01` through `QR-17`), complete with verification evidence requirements, known limits, and open agreements.

Together, these pages pair **behavioral criteria** (assertions derived from documented business rules) with **quantitative proposals** (concrete starting budgets where no repository-wide target has yet been approved).

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Proposed baseline, not an approved SLA:** This chapter is neither a test report nor a guarantee that every scenario currently passes. Existing design mechanisms are supporting design evidence, not proof of end-to-end fulfillment.
- **Numerical proposals require stakeholder sign-off:** Quantitative targets give teams concrete starting points, but product, service, and operations stakeholders must formally agree on final values and measurement conditions before adoption.
- **Known limits stay visible:** Duplicate effects under retries, partial safeguard coverage, eventual consistency, sampled diagnostic data, and an unspecified production topology are called out explicitly rather than hidden.
- **Proposed reviewers vs. named approvers:** Stakeholder roles listed in the overview are suggested reviewers; formal sign-off requires named decision makers.
