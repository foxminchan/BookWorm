This chapter makes BookWorm's architectural qualities concrete through a quality tree and measurable scenarios. It covers the complete platform, including commerce, derived Catalog views, asynchronous work, and AI-assisted discovery.

The five leading goals and their priority order remain authoritative in [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/003-quality-goals]]: **correctness, interoperability, deployability, modifiability, and operability**. This chapter refines those goals and adds reliability, security, privacy, efficiency, and usability requirements rather than repeating the introduction.

## Status and interpretation

This is a **proposed quality-requirements baseline**, not an approved SLA, a test report, or a statement that every scenario already passes.

- **Behavioral criteria** turn documented business invariants and ownership rules into assertions to verify. Existing mechanisms are evidence for the design, not proof of end-to-end fulfillment.
- **Quantitative proposals** provide concrete starting points where the repository has no approved budget. Product, service, and operations stakeholders must agree their values and measurement conditions before adoption.
- **Known limits** remain explicit, especially duplicate effects, partial middleware coverage, eventual consistency, sampled telemetry, and unspecified production topology.
- The page owner maintains the documentation; stakeholder roles on the overview page are proposed reviewers, not named approvers.
