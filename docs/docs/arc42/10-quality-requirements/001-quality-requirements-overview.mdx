This page organizes BookWorm's quality requirements into three priority tiers (`P1`–`P3`), a nine-branch quality tree, and a scenario-mapping matrix connecting business concerns to the 17 measurable checks in [[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements/002-quality-scenarios]]. Use it during architecture reviews to separate non-negotiable invariants from tradeable engineering goals.

## Priority tiers

These tiers evaluate detailed scenarios without replacing the ordered top-five goals in [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/003-quality-goals]]. Lower-tier goals (`P2`, `P3`) must never silently weaken a `P1` invariant.

| Tier               | Meaning                                                                                                        | Permitted compromise                                                                              |
| :----------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **P1 — Essential** | Protects business correctness, access boundaries, privacy, AI governance, and durable work.                    | No silent violation. Any unmet requirement needs explicit risk acceptance and a remediation plan. |
| **P2 — Important** | Protects independent change, incident diagnosis, interactive responsiveness, recovery, and customer usability. | Trade-offs require recorded evidence and must not weaken any `P1` requirement.                    |
| **P3 — Desirable** | Improves engineering effectiveness after essential and important requirements are met.                         | May be deferred with an explicit owner and review trigger.                                        |

## Quality concerns and scenario mapping

Labels follow the pragmatic [arc42 Q42 model](https://quality.arc42.org/); one scenario can support multiple quality attributes.

| Quality concern                    | Q42 labels               | BookWorm-specific requirement                                                                                                            | Scenarios                          | Proposed reviewers                                         |
| :--------------------------------- | :----------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Correctness**                    | `#reliable`              | Base purchases on authoritative **Catalog** and **Basket** facts; keep `Order` status distinct from post-checkout coordination outcomes. | `QR-01`, `QR-02`                   | Product stakeholders; Ordering and Shopping teams          |
| **Interoperability**               | `#flexible`, `#testable` | Keep REST, gRPC, and Kafka contracts explicit, owned, and compatible with supported consumers.                                           | `QR-03`                            | Producer and consumer service teams                        |
| **Deployability**                  | `#flexible`, `#operable` | Release compatible changes in one service without forcing unrelated services to redeploy.                                                | `QR-04`                            | Service and platform teams                                 |
| **Modifiability**                  | `#flexible`, `#testable` | Change business rules inside the owning bounded context without cross-service database edits or hidden coupling.                         | `QR-05`, `QR-17`                   | Domain teams; architecture leadership                      |
| **Operability**                    | `#operable`              | Identify the owning capability and actual stored or queued execution state of any failed or delayed operation.                           | `QR-06`                            | Operations and support teams                               |
| **Reliability and recoverability** | `#reliable`, `#operable` | Preserve queued work, surface uncertain external deliveries, and validate availability and restoration objectives.                       | `QR-07`, `QR-08`, `QR-13`, `QR-14` | Catalog, Notification, platform, and operations teams      |
| **Security and privacy**           | `#secure`                | Enforce service-specific **Microsoft Entra ID** policies and prevent sensitive-data disclosure across APIs, AI models, and telemetry.    | `QR-09`, `QR-10`, `QR-15`          | API and AI service teams; platform teams                   |
| **Performance efficiency**         | `#efficient`             | Bound interactive customer latency under a reproducible workload, separately from asynchronous freshness.                                | `QR-12`                            | Product, Catalog, Shopping, Ordering, and operations teams |
| **Usability and accessibility**    | `#usable`                | Provide clear validation and failure feedback and keep the core purchase journey keyboard- and screen-reader-accessible.                 | `QR-11`, `QR-16`                   | Product stakeholders; frontend teams                       |

## Traceability to solution strategy

- **`QR-01` and `QR-02`** refine **Correctness** through bounded-context data ownership and explicit separation of `Order` state from **Finance** saga and **Notification** outcomes.
- **`QR-03`** refines **Interoperability** through Pact contract verification and Protocol Buffer compatibility checks.
- **`QR-04`** refines **Deployability** through mixed-version release and rollback rehearsals.
- **`QR-05` and `QR-17`** refine **Modifiability** through Vertical Slice Architecture, `BookWorm.ArchTests`, and `turbo boundaries`.
- **`QR-06`** refines **Operability** through OpenTelemetry correlation, Quartz execution history, Durable Task state, and Kafka native dead-letter queues (DLQs).
