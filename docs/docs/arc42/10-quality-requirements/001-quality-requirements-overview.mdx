Quality requirements refine the priorities in [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/003-quality-goals]] into observable outcomes. High priority protects correctness, interoperability, security, and essential reliability; medium priority covers deployability, operability, performance, and customer experience; low priority captures improvements that can follow once higher priorities are met.

## Priority summary

| Quality Attribute           | Priority (High / Medium / Low) | Key Focus Area                                                                              | Linked Scenarios    |
| :-------------------------- | :----------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :------------------ |
| Correctness                 | High                           | Validate authoritative purchase facts and keep order outcomes truthful.                     | QS-01, QS-02        |
| Interoperability            | High                           | Preserve compatibility across supported service consumers.                                  | QS-03               |
| Deployability               | Medium                         | Release and roll back compatible changes independently.                                     | QS-04               |
| Maintainability             | Medium                         | Keep business-rule changes within their owning capability.                                  | QS-05               |
| Maintainability             | Low                            | Enable a new contributor to make a bounded change with focused verification.                | QS-17               |
| Operability                 | Medium                         | Locate failed work and identify its real execution state.                                   | QS-06               |
| Reliability                 | High                           | Recover asynchronous work and preserve delivery integrity.                                  | QS-07, QS-08        |
| Reliability                 | Medium                         | Meet availability and restoration objectives for customer-facing paths and owned state.     | QS-13, QS-14        |
| Security and privacy        | High                           | Enforce access boundaries and prevent sensitive-data disclosure or unauthorized AI actions. | QS-09, QS-10, QS-15 |
| Performance                 | Medium                         | Bound interactive latency under a repeatable workload.                                      | QS-12               |
| Usability and accessibility | Medium                         | Make errors actionable and core journeys accessible.                                        | QS-11, QS-16        |

## Alignment with the architecture

- **Correctness** is supported by authoritative ownership of business facts and by keeping order state distinct from downstream coordination.
- **Interoperability** and **deployability** depend on explicit, compatible contracts and rehearsed independent releases.
- **Maintainability** is supported by capability ownership, localized change, and verification of dependency boundaries.
- **Operability** and **reliability** require correlated diagnostics, recoverable asynchronous work, and measured restoration.
- **Security and privacy** rely on least-privilege access, data minimization, and governed model and tool execution.
- **Performance, usability, and accessibility** are measured at customer-visible boundaries, separately from asynchronous freshness.

These approaches are consistent with [[doc|arc42/04-solution-strategy]]. Scenario targets marked as proposed remain subject to owner approval before they become release commitments.
