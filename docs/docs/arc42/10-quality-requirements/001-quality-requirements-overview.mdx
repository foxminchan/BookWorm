## 1. Context & Problem

High-level architectural goals can easily remain abstract unless they are mapped to specific stakeholder concerns and concrete verification scenarios. Across BookWorm, teams need a shared way to decide which qualities are non-negotiable—such as order accuracy and access control—versus which qualities can be balanced against delivery speed or infrastructure cost.

This page bridges the chapter introduction in [[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements]] and the detailed measurable tests in [[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements/002-quality-scenarios]].

## 2. Business Value

A structured quality overview helps business, product, operations, and engineering stakeholders make aligned decisions:

- **Clear non-negotiables:** Separates essential correctness, privacy, and durability rules from nice-to-have improvements.
- **Faster architecture reviews:** Connects each business concern to named reviewer groups and specific scenarios so nothing falls through the cracks.
- **Shared vocabulary:** Gives technical and non-technical stakeholders a common map from customer impact to system design choices.

## 3. How It Works

BookWorm evaluates quality concerns using three priority tiers and nine concern areas that feed directly into detailed scenarios.

### Priority convention

These tiers are a proposed way to evaluate the detailed scenarios. They do not replace the ordered top-five goals in section 1.2.

| Tier               | Meaning                                                                          | Permitted compromise                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **P1 - Essential** | Protect business correctness, access boundaries, and durable work.               | No silent violation. An unmet requirement needs explicit risk acceptance and a remediation decision. |
| **P2 - Important** | Protect independent change, diagnosis, responsiveness, and the customer journey. | Trade-offs require evidence and must not weaken a P1 requirement.                                    |
| **P3 - Desirable** | Improve engineering effectiveness after essential and important requirements.    | May be deferred with an owner and a review condition.                                                |

### Nine quality concern areas

1. **Correctness:** Base purchases on authoritative facts and keep order status distinct from background coordination outcomes.
2. **Interoperability:** Keep service interfaces explicit, owned, and compatible with supported consumers.
3. **Deployability:** Release compatible changes in one service without forcing unrelated services to redeploy.
4. **Modifiability:** Change business rules inside their owning capability without cross-service database edits or hidden coupling.
5. **Operability:** Identify the owning capability and actual stored state of any failed or delayed operation.
6. **Reliability and recoverability:** Preserve queued work, surface uncertain external deliveries, and validate availability and restoration objectives.
7. **Security and privacy:** Enforce service-specific access rules and prevent sensitive-data disclosure across user interfaces, AI models, and operational logs.
8. **Performance efficiency:** Bound interactive customer response times under reproducible load, separately from background freshness.
9. **Usability and accessibility:** Provide clear validation and failure feedback and keep the core purchase journey accessible to all users.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Coverage map, not a compliance score:** The quality tree organizes BookWorm's actual concerns rather than claiming every generic industry quality characteristic applies.
- **Strict hierarchy of compromise:** P2 and P3 goals (such as speed or developer convenience) must never silently weaken P1 essentials (such as authoritative pricing, access control, or durable work).
- **Proposed reviewers, not final sign-off:** Reviewer roles listed for each concern are starting proposals; named approvers and agreed budgets still require formal stakeholder sign-off.

### Traceability to the architectural strategy

- **QR-01 and QR-02** refine correctness through owned state and explicit runtime outcomes.
- **QR-03** refines interoperability through contract compatibility.
- **QR-04** refines deployability through mixed-version release verification.
- **QR-05** refines modifiability through local ownership and boundary checks.
- **QR-06** refines operability through diagnosis of actual execution and persisted state.

The architectural responses are summarized in [[doc|arc42/04-solution-strategy]]. The [[doc|arc42/09-architecture-decisions]] records their rationale; the linked ADRs remain authoritative for status and scope.
