The scenarios below refine the priorities in [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/003-quality-goals]] and the approaches in [[doc|arc42/04-solution-strategy]]. Each states a stimulus, operating context, required response, measurable acceptance metric, and priority. All numerical service budgets are proposed until their owners approve them.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Non-negotiable conditions and proposed targets">
  Checkout must use authoritative purchase facts; access denials must not expose protected data or cause protected writes; sensitive data must not be disclosed to model providers or telemetry; and failed asynchronous work must not be represented as successful business completion.

The response-time, availability, recovery, freshness, accessibility, rollout, and onboarding budgets below are proposed baselines, not approved service-level commitments. Assign owners and approve test conditions before treating them as release gates.

</Admonition>

## Usage scenarios

| ID                                             | Quality Attribute           | Stimulus (Trigger)                                                                                                     | Context / Environment                                                                                                                                 | Expected Response                                                                                                                                                       | Measurable Metric                                                                                                                                                                             | Priority |
| :--------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------- |
| **QS-01 — Authoritative purchase**             | Correctness                 | A customer submits checkout with valid, missing-item, or altered-price input.                                          | Checkout reads the customer's persisted basket and current catalog facts.                                                                             | Accept valid purchases using catalog-derived prices and basket quantities; reject unavailable items; never trust a client-supplied price.                               | 100% of accepted fixtures preserve authoritative quantities and prices; 0 purchases are created for unavailable-item fixtures.                                                                | High     |
| **QS-02 — Truthful coordination outcomes**     | Correctness                 | Basket cleanup, a retry, notification submission, or timeout returns success, failure, or an uncertain result.         | Order coordination and downstream work may be retried or partially complete.                                                                          | Determine completion or cancellation only from authoritative order state; expose cleanup and delivery failures without implying that retries prove business completion. | 0 order-state transitions are caused solely by cleanup, retry count, or notification outcome across the complete success and failure fixture set.                                             | High     |
| **QS-06 — Incident diagnosis**                 | Operability                 | An operator receives a request, message, scheduled-work, or order identifier associated with a failure.                | A deployed verification environment includes correlated traces and execution records.                                                                 | Identify the owning capability, failed boundary, and actual execution state.                                                                                            | Proposed: diagnose 100% of injected incidents within 15 minutes.                                                                                                                              | Medium   |
| **QS-07 — Recoverable search freshness**       | Reliability                 | A catalog update, deletion, indexing failure, or newer concurrent update occurs.                                       | 1,000 catalog changes at 10 changes per minute with healthy dependencies.                                                                             | Keep failed updates recoverable, prevent older revisions replacing newer ones, and remove deleted items from search.                                                    | Proposed: p95 commit-to-search visibility is at most 10 minutes; 100% of updates converge to the latest revision after recovery.                                                              | High     |
| **QS-08 — Honest notification delivery**       | Reliability                 | The mail provider or idempotency store fails before or after provider acceptance.                                      | Notification work is delivered asynchronously with retries and a dead-letter recovery path.                                                           | Retry transient failures within configured bounds, retain exhausted work for investigation, and distinguish provider acceptance from recipient delivery.                | 100% of exhausted failure fixtures are recoverable from the dead-letter mechanism; 0 provider failures are reported as confirmed recipient delivery.                                          | High     |
| **QS-09 — Enforced access boundaries**         | Security                    | A request has missing or invalid credentials, insufficient permissions, or targets another customer's data.            | Protected APIs and internal message-driven workers operate in a production-like environment.                                                          | Deny unauthorized access without exposing protected records or performing protected writes; valid authorized requests continue to work.                                 | 100% of denial fixtures return an authorization failure; 0 protected records are disclosed and 0 protected writes occur.                                                                      | High     |
| **QS-10 — Sensitive-data minimization**        | Security and privacy        | A request, response, tool result, or exception contains synthetic personal data, a token, or another classified value. | Data crosses model-provider, API error, log, and telemetry boundaries.                                                                                | Remove or protect sensitive values before disclosure; return public errors without credentials or internal diagnostic details.                                          | 0 raw synthetic secrets appear in logs or telemetry; 0 raw synthetic personal data crosses protected model boundaries.                                                                        | High     |
| **QS-11 — Actionable interaction feedback**    | Usability                   | A customer submits invalid data or waits on an outstanding operation.                                                  | Customer-facing forms include validation, conflict, and unexpected-error states.                                                                      | Identify invalid fields, prevent invalid writes, provide progress feedback, and show a correlation reference without internal details for unexpected failures.          | 100% of invalid fixtures identify the affected field and cause no write; proposed: progress feedback appears within 1 second.                                                                 | Medium   |
| **QS-12 — Interactive performance under load** | Performance                 | A representative workload runs for 30 minutes at 50 requests per second with 100 concurrent users.                     | 10,000 active books, baskets up to 20 items, healthy dependencies; workload mix is 60% catalog reads/search, 30% basket operations, and 10% checkout. | Maintain correct results while responding within operation-specific latency budgets; measure asynchronous work separately.                                              | Proposed: p95 catalog and basket latency at most 500 ms; p95 checkout response after order persistence at most 2 s; p99 at most 2x each p95 budget; unexpected error rate below 1% per group. | Medium   |
| **QS-13 — Customer-path availability**         | Reliability                 | Synthetic probes exercise browsing, basket, and checkout once per minute.                                              | Production-like environment measured over a rolling 30-day window, separately for each path.                                                          | Complete each eligible journey within its latency budget; count timeouts, incorrect results, and required-dependency outages as failures.                               | Proposed: at least 99.9% successful executions per path; exclude only intentional invalid-input and authorization-denial probes.                                                              | Medium   |
| **QS-14 — Restore authoritative state**        | Reliability                 | A service and its authoritative store are restored after simulated loss.                                               | Each authoritative store is exercised independently; derived search data can be rebuilt from catalog records.                                         | Restore usable service, reconcile queued and saga work, and avoid claiming exactly-once effects.                                                                        | Proposed: RTO at most 30 minutes and RPO at most 5 minutes for committed authoritative data, measured per store.                                                                              | Medium   |
| **QS-15 — Govern model and tool execution**    | Security and privacy        | A prompt-injection fixture, denied tool request, rate-limit event, or tool-provider failure occurs.                    | Governed model workflows and registered tools run under their deployed policies.                                                                      | Rejected inputs cause no agent handoff; denied tools do not run; allowed flows use only permitted tools; failures produce explicit degraded or failed outcomes.         | 100% of policy fixtures follow the expected decision; 0 denied tool calls execute; rate-limited requests return an explicit limit response.                                                   | High     |
| **QS-16 — Accessible core journey**            | Usability and accessibility | A customer completes browsing, basket, checkout, and error states using keyboard navigation or a screen reader.        | Agreed desktop and mobile browser and assistive-technology combinations.                                                                              | Provide accessible names, logical focus order, field-associated errors, announced status changes, and no keyboard traps.                                                | Proposed: meet applicable WCAG 2.2 AA criteria; 0 keyboard traps and 0 unresolved applicable conformance failures.                                                                            | Medium   |

## Change scenarios

| ID                                         | Quality Attribute | Stimulus (Trigger)                                                                         | Context / Environment                                                                                    | Expected Response                                                                                                                           | Measurable Metric                                                                                                                               | Priority |
| :----------------------------------------- | :---------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------- |
| **QS-03 — Compatible contracts**           | Interoperability  | A producer proposes an additive or breaking interface change.                              | Supported consumers use documented HTTP, RPC, or event contracts.                                        | Verify compatibility with every supported consumer before release; provide an explicit migration and versioning plan for a breaking change. | 100% of supported consumers pass compatibility verification; 0 unplanned breaking changes are released.                                         | High     |
| **QS-04 — Independent compatible release** | Deployability     | A capability releases a compatible change and rehearses rollback.                          | Unchanged consumers remain deployed during the release.                                                  | Release and roll back without unrelated capability releases; unchanged consumers continue to work in both versions.                         | 0 unrelated releases required; proposed: rollout and rollback each complete within 15 minutes.                                                  | Medium   |
| **QS-05 — Local business-rule change**     | Maintainability   | A team implements an agreed small rule change that needs no new cross-capability contract. | The change is limited to one capability and includes focused verification.                               | Change the owning capability without altering another capability's data schema or implementation.                                           | 0 changes to other capabilities' schemas or implementations; proposed: complete implementation and focused verification within 2 engineer-days. | Medium   |
| **QS-17 — New-contributor onboarding**     | Maintainability   | A contributor unfamiliar with the system is assigned an agreed small behavior change.      | Documentation, development tools, and required access are available; waiting time is tracked separately. | Find the owning capability and its contracts, implement the change, and pass focused behavior and boundary checks.                          | Proposed: complete within 5 working days; report active effort separately from access and review delays.                                        | Low      |

## Scenario evaluation prompts

<Prompt
	description="Plan a quality-scenario evaluation"
	actions={["copy", "cursor"]}
	icon="sparkles"
>
	For the selected BookWorm quality scenario, prepare a verification plan using synthetic data in an
	isolated environment. Record the software versions, environment, data set, workload, dependency
	health, and active governance policies. Define the measurement boundary: checkout ends when the
	order is persisted, search freshness ends when the updated revision is queryable, and notification
	evidence ends at provider acceptance. Specify the per-operation and per-customer-path percentiles
	and error rates to collect, the failures that count against the metric, and the persisted side
	effects or asynchronous states to verify. Do not claim exactly-once behavior or promote proposed
	targets to approved commitments.
</Prompt>

<Prompt
	description="Record quality-scenario evaluation evidence"
	actions={["copy", "cursor"]}
	icon="sparkles"
>
	Create an evaluation record for the selected quality scenario with these fields: scenario ID and
	owner; target approval status; evaluation date; frozen conditions and workload; measured result
	against the stated metric; sanitized evidence; known gaps; follow-up owner; and the event that
	should trigger re-evaluation. Report timeouts, incorrect results, and dependency outages as
	failures wherever the scenario requires them. Use only synthetic data and exclude sensitive values
	from the record.
</Prompt>

## Alignment and trade-offs

| Concern                                 | Architectural position                                                                                      | Scenario evidence still required                                                              |
| :-------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Correctness versus latency              | Checkout uses authoritative basket and catalog facts; latency targets do not justify bypassing validation.  | Evaluate QS-01 with QS-12 and define explicit behavior when dependencies fail.                |
| Durable work versus freshness           | Catalog changes do not wait for search indexing; asynchronous work must converge after recovery.            | Approve freshness and backlog budgets and evaluate recovery under sustained outages in QS-07. |
| Retries versus duplicate effects        | Retries preserve work but may repeat cleanup or provider submissions; exactly-once behavior is not assumed. | Evaluate uncertain outcomes and duplicate handling in QS-02 and QS-08.                        |
| Independent release versus coordination | Compatible changes may be released independently; breaking changes require planned migration.               | Approve supported mixed-version windows and rollback criteria in QS-03 and QS-04.             |
| Diagnosis versus privacy                | Correlation supports diagnosis without retaining raw sensitive values.                                      | Approve retention and access controls and verify redaction in QS-06 and QS-10.                |
| Availability versus recovery cost       | Infrastructure configuration is not evidence that recovery objectives have been met.                        | Run isolated drills and verify external dependency commitments for QS-13 and QS-14.           |
| AI usefulness versus least privilege    | Model workflows use explicit policies; denied tools and failed providers do not create fabricated outcomes. | Evaluate policy coverage and provider failure behavior in QS-15.                              |
