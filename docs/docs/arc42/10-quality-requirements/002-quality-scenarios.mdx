## 1. Context & Problem

Quality goals such as "accurate checkout," "fast browsing," or "safe AI assistance" cannot be verified without concrete, repeatable scenarios. Teams also need to distinguish **usage scenarios** (normal traffic and failure handling) from **change scenarios** (how safely and quickly the platform can evolve).

This page refines the priority tiers and quality tree in [[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements/001-quality-requirements-overview]] and [[doc|arc42/10-quality-requirements]]. All acceptance criteria and numerical budgets marked **proposed** are starting baselines for review—not current operational guarantees.

## 2. Business Value

Concrete scenarios give product, business, operations, and engineering teams shared, testable guardrails:

- **Revenue and order integrity:** Ensures customer purchases always reflect authoritative catalog prices and stored basket quantities rather than tampered inputs or guessed workflow states.
- **Customer trust and safety:** Verifies access boundaries, sensitive-data minimization, AI tool controls, clear error feedback, and accessible shopping journeys.
- **Predictable operations and recovery:** Sets concrete targets for diagnosing failed jobs, keeping search indexes fresh, surfacing uncertain email deliveries, and restoring lost data.
- **Lower change risk:** Verifies that teams can release updates independently and change local business rules without breaking neighboring capabilities.

## 3. How It Works

BookWorm defines 17 scenarios across four thematic areas. Each scenario specifies a starting context, a trigger event, a required response, and the evidence needed to prove it passes.

### Four scenario groups

1. **Commerce correctness and independent evolution:** Covers authoritative checkout pricing and quantities, honest reporting of order vs. background workflow outcomes, compatible service contracts, independent zero-downtime releases, and localized business-rule changes.
2. **Operability, durable work, and recovery:** Covers pinpointing failed background operations, keeping search indexes recoverable and eventually fresh, making failed or uncertain email submissions visible, measuring customer-path availability, and rehearsing data restoration.
3. **Security, privacy, and governed AI:** Covers strict access and ownership checks, preventing raw personal data or credentials from leaking into logs or AI model boundaries, and enforcing tool permissions and failure rules on AI paths.
4. **Responsiveness, usability, and engineering effectiveness:** Covers actionable validation messages and progress feedback, interactive responsiveness under steady load, accessible keyboard and screen-reader journeys, and realistic onboarding effort for new developers.

### Measurement and evidence principles

To keep evaluations honest and repeatable, teams follow six rules:

1. **Freeze the test conditions** (versions, dataset, environment, and enabled safeguards) before measuring.
2. **Measure at the real business boundary** (for example, order creation ends when the order is saved; email acceptance by a mail server is not proof of inbox delivery).
3. **Count failures honestly** (include timeouts and wrong results; do not exclude dependency outages from availability math).
4. **Preserve per-path results** so a healthy browsing path never masks a broken checkout path.
5. **Verify stored outcomes, not just surface responses** (inspect saved records, queues, and outbound actions).
6. **Use synthetic test data only** in isolated environments—never real customer records or live production fault injection.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Correctness vs. speed:** Checkout will not sacrifice authoritative pricing or persisted basket facts just to meet a latency budget; teams must agree how to fail clearly when dependencies are unavailable.
- **Durable background work vs. immediate freshness:** Catalog updates commit immediately without waiting for search index updates, relying on queued background work that can lag during outages or large backlogs.
- **Retry safety vs. duplicate work:** Retries and durable queues prevent lost work, but without universal exactly-once guarantees, duplicate commands or repeated notifications remain possible and must be accounted for explicitly.
- **Partial safeguards are not blanket proof:** Documented duplicate-request guards, access helpers, and AI safety middleware operate per service or path—they do not automatically protect every endpoint or catch every possible sensitive pattern.
- **Proposed status until verified:** All 17 scenarios begin as **proposed / not evaluated**. Unresolved budgets, production topologies, backup procedures, and browser matrices must be agreed and resolved through owning service docs and [[doc|arc42/09-architecture-decisions]] rather than silently weakening criteria.

### Evaluation and evidence

#### Measurement rules

1. **Freeze the scenario conditions.** Record component versions, dataset, environment, dependency behavior, enabled policies, and measurement tools. Production-like does not imply an already documented production topology.
2. **Measure the specified boundary.** A checkout response follows persisted Order creation; vector freshness ends when the correct revision is indexed; SMTP submission and recipient delivery are different outcomes.
3. **Count failures honestly.** Include unexpected errors, timeouts, and incorrect results; report expected validation/authorization rejections separately. Do not remove dependency failures to improve a customer-path SLO.
4. **Preserve per-path results.** Calculate percentiles and error rates per operation group over the stated run. For QR-07, report every change's lag, pending age, and whether its final revision converged; failed or unprocessed changes cannot disappear from the result.
5. **Verify side effects as well as responses.** Inspect authoritative records, queue revisions, saga/outbox state, and captured external calls. A successful HTTP response, message acknowledgement, or health check alone is insufficient.
6. **Use safe fixtures.** Use synthetic customers, data, and sensitive markers in isolated verification environments. Do not deliberately inject production faults or expose real personal data.

#### Evidence record

For each scenario, maintain the following information in the owning team's existing acceptance or release record rather than duplicating test results in this chapter:

### Detailed trade-offs and open agreements

| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Correctness versus latency                           | Checkout uses persisted Basket and current Catalog facts; do not meet a latency target by accepting client prices or stale authoritative purchase facts. | Validate QR-01 and QR-12 together; decide explicit failure behavior for unavailable dependencies.                                        |

| Retry recovery versus duplicate effects              | Retries and durable handoffs can preserve work but may repeat commands or SMTP submissions.                                                              | Evaluate QR-02 and QR-08; record deduplication and uncertain-outcome handling without asserting exactly-once semantics.                  |
| Independent release versus contract coordination     | Compatible implementations can evolve independently; breaking contracts need migration.                                                                  | Agree supported version combinations and release/rollback conditions for QR-03 and QR-04.                                                |
| Diagnosis versus privacy and telemetry cost          | Correlation and persisted state support diagnosis; raw payload logging is not the solution to missing evidence.                                          | Agree sampling, retention, access, and redaction coverage; validate QR-06 and QR-10 together.                                            |
| Availability and recovery versus infrastructure cost | Shared defaults and logical deployment diagrams do not establish redundancy or restore capability.                                                       | Agree per-store backup/restore designs, production probes, dependency treatment, and QR-13/QR-14 budgets.                                |
| AI usefulness versus least privilege                 | Tool and model workflows require explicit permission and data minimization; a denied or failed tool cannot be replaced by invented success.              | Confirm policy coverage, provider data handling, and QR-15 fixtures before extending agent access.                                       |
| Scope versus broad quality coverage                  | Include relevant lower-priority requirements without claiming every standard characteristic is required.                                                 | Agree accessibility/browser scope and QR-17's onboarding fixture; review omitted concerns when business or deployment conditions change. |

Unmet requirements and deferred agreements are inputs to architectural risk and technical-debt review. Resolve them through the owning service documentation and the [[doc|arc42/09-architecture-decisions]] rather than silently weakening acceptance criteria.
