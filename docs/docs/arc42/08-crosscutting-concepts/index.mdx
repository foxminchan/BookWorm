## 1. Context & Problem

In a distributed platform like [[domain|BookWorm]], multiple services and business capabilities share foundational concerns: protecting customer identity and privacy, exchanging messages reliably, validating inputs, diagnosing failures, governing AI features, and safely releasing changes.

Without shared crosscutting practices, each team would reinvent these safeguards differently, creating inconsistent user experiences, hidden security gaps, and false assumptions about what the platform guarantees end to end. This chapter documents the practices and mechanisms that span multiple building blocks without repeating service-specific business logic.

## 2. Business Value

Shared crosscutting concepts create clear, practical benefits across business, operations, and delivery teams:

- **Predictable customer protection:** Consistent identity boundaries, privacy controls, and validation rules reduce the risk of unauthorized access or sensitive-data exposure.
- **Honest operational expectations:** Distinguishing local technical defaults from end-to-end business guarantees prevents teams and stakeholders from assuming unstated latency, availability, or recovery promises.
- **Faster incident resolution:** Common observability signals and explicit failure reporting help operations pinpoint which capability owns a delayed or failed step.
- **Safer, faster delivery:** Shared contract-compatibility checks and verification practices let teams evolve capabilities independently without breaking downstream consumers.

## 3. How It Works

BookWorm organizes its shared practices around four core principles and six focused topic guides:

### Shared principles

1. **Keep ownership local:** A shared mechanism supports a capability; it never transfers ownership of that capability's business data or decisions. Structural boundaries are detailed in [[doc|arc42/05-building-block-view]].
2. **Treat asynchronous outcomes explicitly:** Sending a message, receiving an acceptance acknowledgement, or retrying background work does not prove that the overall business operation succeeded. End-to-end flows are traced in [[doc|arc42/06-runtime-view]].
3. **Distinguish common defaults from universal guarantees:** Some safeguards are applied through shared building blocks, while others are enabled only in specific services. Each guide states its exact scope.
4. **Do not infer unstated service levels:** Source-level defaults do not establish system-wide latency, availability, data retention, or recovery targets. Approved goals belong in [[doc|arc42/01-introduction-and-goals/003-quality-goals]].

### Chapter guides

- **Security and privacy:** [[doc|arc42/08-crosscutting-concepts/001-security-and-privacy]] explains identity boundaries, access control, sensitive-data handling, and where protections are currently enforced.
- **Contracts and messaging:** [[doc|arc42/08-crosscutting-concepts/002-contracts-and-messaging]] covers owned service contracts, message structures, durable message handoffs, and compatibility checks.
- **Validation, errors, and resilience:** [[doc|arc42/08-crosscutting-concepts/003-validation-errors-and-resilience]] explains input validation, consistent error responses, retries, timeouts, duplicate-request handling, and their limits.
- **Observability:** [[doc|arc42/08-crosscutting-concepts/004-observability]] covers shared logging, tracing, metrics, health checks, and diagnostic signals.
- **AI governance:** [[doc|arc42/08-crosscutting-concepts/005-ai-governance]] documents the safeguards applied to the [[domain|conversational]] and [[agent|rating-agent]] workflows.
- **Testing and change safety:** [[doc|arc42/08-crosscutting-concepts/006-testing-and-change-safety]] explains how automated checks and contract verification protect service behavior and boundaries.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Shared defaults are not blanket guarantees:** Registering a capability in a shared library does not automatically mean every service or endpoint uses it. Each concept page explicitly calls out partial coverage so teams do not overestimate protection.
- **Resilience mechanisms can introduce duplicate side effects:** Retries and durable handoffs improve recoverability, but without universal exactly-once guarantees, downstream steps must account for repeated work.
- **Evidence-bound documentation prevents drift:** These guides reflect what is actually evidenced in the BookWorm source checkout, calling out limits directly instead of assuming idealized production behavior.
