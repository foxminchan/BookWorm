## Overview

The **Platform Team** is responsible for the underlying technical foundation that supports BookWorm. It owns the shared systems that make it possible for product, domain, and engineering teams to ship changes without creating operational chaos or hidden dependencies.

This includes the operational layers behind the platform's data stores, async processing, notifications, delivery pipelines, and the support services that enable the product to remain dependable under load and change.

## What this team owns

- shared infrastructure and deployment foundations
- monitoring, alerting, and operational reliability practices
- delivery workflows for safe releases and platform change management
- security and access controls for shared platform capabilities

## Core responsibilities

- Manage and monitor the platform's infrastructure, including databases, queues, storage, and runtime services.
- Maintain CI/CD pipelines and deployment automation for the BookWorm ecosystem.
- Ensure reliable operations through structured observability, alerting, and incident support.
- Protect platform integrity through security, access control, and configuration hygiene.
- Partner with domain teams to keep cross-cutting capabilities aligned with system boundaries.

## Operational scope

The Platform Team is not the owner of business state such as catalog inventory, order status, or basket life cycle. Instead, it provides the shared technical capabilities that those domains depend on, such as compute, messaging, storage, notifications, scheduling, and operational tooling.

In practice, this means the team:

- supports the platform where shared infrastructure is required
- ensures services can be deployed and observed predictably
- helps diagnose cross-domain issues without redefining business ownership
- keeps the operational foundation aligned with documented architecture and guardrails

## Reliability expectations

A strong platform posture for BookWorm includes:

- safe deployments and rollback readiness
- clear alerts for latency, failure, and degraded dependencies
- resilient storage and queue patterns for asynchronous work
- well-defined access boundaries for admin and internal operations

## Shared responsibility model

Platform Team responsibilities are strongest in operational surfaces, but the product and domain teams remain responsible for their own business rules, contracts, and quality expectations. This split reduces ambiguity and keeps ownership clear when a problem involves both business consequences and infrastructure behavior.
