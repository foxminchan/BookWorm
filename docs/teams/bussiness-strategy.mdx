## Overview

The **Business Strategy** team sets the commercial direction for BookWorm. It defines the platform's value proposition, prioritizes investments across the major business domains, and ensures that the architecture remains aligned to customer and commercial outcomes.

This team is the primary owner of the shared business narrative across [[domain|BookWorm]], [[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], and [[domain|reviews]]. It works with engineering and platform partners to translate commercial intent into clear requirements, service boundaries, and product decisions.

## What this team owns

- Product strategy and roadmap decisions for the BookWorm platform
- Business priorities across discovery, conversion, checkout, and order lifecycle
- Definitions of success for customer experience, trust, and commercial outcomes
- Governance for cross-domain trade-offs and scope decisions

## Core responsibilities

- Define the long-term vision and strategic goals for the platform.
- Prioritize feature work across catalog, shopping, ordering, and reviews.
- Interpret customer and market signals into product requirements and experiment themes.
- Ensure each subdomain keeps a clear business purpose and measurable outcomes.
- Align engineering work with commercial risk, customer value, and operating constraints.

## Decision model

The team is accountable for the business framing of BookWorm, but not every implementation detail. It decides what matters most to customers and the business, while the engineering and platform organizations decide how to deliver it safely and reliably.

Examples of business decisions include:

- whether new merchandising capabilities should be treated as Catalog or Shopping changes
- which customer journeys are strategically differentiated versus operational commodities
- how to prioritize reliability and conversion improvements across the funnel
- when a customer-facing change requires new messaging, contract review, or domain coordination

## Cross-team collaboration

The Business Strategy team partners closely with:

- [[team|contributor-team]] for delivery planning and implementation trade-offs
- [[team|platform-team]] for infrastructure and operational constraints
- the domain owners for catalog and ordering when cross-context changes affect business invariants

## Success indicators

The team evaluates outcomes using customer and business signals such as:

- product discoverability and conversion quality
- order acceptance reliability
- customer trust in availability, pricing, and purchase flow
- the clarity of domain boundaries during change and evolution

The business model is intentionally explicit in the catalog so that strategy, architecture, and implementation stay connected.
