## 1. Context & Problem

Conversational AI systems introduce terms—such as request routing, specialist agents, sub-researchers, and handoffs—that can easily overlap with core e-commerce concepts like orders, baskets, or customer reviews. Without an agreed vocabulary across product, business analysis, architecture, and engineering teams, requirements can blur what an AI assistant actually owns versus what belongs to transactional business domains.

This ubiquitous language defines the exact domain terms for BookWorm's Conversational subdomain and draws clear boundaries around data ownership.

## 2. Business Value

- **Shared clarity across roles:** Gives architects, product managers, business analysts, and developers one consistent vocabulary for conversational workflows.
- **Clear ownership boundaries:** Makes explicit that books, baskets, orders, and reviews remain owned by their core contexts even when discussed in chat.
- **Safer feature design:** Prevents policy-answering or routing components from being mistaken for transactional workflows that modify business records.

## 3. How It Works

Each term captures a distinct responsibility in handling customer conversations:

- **Customer Request:** The incoming BookWorm-related message being routed or answered. Referencing a book, basket, or order inside a message does not move ownership of those records into the conversational system.
- **Decision Router:** The entry gatekeeper that verifies whether a request is in English and within BookWorm's scope, then selects the right specialist (`book-agent`, `qa-agent`, or `rating-agent`) without needing a full generative AI conversation step.
- **Book Agent:** The specialist (`book-agent`) that helps customers search for titles and receive recommendations using Catalog data and inline sentiment inference, while Catalog remains the authoritative source of book information.
- **Book Catalog Researcher:** The sub-researcher (`book-catalog-researcher`) that executes structured Catalog MCP lookups on behalf of `book-agent`.
- **Book Recommendation Researcher:** The sub-researcher (`book-recommendation-researcher`) that builds tailored reading recommendations for `book-agent`.
- **QA Agent:** The specialist (`qa-agent`) that answers store-policy and service questions (shipping, returns, accounts, orders, billing) strictly as read-only guidance without changing any business records.
- **Rating Agent:** The specialist (`rating-agent`) in Reviews that summarizes book ratings and feedback without altering persisted reviews.
- **Handoff:** A conversational transfer between specialists—either from the QA Agent to the Book Agent, or from the Book Agent to the Rating Agent. A handoff moves dialogue control only; it does not execute a business process such as placing an order.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Strict read-only separation vs. conversational convenience:** Keeping specialist agents out of transactional mutations protects business integrity, though future capabilities that initiate actions must cross explicit domain boundaries rather than hiding inside conversational handoffs.
- **Non-LLM routing efficiency:** Screening language, scope, and target specialist before engaging a generative specialist avoids unnecessary model cost and latency, at the trade-off of maintaining explicit routing rules alongside agent capabilities.
