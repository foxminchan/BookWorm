## 1. Context & Problem

Book discovery requests vary widely—from quick factual lookups on a known title to open-ended, multi-criteria recommendation inquiries. Treating every inquiry identically creates two risks: simple questions become unnecessarily slow if heavy research runs every time, while complex questions produce shallow or inaccurate results if answered without thorough catalog verification and preference matching. Most importantly, guessing missing titles, prices, or availability damages customer trust.

## 2. Business Value

- **Fast answers for simple queries**: Direct lookups resolve immediately without unnecessary multi-step overhead.
- **Higher-quality recommendations**: Complex requests combine verified catalog facts with tailored preference matching for relevant, deduplicated shortlists.
- **Grounded accuracy**: Refusing to invent titles, authors, prices, availability, or ratings builds lasting customer confidence.

## 3. How It Works

1. **Direct Handling and Clarification**: Straightforward searches and simple book questions are answered directly. When a customer's preferences would materially change a recommendation, the assistant asks clarifying questions before suggesting titles.
2. **Parallel Research for Complex Inquiries**: When a request involves both independent factual verification and preference-based curation, the lead book specialist delegates both tracks concurrently—one researcher gathers confirmed catalog facts while a second researcher prepares a preference-matched shortlist. The lead specialist combines the findings and removes duplicates before replying.
3. **Plan Approval for Multi-Step Research**: When operating in planning mode for multi-step discovery, the assistant presents its proposed research plan and waits for customer approval before starting. Simple, single-step lookups skip this planning cycle.
4. **Transparent Result Presentation**: Recommendations are explained in relation to the customer's stated preferences. Confirmed catalog facts are clearly separated from unavailable details, and any gaps in research evidence are stated openly rather than filled with assumptions.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Responsiveness vs. research depth**: Delegation is reserved for complex multi-part requests; a direct answer or clarifying question is treated as a valid, preferred outcome for simpler queries.
- **Honesty over false completeness**: Explicitly calling out what could not be confirmed prevents fabricated prices, availability, or ratings, even if it leaves a response partial.
- **Approval friction vs. alignment**: Pausing for customer sign-off in planning mode adds a conversational turn, but prevents wasted research effort when requirements are ambiguous.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Primary and delegated agents

- Primary handler: [[agent\|book-agent]]
- Concurrent research specialists (when delegated):
  - [[agent\|book-catalog-researcher]] — returns factual Catalog findings.
  - [[agent\|book-recommendation-researcher]] — prepares a preference-matched shortlist.

### Operational rules

- `Book Agent` synthesizes concurrent outputs from both researchers and deduplicates results before responding.
- **Plan Mode**: Present a plan and wait for customer approval before starting multi-step research; single-step lookups do not require this cycle.
- **Grounding**: Keep factual answers grounded in available Catalog information; never invent titles, authors, availability, ratings, or prices.
- **Presentation**: Relate recommendations to stated preferences, distinguish confirmed Catalog facts from unavailable details, and state explicitly what could not be confirmed when evidence is incomplete.
