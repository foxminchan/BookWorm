## Purpose and scope

This flow explains the lifecycle of an accepted BookWorm purchase. It begins at
checkout, continues through the handoff into Finance, and focuses on the point at
which [[domain|ordering]] changes an Order from **New** to a terminal status.

The key distinction is that Shopping owns the basket and Finance owns the saga
around cleanup and notification work, while Ordering remains the owner of the
business state that represents the accepted purchase.

## Business context

The order lifecycle is the backbone of the commerce flow. A checkout is not just
an API call; it is a handoff from the ephemeral shopping experience into a
business record that must remain valid even after the basket is no longer active.

BookWorm intentionally separates the order's business state from the operational
work triggered after checkout. The Basket is a temporary, user-facing collection
of desired items; the Order is the contractual, traceable record of what was
accepted and what happened afterward. This distinction is what allows Finance,
notifications, and downstream systems to react without rewriting the source of
truth.

### Boundary rules

- Shopping creates the purchase intent and hands it to Ordering.
- Ordering owns the Order record and its lifecycle transitions.
- Finance can trigger cleanup and notification work, but it does not own Order
  state.
- Notification delivery is a downstream effect, not the source of truth for the
  actual Order outcome.

## Participants and responsibilities

| Participant                      | Responsibility                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Customer                         | Completes checkout and expects an accepted purchase to transition through a clear lifecycle.                       |
| [[service\|order-api]]           | Creates the Order from the current basket and stores the accepted purchase in the ordering system.                 |
| [[database\|order-database]]     | Keeps the authoritative order record and its status transitions.                                                   |
| [[service\|order-orchestration]] | Coordinates Finance-side follow-up work such as cleanup and order notifications.                                   |
| [[service\|notification-api]]    | Sends customer-facing completion or cancellation notifications when Ordering publishes the relevant status change. |

## State and recovery model

The Order is the source of truth, while terminal status changes are the durable
business events that other systems respond to. The intended lifecycle is simple:

- Creation produces a new order backed by the accepted cart snapshot.
- Ordering may complete or cancel it according to business rules and process
  outcomes.
- Finance can act on the resulting status change without owning the order itself.

Because status changes are explicit rather than inferred from downstream actions,
operators can reconcile with less ambiguity. If a notification fails, the order
state remains valid; the error is in the delivery path, not the purchase record.

## Why this flow matters

This flow captures the difference between a transaction in progress and a
committed business outcome. It ensures that Ordering remains accountable for the
purchase record, while Finance and notification systems remain downstream
participants that handle side effects. That separation is critical for auditability,
customer trust, and operational troubleshooting.
