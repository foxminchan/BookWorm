## 1. Context & Problem

Checkout is the backbone of BookWorm's commerce journey, bridging a customer's temporary shopping experience with a permanent purchase record. Because basket cleanup, order tracking, and customer emails happen across separate capabilities, chaining everything into one tightly coupled step—or letting multiple teams modify order status—would create fragile checkouts and conflicting records.

[[domain|ordering]] solves this by separating the authoritative purchase record from the operational follow-up work triggered after checkout.

## 2. Business Value

- **Auditability and trust:** Preserves a single, authoritative record of what the customer bought and its final business outcome.
- **Clear team accountability:** Separates shopping intent, order ownership, post-checkout coordination, and customer communication so each capability operates safely.
- **Isolated failure recovery:** If a confirmation email fails to send, the underlying order remains valid—support and operations teams immediately know the issue is in delivery rather than the purchase record itself.

## 3. How It Works

The lifecycle of an accepted purchase follows a clear sequence across well-defined boundaries:

1. **Checkout submission:** A customer submits their shopping basket at checkout.
2. **Order creation:** [[domain|ordering]] verifies the buyer, resolves current catalog prices for the basket items, and records a **New** order snapshot.
3. **Post-checkout coordination:** Once the purchase is recorded, Finance begins coordinating follow-up work—such as cleaning up the old basket—while waiting for explicit order status updates.
4. **Explicit business outcome:** According to business rules and process outcomes, Ordering transitions the order from **New** to a final **Completed** or **Cancelled** status.
5. **Customer notification:** When Ordering announces completion or cancellation, the notification capability sends the corresponding customer email.

### Boundary rules

- Shopping creates the purchase intent and hands it to Ordering.
- Ordering owns the order record and its lifecycle transitions.
- Finance triggers cleanup and notification work, but does not own order state.
- Notification delivery is a downstream effect, never the source of truth for the actual order outcome.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Explicit transitions over inferred outcomes:** Requiring Ordering to explicitly move orders to **Completed** or **Cancelled** eliminates ambiguity during reconciliation, though downstream participants must wait for Ordering's status updates rather than assuming completion from cleanup steps.
- **Decoupled side effects:** Running basket cleanup and email delivery asynchronously keeps checkout responsive and resilient, at the trade-off of eventual consistency between the purchase record and customer notifications.
- **Localized troubleshooting:** Keeping notification delivery outside order state ensures delivery errors do not corrupt purchase history, provided operational monitoring tracks both order outcomes and notification delivery.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Participants and responsibilities

| Participant                      | Responsibility                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Customer                         | Completes checkout and expects an accepted purchase to transition through a clear lifecycle.                       |
| [[service\|order-api]]           | Creates the Order from the current basket and stores the accepted purchase in the ordering system.                 |
| [[database\|order-database]]     | Keeps the authoritative order record and its status transitions.                                                   |
| [[service\|order-orchestration]] | Coordinates Finance-side follow-up work such as cleanup and order notifications.                                   |
| [[service\|notification-api]]    | Sends customer-facing completion or cancellation notifications when Ordering publishes the relevant status change. |

### Flow messages and visualization

- **Inbound command:** `CreateOrder` triggers `order-api` to authenticate the buyer, resolve basket and Catalog prices, and persist the `New` Order in `order-database`.
- **Outcome events:** `OrderStatusChangedToCompleteIntegrationEvent` and `OrderStatusChangedToCancelIntegrationEvent` trigger `notification-api` to render and send completion or cancellation messages.
