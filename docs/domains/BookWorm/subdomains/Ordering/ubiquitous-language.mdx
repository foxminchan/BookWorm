## 1. Context & Problem

Order management touches customer identity, delivery locations, line-item pricing, financial totals, and lifecycle states. Without a shared vocabulary across business analysts, product managers, solution architects, and developers, terms like "item", "total", or "cancelled" can easily be interpreted differently across storefront, checkout, and fulfillment discussions.

This ubiquitous language defines the core concepts and lifecycle rules for BookWorm's Ordering subdomain.

## 2. Business Value

- **Aligned requirements and design**: Gives business and technical stakeholders a single, unambiguous definition for buyers, orders, line items, totals, and lifecycle states.
- **Consistent financial rules**: Makes explicit that line totals and order totals are derived strictly from positive item quantities and non-negative unit prices.
- **Clear lifecycle governance**: Establishes which order states are active versus terminal so workflows, customer communications, and reports stay consistent.

## 3. How It Works

The Ordering vocabulary is organized around customer identity, purchase structure, and lifecycle state:

- **Buyer & Address**: A **Buyer** is the named person associated with one or more orders and holds a delivery **Address** containing street, city, and province details.
- **Order, Order Item & Order Total**: An **Order** is a purchase record belonging to a buyer and containing one or more **Order Items**. Each **Order Item** captures a book reference, a positive quantity, and a non-negative price, with its line total equal to price multiplied by quantity. The **Order Total** is the sum of all order item line totals on that order.
- **Lifecycle States (New, Completed, Cancelled)**: Every order begins in **New** and transitions from **New** to either **Completed** (successfully finished) or **Cancelled** (will not complete). Both **Completed** and **Cancelled** are terminal states.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Strict terminal states**: Treating **Completed** and **Cancelled** as final outcomes prevents ambiguous state reversals in reporting and downstream workflows, meaning any post-completion adjustments must be handled as separate processes rather than reopening a closed order.
- **Derived totals over manual overrides**: Computing **Order Total** strictly from recorded **Order Item** quantities and prices preserves arithmetic integrity, while requiring future discounts or fees to be modeled as explicit items or adjustments if introduced later.
