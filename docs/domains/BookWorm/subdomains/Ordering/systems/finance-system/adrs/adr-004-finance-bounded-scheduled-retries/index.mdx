## Context

Checkout coordination cannot wait indefinitely for downstream progress, and
immediate unbounded retries could amplify a [[service|basket-api]] or messaging outage. [[system|finance-system]]
already has durable saga state and scheduled-message support.
The timeout policy must distinguish a missing cleanup outcome from an
authoritative Order status change and limit how many times Finance republishes
its placement command.

This retrospective ADR records the current timeout algorithm, including its
limitations rather than an idealized retry policy.

## Decision

At saga start, emit [[command|PlaceOrderCommand]] and a local
`PlaceOrderTimeout(OrderId, MaxRetryTimeout)` message. On each timeout,
increment `TimeoutRetryCount`; reissue the command and schedule another timeout
only while the count is less than `MaxAttempts`.

The checked-in settings use `MaxAttempts: 3` and a 30-minute delay. In an
uninterrupted timeout sequence, this means the initial command plus two
reissues; the third timeout exhausts the limit. Delays describe scheduled
intervals, not a guaranteed wall-clock completion deadline.

On exhaustion, mark the saga Cancelled, call `MarkCompleted()`, and emit a
cancellation notification command only when name and email are nonblank.
Do not invoke [[domain|ordering]]'s cancel operation.

The delay is applied between scheduled timeout messages, not as a strict
end-to-end deadline. Basket success reports cleanup progress but does not
cancel the outstanding timeout or complete the saga; consequently, an open
saga can still receive a timeout and reissue placement after cleanup success.
Explicit basket failure ends the saga immediately rather than consuming the
remaining timeout attempts.

## Consequences

- Retries are bounded and their count survives through saga persistence.
- The timeout is a local Wolverine `TimeoutMessage`, not the unused
  `PlaceOrderTimeoutIntegrationEvent` Kafka contract.
- The basket-success handler does not cancel the timeout or change the saga
  state. A still-open saga can therefore receive later timeouts and reissue
  placement even after successful cleanup.
- Explicit basket failure ends the saga immediately instead of consuming
  the remaining timeout attempts.
- Reissued placement commands can repeat Basket and Notification work.
  These workflow retries are distinct from transport-level delivery retries.
- A timeout exhaustion changes Finance's saga state only. It is not evidence
  that Ordering cancelled the Order or that a cancellation notification was
  delivered.
- The current placement-command retry can cause duplicate consumer work
  because the same command is consumed by Basket and Notification; consumers
  and operations must account for repeated processing.
- The configured 30-minute delay and maximum-attempt value do not establish a
  wall-clock completion SLA, especially when scheduling, message delivery, or
  downstream processing is delayed.

## Alternatives considered

### Retry indefinitely

Unbounded retries could keep transient work alive, but may repeatedly amplify
a sustained Basket or messaging outage and leave the saga active indefinitely.

### Retry immediately

Immediate retries would provide little recovery time for a transient outage
and could increase load on an already unhealthy dependency. Scheduled delays
space attempts apart.

### Cancel the Order when Finance retries are exhausted

This would turn a Finance timeout into a business-status mutation and create
a competing Order lifecycle owner. Finance marks its own saga complete and
leaves Order cancellation to Ordering's explicit business workflow.

### Use a Kafka timeout event

The implemented timeout is a local Wolverine `TimeoutMessage`, not the
documented-but-unused `PlaceOrderTimeoutIntegrationEvent`. Treating those as
interchangeable would incorrectly imply that timeout scheduling is a
cross-service integration contract.
