## Context

[[system|order-system]] records accepted purchases and owns their business
status. [[system|finance-system]] currently coordinates basket cleanup and
notifications; it does not authorize, capture, or settle payments. Neither
checkout acceptance, basket deletion, nor Order completion proves that a
payment succeeded.

BookWorm needs a payment gateway without introducing card-number or card
security-code handling into its APIs, databases, messages, logs, or telemetry.
Outsourcing payment processing reduces exposure, but does not remove the
merchant's responsibility to validate PCI DSS compliance.

## Decision

Propose **Stripe as the payment gateway**, using a redirect to
**Stripe-hosted Checkout** for one-time Order payments. This ADR does not
describe an implemented integration or certify BookWorm as PCI DSS compliant.

### Payment boundary and workflow

1. An authenticated customer requests payment for an Order they own. A
   server-side payment integration creates a Checkout Session from the
   persisted purchase snapshot and an explicitly validated currency, never
   from browser-supplied prices or totals. Amounts use Stripe's required
   currency units and rounding rules.
2. Correlate each Session and PaymentIntent with an opaque Order ID and a
   persisted payment-attempt ID. Reuse an idempotency key for retries of the
   same operation, prevent concurrent active attempts from charging the same
   Order twice, and resolve uncertain outcomes before creating another attempt.
3. Redirect the customer to the Stripe-provided Checkout URL. Card entry and
   any required authentication, including 3D Secure, happen on Stripe's page.
   BookWorm must not collect, proxy, store, or publish full card numbers,
   security codes, or other sensitive authentication data.
4. Receive Stripe events over HTTPS and verify the `Stripe-Signature` against
   the unmodified request body with the endpoint's signing secret and the
   official SDK's timestamp tolerance. Reject invalid signatures; never
   disable verification when configuration is missing.
5. Durably record verified events before acknowledging delivery. Deduplicate
   by event ID and make payment transitions idempotent by payment attempt.
   Handle retries and out-of-order events without regressing payment state;
   retrieve the current Stripe object when reconciliation is necessary.
6. Confirm the expected Order, payment attempt, amount, currency, and live/test
   environment before recording payment success. A success-page redirect is
   not proof of payment. For Checkout events, require a paid payment status;
   `checkout.session.completed` alone is insufficient for delayed methods.
   If such methods are enabled, handle asynchronous success and failure.
7. Keep payment state separate from Order status and Finance saga state.
   Ordering remains authoritative for business transitions; payment success
   must not automatically imply fulfillment or Order completion. Basket
   cleanup failure must not silently erase a paid purchase. Late success
   after cancellation or soft deletion requires reconciliation and an explicit
   refund or recovery decision.

### PCI DSS responsibilities

- Target eligibility for **SAQ A** through the fully hosted redirect model.
  Eligibility is conditional on the complete deployment and all applicable
  criteria; confirm the required validation with the acquiring bank and,
  where needed, a Qualified Security Assessor before launch.
- Document the payment data flow, assessed scope, and responsibility split.
  Retain Stripe's current PCI DSS Attestation of Compliance and review its
  service-provider compliance status at least annually. Complete BookWorm's
  required annual assessment and attestation; Stripe's certification does not
  certify the merchant.
- Protect the storefront and redirect-creation path against tampering. Apply
  the applicable PCI DSS controls for secure changes, vulnerability management,
  access control, monitoring, incident response, and required scanning or
  testing. Confirm requirements against the current PCI DSS and SAQ rather
  than treating this ADR as an exhaustive compliance checklist.
- Use HTTPS with TLS 1.2 or newer. Keep API keys and webhook signing secrets
  in managed secret storage, separate test and live credentials, grant least
  privilege, rotate secrets, and require MFA for administrative access.
- Persist only necessary provider references, amounts, currency, and payment
  state. Apply explicit retention and access controls to payment records.
  Exclude raw payment payloads, secrets, and sensitive customer data from
  logs, traces, event contracts, and Stripe metadata.

### Acceptance and rollout gates

Before accepting this ADR or enabling live payments:

- Assign the payment integration's service owner and persistence boundary.
  This proposal does not implicitly turn the existing Finance saga into a
  payment processor.
- Define payment outcome contracts, authorization rules, currency policy,
  cancellation and refund handling, reconciliation jobs, and operational
  alerts. Preserve Order ownership and review the current soft-deletion and
  basket-cleanup behavior for races with payment processing.
- Test successful and declined payments, authentication, abandoned Sessions,
  duplicate requests and webhooks, invalid signatures, delayed and reordered
  events, provider timeouts, amount mismatches, and payment/cancellation races.
  Verify that card data never reaches BookWorm storage, messaging, or telemetry.
- Obtain documented approval of the PCI DSS scope, validation approach, and
  required compliance evidence. Roll out behind a disabled-by-default payment
  feature gate with a reconciliation and incident-response runbook.

## Consequences

- Stripe hosts the card-entry surface and handles payment authentication,
  reducing BookWorm's card-data exposure and integration effort.
- BookWorm retains compliance, storefront security, and operational duties;
  neither hosted Checkout nor this proposal guarantees SAQ A eligibility.
- Hosted checkout limits UI customization and introduces provider availability,
  fees, account eligibility, and vendor dependency considerations.
- Reliable payment processing requires durable payment state, duplicate-safe
  event handling, refunds, and reconciliation beyond the existing checkout
  saga. Current Order and Finance behavior remains unchanged until implementation.

## Alternatives considered

- **Collect card data through BookWorm APIs:** rejected because it expands the
  cardholder-data environment and operational compliance burden.
- **Embedded Checkout or Stripe Elements:** defer unless customization needs
  justify another integration and a fresh PCI DSS scope review.
- **Another hosted payment provider:** remains viable if Stripe's commercial,
  regional, or payment-method support does not meet BookWorm's requirements.
