## 1. Context & Problem

Ordering is a **Core Domain** and **Business Critical** area of BookWorm because it owns the accepted customer purchase and its business outcome. While [[domain|shopping]] manages mutable customer [[entity|basket]] intent and [[domain|catalog]] manages live book listings and pricing, the business needs a separate, authoritative home for committed purchases.

Without a clear boundary around Ordering, temporary cart changes, later catalog price updates, or failures in post-checkout cleanup and email delivery could corrupt what a customer actually bought or obscure the true status of their order.

Proposed carrier delivery targets and service terms are documented in the neighboring `sla.yaml` file.

## 2. Business Value

- **Authoritative purchase records:** Converts an authenticated buyer's basket into a durable Order capturing buyer identity, items, purchase-time prices, and calculated totals.
- **Protected pricing integrity:** Selects and locks in the purchase-time price (sale price when active, otherwise regular price) so future catalog changes never rewrite past purchases.
- **Unambiguous lifecycle management:** Moves every Order cleanly from **New** to a terminal **Completed** or **Cancelled** state.
- **Safe post-checkout coordination:** Allows Finance to coordinate basket cleanup and customer notifications independently without taking ownership of the Order model.

## 3. How It Works

### Domain scope and language

- **Order:** A purchase record associated with a Buyer containing one or more Order items.
- **Order item:** Records a book reference, positive quantity, and non-negative price resolved from [[domain|catalog]] at creation; its line total equals price multiplied by quantity.
- **Order total:** The sum of all item line totals recorded on the Order.
- **Lifecycle states:** **New**, **Completed**, and **Cancelled** (see [[diagram|order-lifecycle]]).

Important distinctions:

- A [[entity|basket]] in [[domain|shopping]] is temporary shopping intent; an Order is the permanent purchase record.
- An Order item retains a book reference, quantity, and price, not ownership of Catalog's live product descriptions.
- An Order status is not proof of external payment-provider settlement or shipping carrier completion.
- Finance's follow-up workflow state is separate from Ordering's Order status; for example, a Finance timeout can close its own coordination workflow without cancelling the Order.

### Checkout and follow-up flow

1. **Order creation:** An authenticated buyer submits checkout. Ordering reads the buyer's basket, retrieves current book prices from [[domain|catalog]] (using the sale price when present, otherwise the regular price), and creates the Order in **New** status.
2. **Finance handoff:** Ordering notifies Finance of the checkout along with the order reference, basket reference, buyer contact details, and total.
3. **Basket cleanup:** Finance begins post-checkout coordination and asks Shopping to remove the checked-out basket, retrying up to its configured attempt limit if no outcome arrives.
4. **Cleanup outcomes:** If basket removal succeeds, Finance reports success to Ordering, which logs the outcome without changing the Order status. If removal fails, Finance reports the failure; if the Order still exists, Ordering marks it as removed from active views, and Finance skips sending an order email on this failure path.
5. **Completion or cancellation:** When Ordering moves an Order from **New** to **Completed** or **Cancelled**, Finance finishes its coordination and asks the notification capability to send the corresponding email whenever buyer contact details are present.

If Finance's coordination workflow exhausts its retries, it marks its own workflow cancelled and triggers a cancellation email when possible—but it does **not** cancel the Order in Ordering. Only an explicit cancellation inside Ordering moves an Order to **Cancelled**.

### Business invariants

- An Order is created for the authenticated buyer from the basket and catalog data available at creation time.
- An Order contains one or more items; each item has a positive quantity and non-negative price, and the Order total equals the sum of its item line totals.
- An Order moves from **New** to either **Completed** or **Cancelled**; both outcomes are terminal.
- Basket-removal success does not change Order status; basket-removal failure marks the Order as removed if it still exists.
- Finance workflow cancellation after retries and downstream notification steps do not change Order status.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Single lifecycle owner vs. centralized workflow control:** Keeping Order status exclusively inside Ordering prevents Finance or notification steps from overwriting purchase truth, at the cost of requiring explicit coordination between Order status and Finance workflow state.
- **Distinct operational outcomes:** Separating Order status, Finance workflow state, basket deletion, record removal, and email delivery isolates partial failures, though operations and support teams must understand that a timed-out cleanup workflow or failed email does not equal a cancelled Order.
- **Purchase-time snapshots:** Recording selected prices at checkout protects historical accuracy while leaving product descriptions in [[domain|catalog]].
- **Operational expectations:** Authenticated buyer and admin access rules protect order actions; duplicate-safe operations prevent accidental repeat orders or transitions; and shared order references keep cross-team troubleshooting aligned.

### Technical checkout and Finance integration flow

1. The authenticated buyer calls `POST /orders`. Ordering reads that buyer's basket and requests the corresponding books from Catalog.
2. Ordering selects the sale price when present, otherwise the regular price, creates the Order with status `New`, and publishes [[event|UserCheckedOutIntegrationEvent]] with the Order ID, basket ID, buyer name and email, and total.
3. Finance starts an Order saga and issues [[command|PlaceOrderCommand]] to Basket API to delete the basket. If no deletion outcome arrives, Finance retries this command on its configured timeout up to its configured attempt limit.
4. On successful deletion, Finance sends [[command|DeleteBasketCompleteCommand]] to Ordering. Ordering logs this outcome; it does not change the Order status.
5. On failed deletion, Finance sends [[command|DeleteBasketFailedCommand]] to Ordering. If the Order exists, Ordering soft-deletes it. Finance records the saga as basket-deletion failed and does not send an order email from this failure path.
6. Completing or cancelling an Order through Ordering changes its status from `New` and publishes [[event|OrderStatusChangedToCompleteIntegrationEvent]] or [[event|OrderStatusChangedToCancelIntegrationEvent]]. Finance consumes that event, completes its saga, and sends a completion or cancellation command to Notification when the buyer name and email are present.

Create, complete, and cancel endpoints require an idempotency key and are scoped by the authenticated buyer or admin policy.
