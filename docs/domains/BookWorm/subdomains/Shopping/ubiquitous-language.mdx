## 1. Context & Problem

The Shopping subdomain captures what a customer intends to purchase before an order is formally placed. Without a shared vocabulary across business analysts, product managers, solution architects, and developers, pre-order terms such as "basket", "item", "customer", and "checkout" are easily confused with confirmed orders, catalog records, or inventory holds.

This ubiquitous language defines the authoritative meanings and boundaries for Shopping concepts across BookWorm.

## 2. Business Value

- **Clear pre-order boundaries:** Keeps teams from treating a mutable shopping basket as a committed order, an inventory reservation, or a copy of the product catalog.
- **Unambiguous identity handoff:** Clarifies that the customer identifier used to address a basket is distinct from the buyer identifier managed during ordering.
- **Aligned checkout expectations:** Establishes that checkout starts cross-team order coordination rather than immediately completing or cancelling an order.

## 3. How It Works

The Shopping vocabulary is organized around pre-purchase state, customer addressing, and checkout initiation:

- **Basket & Basket Item:** A **Basket** is a customer's current, changeable collection of items before purchase—representing shopping intent rather than an order, inventory reservation, or catalog copy. Each **Basket Item** holds a book identifier and a positive quantity, without embedding the catalog book record or an order price.
- **Customer Identifier:** The **Customer Identifier** addresses a customer's basket within Shopping and is not automatically identical to the buyer identifier used by Ordering.
- **Checkout & Checked-out Event:** **Checkout** is the action that initiates order placement for a basket, starting coordination across Shopping, Ordering, and the order workflow rather than completing or cancelling an order directly. The **Checked-out Event** is the integration message that carries order and basket identifiers, buyer contact details, and total money into that cross-context workflow.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Slim basket items:** Excluding catalog book details and locked order prices from **Basket Item** keeps shopping intent lightweight and prevents stale catalog data, while requiring live catalog enrichment for display and explicit price recording during order creation.
- **Decoupled customer and buyer identities:** Separating **Customer Identifier** from Ordering's buyer identity protects bounded-context autonomy, requiring explicit identity mapping when checkout begins.
- **Checkout as workflow initiation:** Treating **Checkout** and **Checked-out Event** as the start of coordination—rather than a synchronous completion guarantee—allows downstream workflows to run independently and handle failures or retries explicitly.
