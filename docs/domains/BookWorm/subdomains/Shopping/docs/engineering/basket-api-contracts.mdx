## 1. Context & Problem

Customers browsing the storefront need a reliable place to hold the books they intend to buy before they commit to an order. Without a dedicated, well-defined service boundary for this pre-order selection, shopping cart logic easily becomes tangled with product catalog administration, order history, payment processing, and fulfilment.

This engineering reference defines the contract and boundaries for [[service|basket-api]] inside [[system|cart-system]]. It establishes what the basket service owns—active, customer-scoped [[entity|basket]] state—and explicitly separates that temporary shopping intent from [[domain|catalog]] product metadata, Orders, payments, and fulfilment.

## 2. Business Value

- **Clear team and service ownership:** Separates pre-order cart management from catalog, ordering, and payment teams so capabilities can evolve independently without tight coupling.
- **Accurate customer pricing and product info:** Prevents stale titles or outdated prices from lingering in saved carts by fetching live product details whenever a customer views their basket.
- **Consistent checkout handoff:** Guarantees that post-checkout cleanup produces an explicit success or failure signal, protecting customers from accidental duplicate purchases and helping operations resolve cleanup issues quickly.
- **Predictable client integration:** Standardizes validation and error behavior so storefront and conversational channels handle missing or invalid baskets consistently.

## 3. How It Works

Every basket operation applies strictly to the currently signed-in customer, keeping each shopper's active selection isolated from everyone else's.

1. **Creating and updating a basket:** A customer starts a basket by selecting at least one [[entity|book]] with a quantity greater than zero. Whenever the customer changes their selection, the entire set of requested items is replaced as a single unit rather than merged piecemeal, keeping the basket state predictable.
2. **Reading and enriching a basket:** Stored baskets hold only book references and requested quantities—never copied titles or prices. When a customer opens their basket, the service looks up the distinct books from the product catalog and enriches the view with each book's current name, regular price, and sale price when available. If a book is no longer found in the catalog, the stored [[entity|basket-item]] is still preserved without inventing placeholder details or silently altering the saved basket.
3. **Handling errors and missing baskets:** Invalid requests—such as empty creation requests or non-positive quantities—are rejected with clear validation feedback. A missing basket is reported explicitly rather than masked as an empty cart.
4. **Cleaning up after checkout:** Once checkout finishes handing off a purchase, the basket is deleted and exactly one explicit cleanup outcome is published—either confirming deletion succeeded or reporting that deletion failed—so downstream workflows can correlate the result without guessing.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Full-set replacement vs. partial patching:** Replacing the complete item collection on updates is slightly heavier per update than incremental item patching, but it avoids partial-merge conflicts and keeps client and server basket state deterministic.
- **Read-time enrichment vs. stored product snapshots:** Keeping Catalog fields out of basket storage guarantees shoppers see live prices and titles without complex catalog-sync jobs, at the cost of a read-time lookup to Catalog and graceful degradation when a book is unavailable.
- **Read purity over silent self-healing:** Preserving unresolvable items on read rather than silently deleting them prevents unexpected data loss during temporary Catalog outages, leaving removal decisions to the customer or storefront.
- **Explicit cleanup outcomes vs. implicit assumptions:** Publishing distinct success and failure outcomes adds messaging contracts to maintain, but prevents downstream consumers from misinterpreting a basket cleanup event as payment or Order completion.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Service boundary and ownership

- **Service:** [[service|basket-api]] (internal HTTP and gRPC service in [[system|cart-system]])
- **Owned state:** Active customer-scoped [[entity|basket]] state stored in [[container|basket-database]] under customer-scoped persistence keys (`basket:{customerId}`)
- **Out of scope:** Catalog metadata, Orders, payment state, and fulfilment

### HTTP contract

All operations require authorization and derive the customer identity from the authenticated request:

| Method   | Path              | Responsibility                       | Success          |
| -------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------- |
| `POST`   | `/api/v1/baskets` | Create a basket from initial items   | `201 Created`    |
| `GET`    | `/api/v1/baskets` | Read and enrich the current basket   | `200 OK`         |
| `PUT`    | `/api/v1/baskets` | Replace the complete item collection | `204 No Content` |
| `DELETE` | `/api/v1/baskets` | Delete the current basket            | `204 No Content` |

Validation failures use the documented problem-details responses. A missing basket is not silently converted into an empty successful response.

### Domain invariants

- A create request contains at least one item.
- Every item has a Catalog [[entity|book]] ID.
- Every quantity is greater than zero.
- An update replaces the complete item collection; it does not merge arbitrary state.
- The persistence key is customer-scoped (`basket:{customerId}`).
- Basket state remains transient pre-order state.

### Persistence and Catalog enrichment

- [[container|basket-database]] is authoritative for active basket contents; keep Catalog fields out of the stored basket.
- On `GET`, resolve distinct book IDs through the documented gRPC query and enrich the response with the current name, regular price, and sale price when available.
- If Catalog has no matching record, preserve the stored [[entity|basket-item]] and omit unavailable presentation details. Do not invent product data or mutate the basket as a side effect of a read.

### Integration events

After checkout cleanup, publish one explicit outcome:

- [[event|BasketDeletedCompleteIntegrationEvent]] only after deletion succeeds.
- [[event|BasketDeletedFailedIntegrationEvent]] when deletion fails.

Use the corresponding channels and preserve the order and basket identifiers needed for correlation. Consumers must be idempotent and must not infer payment or Order completion from either event.

### Change checklist

Before changing the contract:

1. Update the relevant command, query, or event schema.
2. Update the OpenAPI, AsyncAPI, or gRPC specification in the service folder.
3. Check validation and problem-details behavior.
4. Check authentication and customer scoping.
5. Check Catalog enrichment behavior for missing products and duplicate IDs.
6. Check both cleanup outcome paths and their channel routing.
7. Update the related ADR or resource documentation when the boundary or consistency behavior changes.
