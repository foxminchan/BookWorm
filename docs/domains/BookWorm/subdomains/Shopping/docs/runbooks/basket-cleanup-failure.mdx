## 1. Context & Problem

After a customer submits a purchase at checkout, the shopping basket must be deleted so the same selection cannot be accidentally submitted a second time. Occasionally, transient infrastructure outages, message delivery errors, or data mismatches prevent that cleanup from completing—leaving the submitted [[entity|basket]] still active and emitting a cleanup-failure notification.

Operations and engineering teams need a clear, standardized procedure to diagnose why cleanup failed and recover safely without deleting a customer's cart prematurely when an order was not actually accepted.

## 2. Business Value

- **Protects customer trust:** Prevents accidental deletion of a customer's cart before confirming whether their purchase was accepted by Ordering.
- **Eliminates duplicate orders:** Ensures lingering baskets from accepted orders are cleanly removed so customers cannot inadvertently re-submit the same purchase.
- **Faster, safer incident resolution:** Gives on-call teams a repeatable triage path that avoids risky manual database edits and preserves a complete audit trail.
- **Clear escalation paths:** Routes ambiguous order states to the right domain owners early, reducing downtime and cross-team handoff delays.

## 3. How It Works

When a basket cleanup failure is detected, responders follow a cautious, verify-first workflow:

1. **Gather context without modifying state:** Collect the incident identifiers—such as the customer, basket, and order identifiers, along with environment and timing details—and verify whether the failure was caused by a temporary outage or a deeper data mismatch.
2. **Confirm the downstream order outcome first:** Before touching the basket, check whether the downstream order workflow accepted, rejected, or is still processing the purchase. A cleanup failure does **not** mean the order or payment failed.
3. **Execute the matching recovery path:**
   - **Temporary service or delivery issue:** Restore the unhealthy dependency or message consumer, then replay the failed cleanup request through approved recovery tooling until a completion outcome confirms the basket is gone.
   - **Order accepted, basket still present:** Verify that the order and basket match the same checkout attempt, trigger the approved repeatable cleanup action, and confirm the basket is no longer accessible.
   - **Order rejected or status unclear:** Stop immediately without deleting the basket and escalate to the Ordering and Finance owners until the purchase outcome is confirmed.
4. **Verify and document:** Confirm that a completion outcome is emitted only after actual deletion, that the customer sees a consistent checkout state, and that the incident record keeps the original failure details without exposing customer secrets or credentials.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Order verification before deletion vs. immediate cleanup:** Pausing to confirm the downstream order status adds an investigation step during incidents, but prevents irreversible cart loss if checkout actually failed.
- **Approved replay/recovery actions vs. direct database edits:** Preferring repeatable, audited recovery flows over manual database mutations keeps downstream consumers synchronized and preserves auditability.
- **Strict data-hygiene in incident logs:** Retaining correlation identifiers while excluding credentials and sensitive customer secrets enables thorough post-incident auditing without creating security exposure.
- **Distinguishing transient vs. contract errors:** Separating temporary availability/delivery issues from schema or data mismatches prevents endless message replays on permanently broken payloads.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Trigger conditions

Use this procedure when [[event|BasketDeletedFailedIntegrationEvent]] is published on [[channel|bookworm.&#123;env&#125;.basket.delete-failed]], or when monitoring reports that a submitted [[entity|basket]] remains available after checkout.

### Safety rules

- Treat the event as a cleanup failure, not as an Order or payment failure.
- Confirm the customer and basket identifiers before taking action.
- Do not delete a basket manually until the downstream order workflow has confirmed whether the purchase was accepted.
- Prefer an idempotent replay or the platform's normal recovery command over a direct database mutation.

### Investigation steps

1. Record the event ID, environment, timestamp, customer identifier, basket identifier, and order identifier.
2. Check the [[service|basket-api]] logs for the delete request and the underlying database error.
3. Check the [[channel|bookworm.&#123;env&#125;.basket.delete-failed]] consumer logs for delivery or processing failures.
4. Check the downstream order workflow to establish whether the Order was created, is still pending, or was rejected.
5. Determine whether the failure is transient (availability, timeout, or broker delivery) or requires engineering intervention (data or contract mismatch).

### Recovery procedures

#### Transient service or delivery failure

1. Restore the affected dependency or consumer.
2. Replay the failed integration message using the approved broker tooling.
3. Verify that the basket deletion succeeds and that a completion event is emitted.
4. Confirm that the completion event is processed exactly once by each downstream consumer.

#### Order accepted but basket still exists

1. Confirm the Order ID and basket ID belong to the same checkout attempt.
2. Run the approved idempotent basket-deletion recovery action.
3. Verify the customer-scoped basket is no longer available.
4. Attach the recovery result to the incident and retain the original failure event for auditability.

#### Order not accepted or status is ambiguous

Stop before deleting the basket. Escalate to the [[domain|ordering]]/Finance owner with the identifiers and evidence collected above. The basket must not be removed until the purchase outcome is resolved.

### Verification criteria

- The original failure is correlated to a recovery action.
- A successful deletion produces [[event|BasketDeletedCompleteIntegrationEvent]].
- No completion event is emitted while the basket remains present.
- The customer sees a consistent checkout result and cannot accidentally submit the same accepted purchase again.

### Escalation

Escalate persistent failures to the [[domain|shopping]] owner ([[team|contributor-team]]) with the event payload, correlation IDs, dependency health, and the last successful cleanup timestamp. Do not include credentials or full customer secrets in the incident record.
