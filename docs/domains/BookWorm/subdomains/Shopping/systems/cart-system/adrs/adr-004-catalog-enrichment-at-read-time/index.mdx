## Context

Customers need names and current price information while reviewing a
[[entity|basket]], but [[domain|catalog]] is the owner of
[[entity|book]] metadata.
Copying those fields into the basket store would create stale duplicated data
and an ownership conflict.

A basket stores a book ID and requested quantity, not a quoted or reserved
price. The Basket API can collect the distinct book IDs and use Catalog's
batch gRPC query when serving a read, avoiding a separate lookup for every
line item while keeping product presentation data with its owner.

## Decision

The [[system|cart-system]] persists only book identifiers and positive requested
quantities. On a basket read, [[service|basket-api]] resolves distinct
[[entity|book]]
identifiers through the Catalog gRPC contract and enriches the response with
the currently available name, regular price, and sale price. Missing Catalog
records remain missing from the presentation; the stored basket is not
silently rewritten and product details are not invented. This response is
current presentation data, not a price guarantee, inventory reservation, or
checkout snapshot.

## Consequences

- Catalog remains the source of truth for book presentation data.
- Basket reads depend on Catalog availability for complete enrichment.
- The persistence model stays small and avoids duplicated metadata.
- Resolving distinct IDs in a batch avoids per-item Catalog calls as basket
  size grows.
- An unavailable or unmatched Catalog record does not authorize the Basket
  service to manufacture details or alter the saved selection.
- Price and product validity at purchase time must still be established by
  the downstream checkout workflow; checkout must not treat a basket's
  displayed price as an accepted price.
- This decision does not define stale-data caching or a specific response
  policy for Catalog transport failures; those behaviors must remain explicit
  in the service contract and implementation.
