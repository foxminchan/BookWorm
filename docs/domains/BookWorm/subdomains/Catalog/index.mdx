## Business purpose

Catalog is the authoritative business capability for the [[entity|book]]s BookWorm
offers. It turns [[entity|publisher]] and [[entity|author]] information into a trusted, searchable
product proposition that can be used by [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|reviews]],
and other customer-facing experiences.

The subdomain is strategically classified as a **Core Domain** because the
quality of its product information directly affects discovery, conversion, and
the consistency of every downstream purchase decision.

### Business outcomes

- Customers can discover [[entity|book]]s using meaningful
  [[entity|category]], [[entity|author]], and [[entity|publisher]] metadata.
- Customers see a consistent price and availability proposition before
  checkout.
- [[domain|shopping]] can use Catalog information without owning or changing Catalog
  business rules.
- [[domain|reviews]] can associate [[entity|feedback]] with a stable
  [[entity|book]] identity without taking
  ownership of book metadata.
- Business and engineering teams have one explicit contract for managing
  catalog information.

## Domain scope and language

Catalog owns the product representation of a book and its descriptive
relationships:

- [[entity|book]] is the aggregate root and stable product identity.
- [[entity|author]], [[entity|publisher]], and [[entity|category]] provide
  discoverability and classification metadata.
- [[entity|book-author]] models the many-to-many relationship between books and
  authors.
- [[domain|reviews]] contributes rating information as a customer-feedback
  projection; Catalog does not own individual reviews.

Important distinctions:

- **Availability** describes whether a Catalog book can currently be offered.
  It is not a stock reservation owned by Shopping or a fulfilment promise
  owned by Ordering.
- **Price** is part of the Catalog product proposition. The checkout process
  must still establish its own purchase-time validity before accepting an
  order.
- **Book identity** is shared through a stable identifier and published
  contracts, not by sharing the Catalog entity or database.

## Contract and interaction surface

At the architecture level, consumers should depend on published identifiers
and representations rather than Catalog persistence:

1. A catalog business user maintains product information through the Catalog
   API.
2. Catalog validates and stores the aggregate and its relationships.
3. Customer-facing contexts read a published product representation.
4. Shopping validates the purchase-time proposition before accepting checkout.
5. Downstream contexts preserve their own models and do not mutate Catalog
   state directly.

## Business invariants

- Every active book has a stable identity.
- A book references valid category and publisher identities.
- A discount price cannot be negative or greater than the original price.
- An unavailable book must not be presented as available for a new purchase.
- Catalog updates must not silently rewrite an accepted Order.
- Reviews and Orders may retain historical snapshots, but Catalog remains the
  owner of the current product description.

## Architecture principles

- **Bounded ownership:** Catalog owns book metadata and product presentation;
  [[domain|shopping]] owns [[entity|basket]] and checkout decisions;
  [[domain|ordering]] owns order lifecycle.
- **Published language:** Downstream contexts consume explicit product
  contracts rather than importing Catalog entities.
- **Read consistency over coupling:** Product discovery may use a projection,
  while purchase acceptance must verify the current purchase proposition.
- **Evolution by contract:** API and message changes should be versioned and
  remain backward compatible where downstream consumers require it.
- **Traceable decisions:** Business-critical changes to price, availability, or
  product identity should be auditable.

## Quality attributes and operational concerns

| Concern       | High-level expectation                                                          |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Availability  | Product discovery should remain available independently of Reviews and Ordering |
| Consistency   | The purchase path must not accept stale price or availability data              |
| Performance   | Browsing and product lookup should support low-latency reads                    |
| Evolvability  | Consumers should be insulated from internal persistence changes                 |
| Observability | Catalog changes and rejected updates should be traceable by product identity    |
| Security      | Catalog administration is restricted to authorized business users               |

## Architectural decisions to confirm

These are intentionally left as solution-architecture checkpoints rather than
invented decisions:

- Whether downstream product data is delivered synchronously, by events, or by
  both patterns.
- Whether availability is mastered in Catalog or integrated from an inventory
  capability.
- Whether rating summary is refreshed synchronously or through a Reviews
  projection.
- The retention and audit policy for historical price and availability
  snapshots.
