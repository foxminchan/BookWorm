## 1. Context & Problem

Customers find books in two distinct ways: structured browsing (filtering by category, publisher, author, or price range) and open-ended text search (entering keywords or natural phrases). Traditional database filtering alone struggles with semantic or fuzzy discovery, while serving book prices and availability directly from a search engine risks showing stale commercial details. BookWorm combines both capabilities while setting clear expectations around data freshness.

## 2. Business Value

- **Better discovery conversion:** Blending meaning-based and keyword matching helps customers locate relevant titles even without exact titles or keywords.
- **Accurate prices and stock in search results:** Hydrating displayed results from the authoritative catalog store ensures search pages never show outdated prices or cover data from the search index.
- **Faster issue triage:** Gives product, support, and engineering teams a shared diagnostic map to tell indexing delays apart from caching or expired image-link behavior.

## 3. How It Works

### Two ways to find books

- **Browse with filters and paging:** When consumers filter by [[entity|category]], [[entity|publisher]], [[entity|author]], or price bounds—along with sorting and paging—results are queried directly from the primary catalog store.
- **Free-text discovery:** When consumers provide a search term, the catalog generates a semantic representation of the query, combines meaning-based and keyword matching in the search index to find candidate book matches, and then loads the current records from the primary catalog store before applying filters and paging.

### Consistency expectations

- **Eventually consistent search index:** A newly added or edited [[entity|book]] may not appear in free-text search until the next background indexing batch finishes.
- **Authoritative result details:** Displayed attributes (title, price, availability, and image) always come from the primary catalog store, never from the search index record.
- **Deletion transition window:** Deleted books are removed from the search index during background ingestion; during a brief window before cleanup completes, the search index may return a deleted candidate whose record is no longer visible when loaded from the primary store.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Eventual search consistency vs. write speed:** Updating the search index asynchronously keeps catalog edits responsive and resilient to search-engine outages, at the cost of a short delay before text search reflects new or edited titles (see [[adr|adr-003-database-queue-for-vector-indexing]]).
- **Candidate lookup plus primary hydration:** Using the search index only to rank candidate identifiers and loading full records from the primary catalog store adds a second lookup step, but guarantees authoritative pricing and availability on every search response (see [[adr|adr-002-hybrid-vector-search-qdrant]]).
- **Clear symptom boundaries:** Because search touches the vector index, read cache, and expiring media links, teams should separate indexing lag from stale cache entries and expired image links before escalating.
