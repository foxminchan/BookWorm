## Context

Product listings need to show ratings and sort or filter by them. Querying the
[[domain|reviews]] domain for every listing would couple catalog reads to
Reviews availability, but copying full [[entity|feedback]] into Catalog would
blur ownership and duplicate review content and lifecycle decisions. Catalog
needs a local summary for product discovery without taking ownership of
individual feedback records.

## Decision

Reviews remains the owner of individual [[entity|feedback]].
[[service|catalog-api]] handles Reviews feedback integration messages and
updates a derived average rating and review count on the [[entity|book]] in
[[container|catalog-database]]. A feedback-created message adds its rating; a
feedback-deleted message removes that rating. The integration contract
identifies both the book and feedback, and the feedback ID is used when
reporting a failed update for a missing book. No individual feedback records
are stored in Catalog.

If a created-feedback message refers to a book that does not exist, Catalog
publishes [[event|BookUpdatedRatingFailedIntegrationEvent]]. If a
feedback-deleted message refers to a missing book, the handler performs no
local update because there is no Catalog projection to correct.

## Decision drivers

Catalog needs rating information for browsing and ranking, but it must not
become the owner of customer feedback data. The Reviews domain is the
authoritative place for the actual review content, moderation decisions, and
future customer-specific decisions.

Maintaining the aggregate beside each book lets product reads return the
summary with the catalog record, while asynchronous integration messages keep
the domain boundary explicit. The projection can be reconciled from Reviews
data if messages fail or the stored summary drifts.

## Alternatives considered

### Copy the full feedback records and summaries into Catalog

This would make feedback queries available locally, but it would create an
ownership conflict and risk inconsistent moderation or deletion logic across
domains. Catalog would inherit more of the Reviews domain's data lifecycle
than it needs for product discovery.

### Let Shopping and customer experiences read Reviews directly

This would increase coupling between subdomains and create a runtime
dependency on Reviews for product listing and discovery. A summary projection
lets Catalog answer common book reads locally, while Reviews remains the
authority for feedback details.

### Make the rating summary a separate fact table without relation to the book

This would keep the summary separate from the book row, but require additional
lookup or join work for a value that is displayed as part of the product
listing. Keeping the average rating and count on each book aligns the
projection with the browsing experience without transferring ownership of
feedback.

## Consistency and recovery constraints

- The summary is a projection, not editable Catalog-owned source data.
  Business-user book create and update operations must not treat
  `average_rating` or `total_reviews` as writable product fields.
- The projection is eventually consistent with Reviews. Delayed or failed
  integration-message handling can leave a book's summary stale; inspect
  message delivery and Catalog handler logs before applying any correction.
- Reviews remains the source for repair and reconciliation. Replay failed
  messages or recalculate the summary from Reviews data through an approved
  recovery process; do not invent rating values by editing the book directly.
- A created-feedback message for an unknown book is surfaced through the
  rating-update-failed event. A deleted-feedback message for an unknown book
  is a no-op, since there is no local book projection to update.
- Rating summaries are not part of the Qdrant search record. Search-index
  membership and rating freshness are separate concerns.

## Consequences

- Catalog listings can return rating summaries without making a synchronous
  read to Reviews for each book.
- Reviews remains authoritative for feedback, while Catalog is responsible
  for the correctness and observability of its local projection.
- Ratings are eventually consistent with Reviews and may require message
  replay or reconciliation after delivery or handler failures.
- Catalog handles the missing-book case explicitly for created-feedback
  messages and treats deletion for an unknown book as a no-op.
- Rating summaries are not part of the vector index and do not affect semantic
  or keyword candidate generation.
