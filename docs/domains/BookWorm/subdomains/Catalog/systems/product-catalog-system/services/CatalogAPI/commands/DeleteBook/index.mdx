## 1. Context & Problem

As a digital bookstore evolves, some titles must be permanently removed from the active catalog—for example, when duplicate entries are created by mistake, test records need cleanup, or a book is permanently withdrawn from sale. Without a dedicated, controlled way to delete a [[entity|book]], outdated or erroneous listings would remain visible to customers and staff, cluttering search results and catalog reports.

## 2. Business Value

- **Catalog accuracy:** Keeps the product catalog clean by removing duplicate, invalid, or retired titles so shoppers only see relevant offerings.
- **Operational control:** Gives catalog managers and automated tools a direct, single-step action to remove a specific title using its unique identifier.
- **Clear outcomes:** Confirms immediately whether the removal succeeded or whether the requested title was not present in the catalog, preventing silent mistakes.

## 3. How It Works

1. A caller or back-office user selects the specific [[entity|book]] to remove by supplying its unique identifier. No additional descriptive details are needed.
2. The catalog service looks up the matching book record.
3. If the book exists, it is permanently removed from the catalog and the caller receives an empty confirmation indicating the deletion is complete.
4. If no book matches the supplied identifier, the catalog service makes no changes and notifies the caller that the book could not be found.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Permanent removal vs. soft archival:** Deleting a book is a permanent catalog change rather than a reversible hide or archive step. Teams should verify that a title is truly meant to be erased before invoking this capability.
- **Handling already-missing titles:** When a requested book is not found, the service reports it as missing rather than treating the request as a completed deletion. Downstream workflows should only treat a missing-book outcome as equivalent to deletion when that behavior is explicitly safe for their business process.

## 5. Technical Reference

<NodeGraph />

### Endpoint & Contract

- **Operation:** `DELETE /api/v1/books/{id}`
- **Path parameter:** `{id}` — UUID of the book resource to delete.
- **Request body:** None.
- **Success response:** `204 No Content` with an empty body.
- **Missing resource response:** `404` when no book exists with the supplied UUID. A client can safely treat an already-absent resource as deleted only when that is explicitly acceptable for its workflow.
