## 1. Context & Problem

Readers, buyers, and catalog managers often identify editions and filter inventory by publisher. Storing publisher names as free-form text on individual [[entity|book]]s would quickly lead to inconsistent spellings and make publisher-level grouping or updates error-prone.

## 2. Business Value

- **Consistent attribution:** Ensures every book from the same publisher shares one canonical publisher name.
- **Reliable filtering & reporting:** Supports accurate catalog searches, merchandising views, and sales or inventory analysis by publisher.
- **Single-point updates:** Lets catalog teams correct or update a publisher's name in one place without touching individual books.

## 3. How It Works

A Publisher represents a publishing house or imprint that releases [[entity|book]]s in the catalog:

- Each publisher has a unique identity, a required display name, and an associated collection of books it publishes.
- **Validation rules:** Every publisher must have a non-empty name, and the catalog prevents operations against publishers that have been marked as deleted.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Focused entity scope:** Limiting the publisher model to an identity and display name keeps catalog entry lightweight, while leaving room to add contact, address, or imprint-hierarchy fields later if supplier workflows grow.
- **Deletion guard:** Enforcing non-deleted checks ensures books are never linked to retired publisher records.
