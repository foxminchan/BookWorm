## 1. Context & Problem

The book is the central product in BookWorm—every customer journey from catalog browsing and pricing to reviews, shopping baskets, and order fulfillment revolves around it. Without a single authoritative Book record governing core product attributes, classifications, pricing, and lifecycle rules, product data would fragment across services and become difficult to keep consistent.

## 2. Business Value

- **Unified product truth:** Gives merchandising, storefront display, and commerce workflows one consistent source of book details and pricing.
- **Promotional flexibility:** Supports both regular (original) pricing and discounted sale pricing directly on the product.
- **Protected edits:** Prevents simultaneous updates from silently overwriting each other.
- **Auditability & safe retirement:** Retains creation and update history and removes retired books softly so past orders and customer reviews remain intact.

## 3. How It Works

A Book captures all core merchandising, classification, and summary information for a title in the catalog:

- **Product details & pricing:** Stores a unique identity, required title, optional description and cover image, current availability status, and pricing covering both the original price and any active sale price.
- **Catalog classification:** Each book is assigned to one [[entity|category]] and one [[entity|publisher]], and can be credited to multiple [[entity|author]]s.
- **Customer sentiment summary:** Holds a derived average rating and total review count based on [[entity|feedback]] records so storefront listings can display ratings immediately.
- **Commerce connections:** Books can be placed into multiple customer [[entity|basket]]s and included in orders managed by the [[domain|ordering]] domain.
- **Lifecycle & concurrency safety:** Books track when they were created and last modified, use a deletion marker rather than permanent removal, and carry a version stamp so concurrent updates are detected safely before saving.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Soft deletion vs. hard deletion:** Marking retired books as deleted preserves historical links for past orders and reviews, at the cost of ensuring all active storefront and search views filter out deleted titles.
- **Derived rating summary:** Storing the average rating and review count directly on the book makes catalog browsing fast and independent of the Reviews domain, but introduces eventual consistency when feedback changes asynchronously.
- **Single category and publisher:** Requiring exactly one category and one publisher simplifies catalog governance and filtering, though multi-category tagging would require expanding the relationship model later.
- **Concurrency protection:** Checking a version stamp on updates prevents silent lost writes during concurrent editorial or rating updates, though conflicting updates must be retried.
