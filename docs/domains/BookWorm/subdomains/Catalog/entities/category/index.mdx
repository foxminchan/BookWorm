## 1. Context & Problem

As a bookstore catalog grows, shoppers need a clear taxonomy of genres and subjects to browse and filter titles. If category labels were entered as unvalidated text on each [[entity|book]], typos and duplicate labels would fracture storefront navigation and reporting.

## 2. Business Value

- **Better discoverability:** Gives customers clean, predictable subject groupings for browsing and filtering the catalog.
- **Centralized taxonomy management:** Allows merchandising teams to rename a category once and have the change apply across all grouped titles.
- **Catalog hygiene:** Prevents blank or retired categories from polluting active storefront menus.

## 3. How It Works

A Category represents a distinct classification used to group [[entity|book]]s in the catalog:

- Each category has a unique identity, a required display name, and an associated collection of books categorized under it.
- **Validation rules:** The catalog requires every category to have a non-empty name and enforces that operations cannot proceed against a category marked as deleted.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Flat vs. hierarchical taxonomy:** Modeling Category as a simple, single-level grouping keeps navigation and maintenance straightforward, though supporting nested subcategories (parent/child genres) or assigning a book to multiple categories would require extending the model later.
- **Deletion guard:** Blocking use of deleted categories protects catalog integrity so active books cannot remain tied to retired classifications.
