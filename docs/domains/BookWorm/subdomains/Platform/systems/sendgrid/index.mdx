## 1. Context & Problem

BookWorm needs to send transactional emails—such as checkout and order notifications—reliably without building or operating its own internet mail-routing infrastructure. At the same time, BookWorm cannot allow an external email vendor to dictate internal business state, lose pending notifications during provider downtime, or expose sensitive customer communications across unrelated services.

SendGrid serves as BookWorm's external email delivery boundary, isolating third-party delivery mechanics from BookWorm's internal business workflows.

## 2. Business Value

- **Reliable customer communication**: Offloads complex email routing, reputation management, and downstream mail-provider delivery to a dedicated external platform.
- **Resilience to provider disruptions**: Keeping notification work queued inside BookWorm prevents temporary email-provider outages from dropping customer messages or blocking core workflows like checkout.
- **Clear accountability**: Splitting provider delivery responsibilities from BookWorm business ownership ensures operations, support, and product teams share a clear view of what "submitted" versus "completed" means.
- **Protected customer data**: Concentrates email credentials and sensitive message content within a tightly controlled outbound integration boundary.

## 3. How It Works

The [[system|notification-system]] prepares notification messages and submits them through [[service|sendgrid-api]], while SendGrid handles external validation, routing, rate limiting, and delivery processing.

<ContextDiagram />

SendGrid never owns BookWorm's notification state or business outcomes. The [[system|notification-system]] retains full ownership of the [[entity|outbox]] record, retry decisions, and the distinction between a pending, failed, or successfully submitted email.

| Concern             | SendGrid responsibility                                      | BookWorm responsibility                                |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Message submission  | Accept email requests through the SendGrid API               | Build the request from an outbox item                  |
| Provider validation | Validate provider-specific request details                   | Validate required application fields before submission |
| Delivery processing | Route and process the message with downstream mail providers | Track the provider response against the outbox item    |
| Failures and limits | Return errors for invalid requests, outages, or rate limits  | Preserve pending work and decide when to retry         |
| Business meaning    | Deliver the supplied message                                 | Own the business event and notification content        |

A provider response indicates only whether SendGrid accepted or rejected a submission—not whether the underlying business operation succeeded or the message reached the recipient's inbox.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Bounded reliability over naive retries**: Pending outbox records must survive provider outages, temporary failures should be retried in a controlled manner, rate limits must be observable without infinite retry loops, and permanent rejections must be captured with enough non-sensitive context for diagnosis.
- **Exactly-once expectation management**: A successful submission updates BookWorm's internal state once, yet distributed communication means rare duplicates or ambiguous outcomes must still be accounted for operationally.
- **Strict data minimization**: Credentials must stay out of code, documentation, and logs, while email addresses and message bodies are treated as sensitive data accessible only to components required for delivery and authorized support.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Integration & Architecture Details

- **Scope**: External system (`sendgrid`), exposing [[service|sendgrid-api]] (`1.0.0`).
- **Flows**: Participates in `user-checkout` (`1.0.0`).
- **Security**: Outbound authenticated integration using runtime secret configuration; credentials and message content must not appear in catalog documentation, source control, or routine operational logs.
- **Ownership boundary**: [[system|notification-system]] persists [[entity|outbox]] records, applies retry and rate-limit policies, and updates delivery state exactly once per successful provider response from the Notification System's perspective.
