## 1. Context & Problem

BookWorm depends on recurring background maintenance—such as updating product search embeddings and retrying or archiving email notifications—that must run reliably without waiting for customer traffic. Scattering independent timers across every business service would fragment operational visibility, risk duplicate runs across scaled instances, and tangle scheduling rules with domain logic. The Scheduler System provides a dedicated platform coordination boundary that manages when recurring work runs while leaving business rules, authorization, and data ownership with each receiving system.

## 2. Business Value

- **Centralized visibility and control**: Administrators gain a single place to inspect recurring schedules, pause jobs during maintenance, and investigate failures.
- **Reliable background operations**: Durable schedules ensure catalog search updates and notification maintenance resume automatically after restarts rather than being silently skipped.
- **Stronger service boundaries**: Keeping scheduling separate from business data allows Catalog and Notification teams to change their internal rules and data models without breaking the scheduler.
- **Protected credentials and data**: Separating authentication secrets and business payloads from schedule records reduces security risk during daily operations.

## 3. How It Works

1. **Schedule evaluation**: The system persists job definitions and timing rules, evaluating calendars, pause states, and missed-run policies to decide when work is eligible to start.
2. **Authenticated dispatch**: When a run is due, the active scheduler instance records the firing, retrieves service-to-service credentials from secure runtime configuration, and invokes the target system through its supported contract.
3. **Downstream execution**: The receiving system validates the caller, applies its own concurrency locks, and updates its own data:
   - **Catalog search maintenance**: [[service|catalog-ingestion]] processes a bounded batch of pending [[entity|book]] updates in [[container|catalog-vector]] and confirms only items that have not changed concurrently.
   - **Notification maintenance**: [[system|notification-system]] evaluates its durable [[entity|outbox]] to make failed deliveries eligible for retry, clean up expired records, or emit a health signal.
4. **Outcome recording**: The scheduler records completion, duration, or failure details so operators can monitor execution health and diagnose issues.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Dispatch success vs. business completion**: A completed run in the scheduler only confirms that the target service was called—operators must check Catalog's pending updates or Notification's outbox and provider health to verify business completion.
- **Targets must tolerate duplicate runs**: Because timeouts or restarts can leave uncertainty about whether a call finished, receiving services must be idempotent or backed by durable work queues so repeated triggers are harmless.
- **No direct domain table access**: Prohibiting the scheduler from reading or writing Catalog or Notification tables keeps boundaries clean, though diagnosing a backlog requires correlating scheduler outcomes with target-side metrics.
- **Visibility without secret exposure**: Retaining correlation identifiers and sanitized error categories allows operators to trace issues end to end without copying credentials, notification text, or personal data into schedule metadata or routine logs.

### Responsibilities and boundaries

| Responsibility         | Scheduler owns                                                                 | Target system owns                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Schedule management    | Job definitions, trigger cadence, calendars, pause state, and misfire handling | The meaning of the work being scheduled                            |
| Invocation             | Starting a job and sending the authenticated request                           | Endpoint authorization and request validation                      |
| Execution coordination | Instance check-ins, fired-trigger records, locks, and job status               | Processing semantics and domain-level concurrency                  |
| Failure handling       | Recording execution failures and making eligible work runnable again           | Retrying or preserving the business work requested by the job      |
| Observability          | Scheduler health, trigger state, and execution history                         | Processing metrics, data quality, and downstream business outcomes |

The Scheduler must not read or write Catalog, Notification, or other domain tables directly; it invokes versioned commands or HTTP contracts instead.

### Scheduled work and contracts

- **Catalog vector maintenance**: Runs `ProcessVectorBatchJob` every five minutes UTC and publishes [[event|ProcessVectorBatchIntegrationEvent]] to `scheduler.process-vector-batch` via Wolverine's PostgreSQL outbox. [[service|catalog-ingestion]] consumes that event via `[KafkaTrigger]`, acquires a PostgreSQL advisory lock on `catalogdb`, updates [[container|catalog-vector]], and acknowledges unchanged revisions.
- **Liveness monitoring**: Runs `HeartbeatJob` every 30 seconds UTC for internal operational liveness telemetry.

### Persistence, reliability, and security

- **Quartz persistence**: Operational state resides in [[container|scheduler-database]] across Quartz.NET tables (`qrtz_job_details`, `qrtz_triggers`, `qrtz_cron_triggers`, `qrtz_simple_triggers`, `qrtz_simprop_triggers`, `qrtz_blob_triggers`, `qrtz_calendars`, `qrtz_paused_trigger_grps`, `qrtz_paused_job_grps`, `qrtz_fired_triggers`, `qrtz_scheduler_state`, `qrtz_locks`, `qrtz_job_status`, `qrtz_execution_history`, and `qrtz_misfire_history`). Consumers must not query Quartz tables as a job API or infer Catalog business state from scheduler rows.
- **Reliability**: Durable trigger state survives restarts; misfires must remain visible and follow configured recovery policies (never silently skipped); non-concurrent jobs and target-side locks prevent overlapping work; failed invocations remain observable with job, trigger, target, and execution context; retries must be bounded and idempotent.
- **Monitoring & secrets**: Operators monitor scheduler-instance check-ins, fired-trigger age, misfire/failure counts, execution duration, and last successful run, comparing them against pending vector updates (Catalog) or outbox state and provider health (Notification). Outbound credentials belong in runtime secret configuration; authorization headers, notification content, and personal data must never appear in Quartz metadata or routine logs.
