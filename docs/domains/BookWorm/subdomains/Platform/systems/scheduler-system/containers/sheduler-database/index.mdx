## Overview

Scheduler Database is the operational system of record for the
[[system|scheduler-system]]. It stores Quartz definitions and runtime state so
scheduled work can survive process restarts, coordinate scheduler instances,
and remain diagnosable after a failed or delayed firing.

It is authoritative for scheduling state, but it is not authoritative for
Catalog, Notification, or any other business data touched by a job. Downstream
systems own the work requested by the Scheduler and must expose their own
durable queues, outboxes, or processing state.

## What it stores

- **Job definitions** — registered job types, groups, durability, and job data.
- **Trigger definitions** — cron, simple, and blob trigger schedules,
  calendars, pause state, and misfire configuration.
- **Runtime coordination** — fired triggers, scheduler-instance check-ins,
  locks, and job status.
- **Execution diagnostics** — execution history and misfire history used to
  investigate duration, completion, failure, and recovery behavior.

The catalogued Quartz entities describe the principal tables and their
relationships:

- [[entity|qrtz-job-details]] and [[entity|qrtz-triggers]] define what can run
  and when it is eligible.
- [[entity|qrtz-cron-triggers]], [[entity|qrtz-simple-triggers]], and
  [[entity|qrtz-blob-triggers]] store trigger-specific scheduling data.
- [[entity|qrtz-calendars]] and paused group entities constrain or suspend
  eligible schedules.
- [[entity|qrtz-fired-triggers]] and [[entity|qrtz-scheduler-state]] represent
  active firings and scheduler-instance liveness.
- [[entity|qrtz-locks]], [[entity|qrtz-job-status]],
  [[entity|qrtz-execution-history]], and [[entity|qrtz-misfire-history]] support
  coordination and operations.

## Access patterns

[[service|sheduler-dashboard]] is the Scheduler Database's application owner.
The Scheduler service uses Quartz's persistent store to read and write job,
trigger, coordination, and execution records. Its dashboard and HTTP API
present selected operational state to authorized operators.

No Catalog or Notification service should query this database directly.
Scheduler jobs invoke versioned service contracts or publish integration work;
the receiving system owns authorization, processing, retries, and business
outcomes.

## Retention and operational behavior

- Quartz scheduling state is durable and must remain available across Scheduler
  restarts.
- Execution history is enabled for the Scheduler and retained for 30 days,
  with a maximum of 100,000 entries per scheduler.
- Misfire records and failed executions must remain observable long enough to
  diagnose missed schedules and downstream failures.
- A successful row in execution history means the Scheduler completed its
  invocation path. It does not confirm that Catalog indexing or Notification
  delivery completed successfully.
- Direct edits to Quartz tables are unsafe. Use the dashboard or the
  Scheduler's supported management surface for schedule changes.

## Reliability and security

The database is provisioned as the `schedulerdb` PostgreSQL resource and the
Scheduler waits for it before starting. Persistent state allows the Scheduler
to recover trigger definitions and continue eligible work after a restart.

Access is restricted to the Scheduler service and authorized operators.
Connection strings, credentials, authorization headers, notification content,
and personal data must not be stored in job data or copied into routine logs.
Operational queries should use job, trigger, and fire-instance identifiers to
keep diagnosis precise and avoid exposing unrelated scheduler data.
