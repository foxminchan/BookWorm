## Context

A process restart, database interruption, timeout, or downstream failure can
leave the [[system|scheduler-system]] uncertain whether a target operation completed. Silently
discarding a missed trigger loses work, while replaying every missed interval
or retrying indefinitely can overload a target and create duplicate effects.
Each job therefore needs a policy matched to its target contract: a recurring
maintenance trigger may run once after a misfire, whereas replaying every
missed interval could create unnecessary load without recovering a distinct
business event.

## Decision

Every durable job must define an explicit misfire policy and retain observable
firing and execution history. Jobs use the configured recovery behavior,
including `FireOnceNow` where appropriate, rather than silently skipping
eligible work or replaying an unbounded backlog.

Jobs that invoke external services or publish integration work must be safe to
run more than once. The Scheduler prevents overlapping executions where the
job requires it, while the receiving system provides idempotency, durable
queues, or equivalent duplicate protection. Retries remain bounded and
observable, and a successful Scheduler invocation is never treated as proof of
downstream business completion.

The current recurring jobs use `FireOnceNow` for misfires and disallow
concurrent executions. The Catalog batch is also protected by its own
database advisory lock and revision-aware queue acknowledgement. Notification
maintenance handles at most 100 records per request, but that batch limit is
not a per-email lifetime retry limit; unsent records remain eligible on later
hourly runs.

## Consequences

- Missed schedules and recovery attempts remain visible to operators.
- A restart can make eligible work runnable without replaying every missed
  interval.
- Target systems must design contracts and state transitions for repeated
  invocation.
- Operators need both Scheduler execution history and downstream business
  metrics to distinguish a retry from duplicate business work.
- Job-specific retry and misfire policies must be documented and tested.
- `FireOnceNow` makes one execution eligible after a misfire; it does not
  replay each missed interval or guarantee immediate downstream completion.
- Non-overlapping Scheduler executions prevent two firings of the same job
  from running concurrently in that scheduler configuration, but do not
  replace target-side locks, idempotency, or duplicate-safe processing.
- A bounded target batch limits work per invocation, not backlog size or
  lifetime retries. Monitoring must distinguish invocation frequency from
  target queue age, backlog, and per-item outcomes.
- Retry limits and idempotency belong to the relevant job and target contract;
  they cannot be inferred from the fact that Quartz persists execution
  history.

## Alternatives considered

### Replay every missed interval

This could preserve the nominal number of executions but may create a burst
of redundant work after downtime. The current jobs use one immediate eligible
execution after a misfire rather than replaying an unbounded interval backlog.

### Skip all misfired executions

This would avoid catch-up load but silently lose scheduled opportunities for
durable maintenance. A visible one-time recovery execution is preferred for
these jobs.

### Rely only on Scheduler-level non-concurrency

Scheduler non-concurrency does not cover manual calls, other consumers, or
target-side retries. Target systems must still make work durable and handle
duplicate invocations safely.

### Treat every bounded batch as a bounded retry policy

A 100-record batch limits one Notification invocation, but leaves unsent rows
eligible in later runs. Batch size is not a per-record attempt count or
terminal-failure rule.
