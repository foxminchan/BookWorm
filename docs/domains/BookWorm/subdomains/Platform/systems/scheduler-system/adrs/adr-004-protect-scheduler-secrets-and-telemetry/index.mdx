## Context

[[system|scheduler-system]] jobs call internal services with service-to-service credentials and
may handle notification content or other sensitive request data. Quartz job
metadata and routine logs are durable, widely queried operational surfaces, so
placing secrets or full payloads there would increase the impact of a
credential leak or data disclosure. Operational investigation should still be
possible using firing, job, trigger, and target correlation identifiers without
recording the credentials or personal data used by the target.

## Decision

Outbound credentials are supplied through runtime secret configuration and are
never embedded in job definitions, committed to the catalog, or written to
routine logs. Quartz job data and execution history contain only the minimum
non-sensitive identifiers and operational metadata needed to execute and
diagnose a job.

Logs and telemetry should retain correlation identifiers, target identifiers,
status, duration, and sanitized failure categories instead of authorization
headers, credentials, notification contents, or personal data. Any controlled
diagnostic capture of sensitive values requires restricted access and the
platform retention policy.

Target-specific authentication material belongs in runtime secret
configuration. Job data should contain only the minimum non-sensitive
identifiers required to make the authenticated call; for example, Notification
maintenance requests carry request metadata and let Notification choose its
own outbox records.

## Consequences

- Credentials can be rotated through deployment configuration without changing
  documented resources or schedules.
- Operators can trace a firing to a target without routinely exposing secrets
  or message content.
- Detailed support investigations may require authorized access to the
  downstream system's records.
- Secret scanning, access control, and retention monitoring remain required
  operational controls.
- Quartz job definitions and execution history are durable and operationally
  useful, so minimizing their payloads reduces the impact and spread of
  accidental disclosure.
- Downstream services remain the appropriate place to inspect recipient,
  business, or item-level detail under their own authorization controls.
- Correlation identifiers and sanitized errors must be sufficient to connect
  scheduler execution history with target-side processing without relying on
  copied secrets or full request bodies.
- Runtime secret storage does not remove the need to restrict access to
  Quartz administration, logs, traces, and diagnostic retention.

## Alternatives considered

### Store access tokens or passwords in Quartz job data

Quartz state is durable and available to operators and scheduler instances.
Persisting credentials with schedules would expand access and leave secrets
behind when rotation is needed. Jobs obtain credentials from runtime
configuration instead.

### Log full requests and downstream responses

Full payloads can simplify debugging but may contain recipient details,
notification content, or other personal data. Routine telemetry uses
correlation data and sanitized failure categories, with restricted downstream
inspection for cases that require detail.

### Omit all operational context from telemetry

Removing every identifier would reduce exposure but make it difficult to
correlate a firing with its target-side processing. The decision retains
minimum non-sensitive identifiers and status information.
