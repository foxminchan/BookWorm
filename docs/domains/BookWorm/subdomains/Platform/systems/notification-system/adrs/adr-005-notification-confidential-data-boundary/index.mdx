## Context

Email outbox rows contain recipient names, addresses, subjects, and rendered
order content. SMTP and PostgreSQL connections also require privileged runtime
configuration. Copying these values into logs, scheduler metadata, or
documentation expands access beyond what delivery requires.

## Decision

Treat [[container|notification-database]] as confidential. Limit recipient and
message-content access to [[service|notification-api]] and authorized operators
with a delivery or support need. Calling domains remain authoritative for
business state; the outbox is not a general-purpose order-data export.

Keep SMTP authentication and database connection secrets in runtime secret
configuration, never in catalog content, source control, outbox rows, or routine
logs. Scheduler maintenance messages carry only request metadata and do not
need recipient details or message bodies.

Prefer outbox identifiers, sequence numbers, timestamps, aggregate backlog
counts, and sanitized error categories for diagnostics. Provider acceptance,
cleanup, and email-state changes must not be presented as confirmation of the
underlying business operation.

## Access and observability implications

The Notification service needs recipient and rendered content to submit a
message, but routine operational checks usually need only row identifiers,
counts, age, status, and sanitized failure information. Database access,
support tooling, and logs should follow that distinction. Scheduler requests
need only maintenance metadata and must not become a transport for email
content.

The intended sanitized logging policy does not describe the complete current
implementation: resend logs include recipient addresses, and SMTP failure
logs include recipients and subjects. Those paths require review and
remediation; this ADR does not claim that existing logs already meet the
policy.

## Consequences

- Delivery and authorized support still require access to confidential email
  data, so database permissions and operational log access need explicit control.
- Routine backlog inspection can avoid selecting recipient information or
  rendered content.
- Current resend logs include recipient addresses, and SMTP failure logs include
  recipients and subjects. Sanitized logging is an accepted requirement, not
  a claim that those existing log statements already satisfy it.
- Cleanup currently deletes sent records without an age threshold; unsent records
  have no age-based expiry. Neither behavior establishes a formal privacy
  retention period or delivery audit policy.
- Any future retention or logging change must preserve recovery needs while
  minimizing unnecessary exposure of email data.
- Email outbox data remains confidential after SMTP submission until cleanup
  removes the row. The current cleanup cadence may leave sent content available
  until the next run, but does not define a retention period.
- Aggregated queue metrics and sanitized failure categories are preferred for
  monitoring; operational queries should avoid selecting recipient or body
  columns unless the support task requires them.
- Logging, database, and secret-management controls are separate safeguards:
  placing credentials in runtime configuration does not by itself restrict
  access to recipient data or rendered content.

## Alternatives considered

### Include recipient and message content in scheduler requests

Scheduler only decides when maintenance should run. Passing email content
through its messages would duplicate confidential data and couple scheduling
to the Notification delivery model.

### Log complete messages for easier debugging

Full bodies and recipient details may simplify local diagnosis but increase
exposure in routine logs and monitoring systems. Prefer identifiers and
sanitized failure details, with narrowly authorized support access to the
outbox when necessary.

### Delete sent records immediately after submission

Immediate deletion would reduce the time content remains in the outbox, but
would remove the existing bounded cleanup and operational inspection window.
Any revised retention behavior needs an explicit policy that balances privacy,
support, and audit requirements; the current hourly cleanup is not such a
policy.
