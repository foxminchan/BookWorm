## Context

Unsent emails need another delivery opportunity, and sent records need cleanup.
Putting email-table queries in Quartz jobs would couple Scheduler to
Notification's schema and confuse scheduler execution with completed email work.

## Decision

[[system|scheduler-system]] owns timing and publishes
[[event|ResendErrorEmailIntegrationEvent]] and
[[event|CleanUpSentEmailIntegrationEvent]] through Kafka. The current jobs fire
hourly in UTC. Requests contain inherited identity and creation time, not
recipient data, row selections, batch sizes, or retention criteria.

[[service|notification-api]] owns selection and processing in
[[container|notification-database]]. Each request handles at most 100 records,
ordered by `sequence_number`:

- Resend selects all unsent records with tracking enabled. Despite the event
  name, eligibility does not require a recorded error. Individual delivery
  failures are logged and the batch continues; successful originals are marked
  sent. Handler-token cancellation, selection failures, and final save failures
  propagate.
- Cleanup selects only sent records, removes the selected batch, and saves.
  Failures are logged and rethrown. Selection has no minimum age, retention
  duration, or audit-hold check.

Scheduler never accesses the Notification tables directly. Completed scheduler
publication is not evidence that Notification processed the batch or delivered
any email. No application-level maintenance result event is published.

## Operational behavior

Each hourly scheduler firing publishes a request; Notification performs one
bounded selection of up to 100 rows for that request. A backlog larger than
one batch therefore requires subsequent scheduled requests to drain. Resend
orders candidates by `sequence_number` and includes all unsent rows, so
repeatedly failing older records can delay newer ones. Cleanup selects sent
rows without a minimum age, retention window, or audit hold.

Resend item failures are logged while the batch continues, so a successfully
handled maintenance request is not equivalent to every selected email being
submitted. Conversely, publication success from Scheduler only confirms the
request left Scheduler; it does not confirm consumer execution. Operators
correlate job history with Notification's outbox state and provider health.

## Consequences

- Scheduler and Notification can evolve their persistence independently.
- Bounded selection limits work per invocation, not lifetime retry counts or
  the total backlog. Unsent records remain eligible regardless of age.
- Persistent failures among the oldest records can delay later work. Resend
  also creates additional outbox rows through the shared sender wrapper.
- Sent records can be deleted at the next cleanup, so the table is not a
  durable delivery audit archive.
- Non-overlapping Quartz jobs do not provide Notification-side row claims or
  exactly-once processing across duplicate requests and consumers.
- Operators must correlate Scheduler firings with unsent count, oldest pending
  age, resend success/failure counts, and SMTP health.
- A single successful hourly invocation cannot guarantee backlog drain or
  provide a delivery-time SLA; each run is bounded and downstream SMTP work
  can fail independently.
- Oldest-first selection without persisted attempt limits can starve newer
  unsent records when an older record repeatedly fails.
- Duplicate maintenance requests and overlapping consumers are not guarded by
  Notification-side row claims or an exactly-once processing mechanism.
- Sent-record cleanup is operational deletion, not retention-policy
  enforcement or a guaranteed delivery audit history.

## Alternatives considered

### Have Scheduler query and delete Notification records directly

This would couple Scheduler to Notification's schema and make the scheduler
responsible for target-system data semantics. Notification remains the owner
of selection and processing; Scheduler only requests maintenance.

### Process the entire backlog in one job

An unbounded run could monopolize resources or make job duration grow with
backlog size. The current batch limit bounds each request, at the cost of
requiring later runs to work through larger backlogs.

### Treat job completion as delivery completion

Scheduler owns invocation timing, not email processing or provider delivery.
The systems expose no application-level maintenance result that would justify
this interpretation.

This complements the existing
[[adr|adr-002-scheduler-invocation-boundary]].
It does not add a terminal-failure policy or lifetime attempt cap.
