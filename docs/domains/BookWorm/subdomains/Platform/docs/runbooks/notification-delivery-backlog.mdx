## 1. Context & Problem

BookWorm uses a durable notification outbox to queue and send customer emails such as order communications. When downstream dependencies slow down, fail, or reject messages—such as the mail delivery provider, the messaging pipeline, or the Notification service itself—unsent email records begin piling up and aging without clearing.

If operators respond hastily—for example, by deleting queued records, manually marking them as sent, or running the wrong maintenance job—customers can permanently lose important emails or receive confusing duplicate messages, while sensitive customer information risks leaking into incident notes. This runbook establishes a safe, repeatable way to diagnose and clear a notification backlog.

## 2. Business Value

- **Protects Customer Experience:** Ensures delayed customer notifications are recovered intact rather than silently dropped during incident response.
- **Avoids Misdiagnosing Order Health:** Clarifies that an email backlog does not mean customer orders failed; business status remains safely tracked in its own owning domain.
- **Prevents Duplicate & Data-Leak Incidents:** Guards against unsafe manual replays and keeps sensitive customer addresses, message bodies, and credentials out of logs and incident tickets.
- **Speeds Up Triage:** Clearly separates Notification application issues from Platform infrastructure issues so the right team engages immediately.

## 3. How It Works

### Recognizing a Backlog

A delivery backlog is typically identified when the number or age of unsent email records keeps climbing, scheduled hourly retry cycles run without shrinking the queue, or delivery/processing errors appear in sanitized monitoring logs.

### Investigation Flow

1. **Measure the Backlog Safely:** Check how many records are unsent and when the oldest pending record was created, without viewing any recipient email addresses or message contents.
2. **Verify the Retry Schedule:** Confirm in the scheduler monitoring view that the hourly retry task is enabled and running on its expected schedule. Remember that a successful scheduler run only confirms the retry signal was sent—not that emails were actually delivered.
3. **Check Message Pipeline Health:** Verify that the messaging broker and Notification consumer are healthy and check whether messages are landing in the dead-letter path, without copying message payloads into incident notes.
4. **Check Mail Provider Health:** Verify connectivity, credentials status (never printing secrets), rate limits, or rejections at the external mail delivery provider.
5. **Check for Intentionally Skipped Messages:** Confirm that the originating Finance request actually included a non-blank email address, because blank addresses are intentionally skipped.

### Safe Recovery & Verification

- **Fix the Root Dependency:** Restore the failing database, messaging pipeline, Notification service, or mail provider. Unsent records stay safely queued and eligible for retry.
- **Let Bounded Batches Drain the Queue:** Wait for the next scheduled hourly retry (or run an authorized manual trigger). Each cycle handles up to 100 unsent records, so a large backlog will clear over multiple cycles.
- **Never Alter Records Manually:** Do not delete unsent records or mark them as sent just to make metrics look clean. Do not replay requests blindly without checking whether an earlier attempt already succeeded.
- **Do Not Run Cleanup:** Sent-email cleanup only removes already-sent records and will not help clear an unsent backlog.
- **Verify & Escalate:** Confirm recovery by watching the unsent count and oldest record age trend downward across retry runs and verifying delivery errors stop. Escalate application/delivery logic issues to the contributor team and scheduler, database, broker, or provider connectivity issues to the platform team.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Gradual Drain (100-Record Cap) vs. Instant Recovery:** Processing at most 100 unsent records per retry run protects the mail provider from rate-limit bans and database spikes during recovery, at the cost of taking multiple hourly cycles to clear a large backlog.
- **Ambiguous Delivery Outcomes & Duplicate Risk:** Because external mail providers can time out after accepting a message, retrying can occasionally create duplicate submissions. Checking prior delivery state before manual intervention minimizes unwanted duplicates.
- **Strict Separation of Order & Email State:** Decoupling the email outbox from order completion ensures email outages never block core business transactions, though operators must verify order completion in the Finance/Order domain rather than assuming it from email status.
- **Operational Privacy Hygiene:** Limiting diagnostic checks to aggregate counts and timestamps prevents exposing customer PII and credentials in incident channels.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Symptoms

- The count or age of unsent records in [[container|notification-database]] continues to increase.
- The `ResendErrorEmailJob` fires, but unsent records do not decrease.
- SMTP submission failures or notification-consumer errors appear in sanitized application telemetry.

### Impact

Email notifications may be delayed or submitted more than once. The outbox does not determine whether an order or other business operation completed; check that state in its owning domain.

### Diagnose

1. Check outbox counts and age without selecting recipient or message content:

   <CodeGroup dropdown>

   ```sql
   SELECT
       is_sent,
       COUNT(*) AS record_count,
       MIN(created_at) AS oldest_created_at
   FROM outboxes
   GROUP BY is_sent;
   ```

   </CodeGroup>

2. In [[service|sheduler-dashboard]], inspect the `ResendErrorEmailJob` trigger, UTC schedule, firing history, and completion status. A successful Scheduler execution confirms that the request was published; it does not confirm that Notification delivered email.
3. Check Kafka and Notification consumer health, including the configured dead-letter path [[channel|bookworm.&#123;env&#125;.notification.dead-letter-queue]]. Use the environment's approved broker and telemetry tools; do not copy message payloads into routine incident notes.
4. Check SMTP provider connectivity, authentication configuration, and rate-limit or rejection errors. Do not print or paste credentials.
5. Confirm that the original Finance command supplied a nonblank email address. Notification intentionally skips blank addresses.

### Recover

- Restore the failing database, Kafka, Notification consumer, or SMTP dependency. Unsent records remain eligible for a later resend request.
- Allow the next scheduled resend, or use the approved operational procedure for an explicitly authorized invocation. Each request processes at most 100 unsent records, so a larger backlog may take multiple runs.
- Do not delete or mark records sent to clear the backlog. Do not replay requests without checking for an earlier submission: SMTP outcomes can be ambiguous, and resend can create additional outbox records.
- Do not run cleanup as a backlog remedy. Cleanup selects sent records only.

### Verify

- Unsent record count and oldest-record age trend down across resend runs.
- SMTP submission failures stop or return to an understood, bounded level.
- Confirm outcomes in Notification state and the owning domain; Scheduler completion alone is not delivery confirmation.

### Escalation

- Contact [[team|contributor-team]] for Notification consumer and delivery behavior.
- Contact [[team|platform-team]] for Scheduler, database, broker, provider connectivity, or infrastructure issues.
- Use the established support or on-call channel for urgent incidents; no specific channel or rotation is documented here.
