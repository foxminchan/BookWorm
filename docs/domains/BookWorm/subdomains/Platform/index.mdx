## 1. Context & Problem

Every business domain in BookWorm—such as [[domain|ordering]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|catalog]], and [[domain|reviews]]—needs reliable background infrastructure, including scheduled maintenance jobs and customer email delivery. If each domain built and maintained its own job timers, retry logic, and external mail integrations, teams would duplicate complex plumbing, create inconsistent reliability guarantees, and risk tying core business data to external delivery outages.

Platform exists as a **Generic Domain** to provide these shared operational capabilities centrally. It coordinates recurring background work and delivers email notifications through an external mail provider while remaining strictly separated from core business ownership of [[entity|book]]s, [[entity|basket]]s, orders, payments, or customer [[entity|feedback]].

## 2. Business Value

- **Faster delivery for product teams:** Core and supporting domains reuse a single, dependable scheduling and notification foundation instead of building custom infrastructure.
- **Predictable recurring maintenance:** Catalog indexing and system health checks run on a steady, observable timetable.
- **Resilient customer communications:** Email requests are recorded safely before external submission so temporary mail provider issues do not silently drop customer messages.
- **Clean domain boundaries:** Operational delivery outcomes and job schedules stay isolated from business truth, preventing notification glitches from corrupting orders or customer records.
- **Pluggable external delivery:** External mail providers sit behind a clear boundary so provider configuration can evolve without rewriting business workflows.

## 3. How It Works

Platform brings together two internal capabilities and one external integration boundary:

1. **Scheduled processing:** A central coordination system triggers recurring background work on a predictable timetable (for example, prompting catalog search-index updates every five minutes). The scheduler only starts the work; the receiving business domain validates and executes the actual task.
2. **Email notification intake and formatting:** When Finance emits an order notification request, the notification capability checks for a valid recipient (skipping blank email addresses), formats the message, and saves a durable pending record.
3. **External email submission:** The notification capability hands the formatted email to a configured external email provider using built-in resilience safeguards. No completion result is sent back to alter the originating business workflow.
4. **Operational monitoring:** Platform operations teams monitor job execution histories, provider health, and dead-letter queues to catch stalled background jobs or delivery failures early.

### Business Invariants

- Mail provider acceptance proves a handoff occurred, not that the customer received or read the email.
- Repeated business commands can lead to duplicate email submissions.
- A scheduled trigger finishing its run is not proof that the downstream business domain finished processing the work.
- Platform delivery outcomes must never change ownership or state for orders, baskets, books, or customer feedback.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Coordination versus execution ownership:** Because the scheduler only fires time-based triggers, target domains must handle duplicate triggers safely (idempotency and concurrency checks) rather than assuming each trigger runs exactly once.
- **At-least-once communication posture:** Persisting email requests before delivery protects against lost messages, but combined with retries and repeated upstream commands, it can produce duplicate emails rather than guaranteeing strictly-once delivery.
- **Strict contract boundaries over shared databases:** Consumers integrate with Platform through explicit versioned contracts rather than reading or writing Platform's internal tables directly, and Platform never reaches into another domain's database.
- **Operational readiness across concerns:**
  - **Reliability & Resilience:** Outbox records protect pending requests during provider outages, though retries may duplicate submissions.
  - **Observability & Consistency:** Job history, delivery states, and provider responses remain traceable, and delivery status is never treated as business completion.
  - **Security & Maintainability:** Scheduler triggers require authentication, notification data and provider credentials are restricted, and new scheduled jobs can reuse the same boundary without shifting domain ownership.

### Owned Systems & External Boundaries

- **[[system|scheduler-system]]:** Orchestrates recurring work and publishes [[event|ProcessVectorBatchIntegrationEvent]] for [[domain|catalog]] every five minutes (UTC) over Kafka. Target systems remain responsible for idempotency, concurrency controls, and processing.
- **[[system|notification-system]]:** Consumes order commands, skips blank email addresses, renders email content, persists outbox work, and submits MIME messages over SMTP using MailKit with a resilience pipeline. Does not call SendGrid's HTTP Mail Send API.
- **[[system|sendgrid]]:** Documents an external email-provider boundary rather than a BookWorm system of record. The active submission path uses a configured SMTP provider, accounting for provider availability, rate limits, and response errors.

### Interaction Surface
