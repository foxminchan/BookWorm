## 1. Context & Problem

When customers place or update orders in BookWorm, they expect timely email confirmation. However, sending an email depends on external mail providers that can be slow, temporarily unavailable, or reject individual requests. If core business workflows—such as order placement or financial processing—waited directly on email delivery, a mail outage could block customer purchases or corrupt the status of an order.

This flow establishes a clear operational boundary for BookWorm's email notifications. Business workflows provide the intent and customer details for a message, while a dedicated Notification capability owns formatting, durable tracking, delivery attempts, retries, and cleanup. Email delivery remains observable and dependable without ever being confused with whether an order or purchase actually succeeded.

## 2. Business Value

- **Protects revenue-critical workflows:** Order and financial operations complete regardless of external email provider slowdowns or outages.
- **Prevents lost customer communications:** Outgoing email requests are saved safely before external delivery is attempted, surviving service restarts or transient provider errors.
- **Keeps business truth clean:** Delivery failures, retries, or record cleanup never alter or roll back the underlying order, feedback, or workflow outcome.
- **Improves operational visibility:** Support and operations teams can see which messages were requested, which were accepted by the mail provider, and which still need another delivery attempt or cleanup.
- **Controls storage growth:** Completed notification records are periodically cleaned up so operational tables do not grow indefinitely.

## 3. How It Works

1. **Business intent is raised:** When an order event occurs, the Finance workflow sends a notification request containing the recipient and order details.
2. **Request validation and formatting:** The Notification capability verifies that a recipient address is present and formats the email content using a standard order template.
3. **Durable saving before sending:** Before contacting the external mail provider, the Notification capability saves a durable outgoing record marked as pending.
4. **Submission to the mail provider:** The formatted email is submitted to the configured external mail provider using built-in retry safeguards.
5. **Outcome recording:**
   - If the provider accepts the message, the pending record is updated to indicate that submission succeeded.
   - If the provider rejects or fails to accept the message after initial retries, the message is routed to a failure queue for operator attention, and the pending record remains eligible for scheduled follow-up.
6. **Scheduled retry and cleanup:** Periodically, the scheduling capability triggers maintenance runs so pending messages can be retried under bounded rules and previously sent records can be cleaned up—without altering the original business event.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Provider acceptance is not inbox delivery:** A successful handoff means the external mail provider accepted the message for sending; it does not confirm that the email reached the customer's inbox or was opened.
- **Business state and communication state stay strictly separated:** Customers' orders remain valid even if their confirmation email is delayed or fails. Conversely, sending an email never implies an order succeeded.
- **Resilience favors eventual delivery over strict single-send guarantees:** Because external providers do not share transactions with BookWorm's internal storage, rare network or disconnection failures after handoff can result in a message being retried and delivered more than once.
- **Scheduler triggers work without owning data:** The scheduler initiates retry and cleanup cycles on a timetable, while Notification retains full ownership of which messages are eligible and what happens to them.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Participants & Responsibilities

| Participant                          | Responsibility                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Finance workflow                     | Issues the business intent to send an email for an order or status update.                           |
| [[service\|notification-api]]        | Persists the outgoing message in the outbox, validates the request, and tries SMTP delivery.         |
| [[container\|notification-database]] | Stores durable email state and the retry/cleanup lifecycle of pending and sent items.                |
| SMTP provider                        | Accepts or rejects the actual email submission attempt.                                              |
| [[service\|sheduler-dashboard]]      | Triggers resend and cleanup jobs to maintain the notification outbox without owning business intent. |

### Implementation Notes

- **Trigger Command:** `PlaceOrderCommand` (`1.0.0`) emitted by the Finance workflow.
- **Template & Format:** `notification-api` validates the recipient, renders the `Orders/OrderEmail` MJML template, and constructs a MIME message.
- **Transport & Resilience:** Delivery runs over SMTP via MailKit with a MailKit resilience pipeline; failures trigger Wolverine retries before routing to a dead-letter queue.
- **Persistence Pattern:** Uses the outbox pattern in `notification-database` to persist outgoing email records before calling SMTP, keeping pending items recoverable across restarts and eligible for bounded scheduled resend and cleanup batches.
