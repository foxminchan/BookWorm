## Purpose and scope

This flow documents the operational platform boundary for BookWorm's email
notifications. Finance and the domains that initiate business actions provide the
intent and content; the Notification System owns the durable outbox, delivery
attempt, and maintenance policy.

Scheduler is the trigger mechanism, not the owner of notification business
state. The important outcome is that delivery state remains explicit and
observable without confusing email submission with order or purchase success.

## Business context

BookWorm treats notifications as a platform capability rather than a business
state domain. The business systems say what should be sent, while the
Notification System owns the durable mechanics of rendering, retrying, and
cleaning up those sends. This makes the platform reliable without causing a
notification failure to mutate the source of truth for the original order,
feedback, or workflow event.

The result is an operational model that remains observable: the system records
what was requested, whether it was accepted by the SMTP provider, and whether a
later resend or cleanup cycle is still needed. That explicit state is crucial for
reliability, because email delivery is naturally uncertain even when upstream
business events already succeeded.

## Participants and responsibilities

| Participant                         | Responsibility                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Finance workflow                    | Issues the business intent to send an email for an order or status update.                           |
| [[service\|notification-api]]       | Persists the outgoing message in the outbox, validates the request, and tries SMTP delivery.         |
| [[database\|notification-database]] | Stores durable email state and the retry/cleanup lifecycle of pending and sent items.                |
| SMTP provider                       | Accepts or rejects the actual email submission attempt.                                              |
| [[service\|sheduler-dashboard]]     | Triggers resend and cleanup jobs to maintain the notification outbox without owning business intent. |

## Reliability model

This flow uses the outbox pattern to separate business intent from delivery
execution. The Notification API writes a durable record before SMTP is called,
meaning the notification request can survive restarts and service retries. The
outbox item remains marked as pending while delivery is in progress or has not
yet succeeded.

When the SMTP provider accepts the email, the system records that the send was
attempted successfully. When it rejects or defers the email, the item remains in
pending state so Scheduler can re-run the send under bounded retry rules. Sent
records are later eligible for cleanup, which prevents the outbox from growing
without bound.

## Operational safety rules

- The source business workflow owns the message's meaning and intent.
- The Notification System owns the operational mechanics of delivery.
- Delivery success is not the same as business success; a message may be
  accepted by the provider while the underlying order or event remains unchanged.
- Retrying or cleaning up notification records never rewrites the original
  business outcome associated with the message.

## Why this flow matters

This is the platform layer that keeps BookWorm's downstream communication
reliable without contaminating the transactional domains with message-delivery
state. It turns a potentially fragile external dependency into a controllable,
observable system with explicit retry and cleanup behavior.
