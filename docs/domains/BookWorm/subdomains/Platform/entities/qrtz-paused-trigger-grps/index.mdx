## 1. Context & Problem

A single background job may have multiple schedules, or related schedules across different jobs may be grouped together by cadence, integration, or operational domain. Operations needs a way to temporarily suspend an entire group of schedules without altering underlying job definitions or losing visibility into why the schedules were halted.

## 2. Business Value

- **Targeted Schedule Control:** Enables teams to freeze a related set of schedules in one step while leaving other trigger groups and job definitions untouched.
- **Clear Audit Trail:** Records the pause reason, who initiated the pause, and when it occurred, improving traceability across team handoffs.
- **Cluster-Wide Consistency:** Ensures every active scheduler node honors the same paused-group state through shared persistence.

## 3. How It Works

Triggers are organized into named trigger groups within a scheduler instance. When a trigger group is paused, a record is created for that scheduler and trigger group, along with optional audit details indicating why it was paused, who paused it, and when.

As long as the pause record exists, the scheduler skips firing any triggers belonging to that group. Once the group is resumed, the record is cleared and eligible triggers resume according to their configured schedules and missed-run rules.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Trigger Group vs. Job Group Scope:** Pausing a trigger group suspends schedules in that group rather than every trigger on a job; teams must align trigger group naming with operational boundaries.
- **Catch-Up Burst Risk on Resume:** Depending on how missed runs are configured on each trigger, resuming a group after a prolonged pause can trigger catch-up executions if misfire policies are not tuned carefully.
- **Audit Completeness Depends on Callers:** Since audit fields are optional, operational tooling should supply actor and reason details whenever pausing schedules.
