## 1. Context & Problem

Even well-configured schedules occasionally miss their intended execution window—for example, during service restarts, maintenance pauses, or periods when all worker threads are busy. When a trigger misses its planned firing time, teams need a clear record of what was missed and why, rather than discovering silent gaps in business processing later.

## 2. Business Value

- **Auditability for missed schedules**: Gives operations and business teams visibility into when scheduled triggers failed to fire on time.
- **Root-cause clarity**: Pairs the planned schedule time, misfire detection time, and reason code to distinguish capacity bottlenecks from downtime or scheduling pauses.
- **Better capacity and priority tuning**: Repeated misfire entries highlight triggers that need higher priority, wider misfire tolerances, or additional worker capacity.

## 3. How It Works

When the scheduler detects that a trigger's planned fire time has passed beyond the allowed threshold without executing, it can optionally record a misfire history entry.

- **Affected schedule context**: Each entry identifies the scheduler, the worker instance that observed the misfire, the trigger that missed its window, and—when applicable—the associated job definition.
- **Timing comparison**: It captures both the original scheduled time and the time the misfire was detected so teams can see how far behind schedule the trigger fell.
- **Cause classification**: An optional reason indicator records why the misfire occurred, creating a dedicated audit trail separate from completed job runs.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Storage growth during outages**: If many high-frequency triggers misfire during prolonged downtime, misfire records can accumulate rapidly; teams enabling this optional table should define retention rules.
- **Records the miss, not the recovery action**: Misfire history documents that a schedule window was missed and why, while any resulting catch-up execution or skip behavior depends on the trigger's configured misfire policy.
- **Code translation needed for reporting**: Storing the misfire reason as a compact numeric code keeps records small, but operational dashboards or runbooks should translate those codes into clear labels for non-technical readers.
