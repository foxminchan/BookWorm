## 1. Context & Problem

Individual customer [[entity|feedback]] carries its own lifecycle rules, reviewer details, a rating, and an optional comment. [[domain|catalog]] needs rating summaries to support product discovery, but storing or querying individual feedback records inside Catalog would shift [[domain|reviews]] ownership into the wrong domain and tightly couple the two areas.

To keep creation, browsing, and deletion consistent, BookWorm needs a single authoritative home for feedback identity, reviewer constraints, and review content—preventing duplicate copies from drifting out of sync across domains.

## 2. Business Value

- **Single source of truth:** One authoritative store ensures customer reviews are never fragmented across multiple domains.
- **Protected domain boundaries:** Other parts of the platform cannot bypass validation, authorization, or business rules by reaching directly into Reviews data.
- **Transactional reliability:** Creating, listing, and removing feedback operate against consistent, integrity-checked records.
- **Privacy isolation:** Review text and reviewer names stay inside Reviews rather than being copied unnecessarily into Catalog's product discovery store.

## 3. How It Works

[[container|rating-database]] serves as the sole authoritative store for all individual [[entity|feedback]] records, and only the Rating service ([[service|rating-api]]) is permitted to write to or manage them.

- When a customer submits feedback, the service checks that the rating is between 0 and 5 and saves the rating, book reference, reviewer information, and optional comment in one place.
- The store enforces uniqueness for each combination of book and reviewer name so duplicate submissions with the same details are prevented.
- Other domains interact only through published integration notices or supported service queries—never by reading the Rating store directly.
- Catalog receives only the identifiers and rating needed to maintain its own derived summary, without receiving review comments or reviewer details.
- Both AI-generated summaries and Catalog's aggregate rating view are treated as separate, rebuildable derived views rather than replacements for the authoritative feedback store.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Availability dependency:** Both the Rating service and [[agent|rating-agent]] depend on the database being available for feedback writes, listings, and summarization reads.
- **Eventual consistency for consumers:** Because external domains cannot query the database directly, cross-domain views such as Catalog's rating summary must tolerate brief delays between a review change and its reflection elsewhere.
- **API validation remains essential:** Database uniqueness and integrity rules complement, but do not replace, service-level validation of the 0-5 rating range and request rules.
- **Name uniqueness is not identity verification:** Preventing duplicate entries by book and reviewer name does not constitute verified customer identity; downstream callers should not treat it as proof of identity.
- **No cross-database foreign keys:** A feedback entry can reference a book that Catalog later reports as missing. That mismatch is resolved via an asynchronous compensation flow rather than a direct cross-domain database constraint.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Store individual feedback in Catalog:** Rejected because it would move Reviews lifecycle and customer-content ownership into the Catalog domain.
- **Let consumers read the Rating Database directly:** Rejected because direct access couples downstream services and agents to internal storage structures and bypasses validation, authorization, and integration contracts.
- **Use Catalog's rating projection as the review source:** Rejected because the projection holds only aggregate numbers, not individual review records or their lifecycle.

## 5. Technical Reference

- **Authoritative store:** [[container|rating-database]] (PostgreSQL) for the [[entity|feedback]] aggregate.
- **Exclusive writer:** [[service|rating-api]].
- **Validation & constraints:**
  - `rating-api` validates ratings in the inclusive `0`-`5` range at the API boundary before persistence.
  - PostgreSQL enforces a uniqueness constraint on the book and reviewer name combination alongside relational integrity for feedback IDs, book IDs, reviewer details, and optional comments.
- **Downstream contract boundary:** Projection events emitted to [[domain|catalog]] carry only identifiers and the rating value—omitting comment text and reviewer details.
- **Dependencies:** Feedback listing, `rating-api` writes, and [[agent|rating-agent]] reads rely on PostgreSQL availability; missing-book references are handled asynchronously via the Catalog rejection compensation flow rather than cross-database foreign keys.
