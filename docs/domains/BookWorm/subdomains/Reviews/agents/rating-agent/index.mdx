## 1. Context & Problem

Customers and merchandising teams often want a quick, reliable read on how well a book is performing without having to scroll through dozens or hundreds of individual comments. At the same time, raw star averages alone can be misleading—a 5-star average from two reviews carries very different weight than a 4.6-star average from two hundred reviews.

The Rating Agent exists within [[domain|reviews]] to turn raw [[entity|feedback]] into an on-demand, human-readable quality assessment. Crucially, this assessment is purely advisory: it must never alter stored customer reviews, change official book scores, or expose personal details that customers may have typed into their comments.

## 2. Business Value

- **Clear merchandising signals**: Translates raw ratings and review volume into consistent tiers—**Best Seller**, **Good**, **Bad**, or **No Data**—with a clear explanation and confidence indicator.
- **Faster decision-making**: Helps shoppers, product managers, and merchandisers understand sentiment patterns and rating distribution at a glance.
- **Customer privacy protection**: Automatically detects and masks personal information in review text before analysis and before returning results, while keeping the original review records untouched.
- **Operational safety**: Runs under strict read-only rules and usage limits, ensuring AI analysis cannot corrupt official review data or trigger unintended system changes.

## 3. How It Works

When a user or downstream experience requests a feedback summary for a book, an orchestration workflow validates the request, coordinates with shared specialist capabilities and the Rating Agent, and formats the final response:

1. **Gather book context first**: The Rating Agent retrieves up-to-date catalog details for the book (such as title, authors, category, price, and overall score counts) before inspecting individual reviews.
2. **Read customer reviews**: It then retrieves the book's submitted ratings and comments in read-only mode, subject to strict per-minute usage caps.
3. **Protect sensitive data**: Privacy guardrails inspect incoming and outgoing text to detect and mask personal information before it reaches the language model or appears in the final summary.
4. **Classify and summarize**: Using consistent business thresholds, the Rating Agent assigns one of four classifications:
   - **Best Seller**: Average rating of 4.5 or higher with at least 50 reviews, or 4.0 or higher with at least 200 reviews.
   - **Good**: Average rating of 3.5 or higher with at least 10 reviews.
   - **Bad**: Average rating below 3.5, or recurring negative feedback patterns.
   - **No Data**: No ratings available for the book.
5. **Return derived insight**: The response includes the classification, supporting metrics (average, total count, and score breakdown), a brief justification, and a confidence level.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Point-in-time insight vs. stored state**: Generating summaries on demand avoids storing stale AI text and keeps official scores clean, though each summary reflects only the reviews available at request time and slightly increases response latency.
- **Strict read-only governance**: Blocking all write actions and limiting review lookups protects data integrity and controls model costs, at the expense of requiring separate workflows for any moderation or editing needs.
- **Inline privacy redaction**: Routing text through an external privacy service prevents accidental personal data exposure in AI prompts and outputs, introducing an external dependency that must stay healthy for summaries to run.
- **Context size management**: Automatically condensing long conversation and review histories keeps large review sets within model limits, which means very large volumes of comments are summarized rather than analyzed word-for-word in full.

## 5. Technical Reference

The Rating Agent is the sole Rating-domain agent resource. Hosted inside the Rating service, it turns raw [[entity|feedback]] into a derived, read-time insight and never changes stored feedback or Catalog's rating projection (see [[domain|reviews]]).

The `RatingSummarizer` workflow is orchestration for the feedback-summary endpoint, not a separate Rating-domain agent. It validates input, routes work to the shared Chat service's specialist agents and this Rating Agent, then formats the response.

### Behavior & Runtime Settings

- The agent instructions request `get_book` first, followed by `GetCustomerReviews`.
- Classification rules:
  - **Best Seller**: average >= 4.5 with 50+ reviews, or >= 4.0 with 200+ reviews
  - **Good**: average >= 3.5 with 10+ reviews
  - **Bad**: average below 3.5 (`< 3.5`) or negative feedback patterns
  - **No Data**: no ratings
- Output: classification, metrics (average, count, distribution), brief justification, confidence level.
- Runtime settings: model `model-router` (`2025-11-18`, Microsoft), temperature `0.4`, max `1,500` output tokens, multiple tool calls allowed.

### Guardrails and Governance

- Agent Governance policy `rating-agent.yaml` uses `default_action: deny`: only allow-listed read tools run, write tools and destructive SQL (`DROP`, `DELETE`, `UPDATE`, ...) are blocked, and `get_reviews` is limited to 30/minute.
- PII middleware and guardrail middleware wrap the chat client.
- The Presidio PII middleware calls [[service|PresidioAPI]] in the external [[system|data-privacy]] system through [[command|AnalyzeText]] to detect PII, then [[command|AnonymizeText]] with the detected entities to redact sensitive text before it reaches the model or the output; stored feedback is unchanged. These are HTTP calls made by middleware, not model-selected tools or Kafka messages.
- Context compaction pipeline: tool-result compaction (20+ messages), summarization (3,000 tokens), sliding window (12 turns), truncation (24,000 tokens).
