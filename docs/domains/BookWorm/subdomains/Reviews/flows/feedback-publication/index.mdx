## 1. Context & Problem

When a customer submits a book review, that feedback needs to power product discovery ratings in [[domain|catalog]] without turning Catalog into a second owner of customer comments, reviewer identities, or moderation history.

This flow captures the exact boundary between [[domain|reviews]] and [[domain|catalog]]. It ensures a submitted review is persisted in Reviews, reflected as an aggregated rating summary in Catalog, and automatically rolled back if the referenced book does not exist.

## 2. Business Value

- **Trusted product discovery**: Surfaces timely average ratings and review counts to shoppers browsing the catalog.
- **Clear domain boundaries**: Keeps Catalog focused on merchandising summaries while Reviews remains the single system of record for individual customer feedback.
- **No silent data drift**: Automatically cleans up orphaned reviews when a book reference is invalid instead of leaving mismatched records behind.
- **Auditable and recoverable design**: Makes every stage—submission, projection update, and failure compensation—explicit and traceable for support and operations.

## 3. How It Works

The end-to-end publication and compensation flow follows two paths:

1. **Happy path — submit and project**:
   - A customer submits a rating and optional comment for a specific book.
   - Reviews validates the book reference, rating score, reviewer names, and comment, then saves the authoritative feedback record.
   - Once saved, Reviews broadcasts a notification that new feedback was created.
   - Catalog receives the notification, recalculates the book's derived average rating and review count, and makes the refreshed summary visible for product discovery—without storing the individual review text.
2. **Failure path — missing book compensation**:
   - If Catalog cannot apply the rating update because the referenced book does not exist, it does not retain a partial score or silently ignore the error.
   - Instead, Catalog emits a failure notice.
   - Reviews responds to that failure notice by deleting the invalid feedback record, keeping both domains aligned without leaving orphaned rating data.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Optimistic acceptance with compensation vs. synchronous pre-check**: Accepting valid feedback immediately in Reviews and compensating only when Catalog reports a missing book avoids tight runtime coupling and keeps review submission resilient, at the cost of needing an explicit cleanup path for rare invalid-book cases.
- **Derived projection vs. shared ownership**: Treating Catalog's rating summary as a derived cache ensures the summary can always be rebuilt from Reviews' authoritative records, while avoiding duplicated review storage.
- **Operational clarity**: Making the failure path explicit yields three clear rules—reviews are accepted only when the review store commits them, catalog updates are derived downstream, and failed projections trigger cleanup rather than silent drift.
