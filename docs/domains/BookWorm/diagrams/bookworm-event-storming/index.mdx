## Event storming board

This workshop artifact was created to align the team around the BookWorm business
journey before implementation. It captures the end-to-end flow from product discovery
to purchase, fulfillment, and post-purchase experiences, while also surfacing the
critical events, commands, policies, and external dependencies that shape the domain.

The board intentionally focuses on behavior first: what happened, why it mattered,
and which aggregate or bounded context owns the decision. That makes it a strong
foundation for the rest of the domain model, including the subdomain boundaries,
commands, and integration contracts documented elsewhere in BookWorm.

### What is on the board

The event-storming model highlights several layers of the domain:

- Business events such as `BookListed`, `BookAddedToBasket`, `CheckoutStarted`,
  `OrderCreated`, and `OrderShipped`.
- Commands that trigger those events, such as search, add-to-cart, checkout,
  confirm payment, and fulfilment updates.
- Policy checks and invariants, including product availability, stock validation,
  pricing consistency, and order acceptance rules.
- Read models and projections used to support customer-facing experiences such as
  product details, basket summaries, and order status.
- External collaborators such as payment, delivery, and notification systems that
  sit outside the core BookWorm domain boundaries.

### Business flow captured in the workshop

1. A customer discovers a book and reviews catalog metadata.
2. The customer adds selections to a basket and confirms the purchase intent.
3. The system validates the basket, price, and availability before accepting the
   checkout.
4. An order is created and ownership shifts from shopping to ordering.
5. The platform manages fulfilment, notification, and customer communication.
6. Post-order experiences such as rating, review, and conversational assistance
   rely on domain events without re-creating the canonical source data.

### How to read the board

- Event cards describe what happened in the business, not what the system did.
- Commands sit on the left or in the workflow path that triggers a state change.
- Aggregates and bounded contexts clarify which teams or subsystems own the decision.
- External systems are shown as actors or integrations to make dependencies visible.
- Shared pain points and inconsistencies in the original process often surface as
  missing events, duplicated states, or unclear ownership.

### Outcomes of the workshop

The event-storming session established a common understanding of:

- the core BookWorm purchase journey,
- the roles of the catalog, shopping, ordering, and conversational subdomains,
- where domain boundaries and integration seams should exist,
- and the event-driven information flows needed to keep the platform scalable and
  understandable.

This artifact is a living design reference. It complements the implementation docs,
bounded context definitions, and flow diagrams in the BookWorm catalog, and helps
explain how business intent becomes technical behavior.
