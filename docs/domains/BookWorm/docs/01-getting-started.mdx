## 1. Context & Problem

BookWorm is an online bookstore covering book discovery, basket management, checkout, order tracking, customer feedback, and conversational assistance. Without a clear map of how these areas fit together and which team owns each decision, newcomers—whether architects, project managers, analysts, or developers—can easily misunderstand boundaries, route questions to the wrong group, or introduce changes in the wrong part of the platform.

This guide serves as the entry point to [[domain|BookWorm]], explaining the platform's business scope, how documentation is organized, and where accountability lives.

## 2. Business Value

- **Faster onboarding**: Gives any new contributor a structured path from high-level business strategy down to system behavior and operational guides.
- **Clear team accountability**: Removes ambiguity over who owns business rules, system operations, and domain decisions.
- **Safer change delivery**: Reduces the risk of cross-team conflicts and accidental coupling by clarifying ownership boundaries up front.

## 3. How It Works

### Recommended reading order

Navigate the documentation in five steps so you understand both what BookWorm does and why it is structured this way:

1. Start with [[domain|BookWorm]] for strategic direction and the overall landscape map.
2. Read the subdomain pages to understand business responsibilities and ownership boundaries.
3. Review the system pages for concrete boundaries between major components.
4. Consult guides and runbooks when handling operational issues or recurring change patterns.
5. Check interaction agreements and flows before altering any cross-area behavior.

### Subdomains and ownership

Each business area has a designated domain owner accountable for its scope and business decisions:

| Subdomain                  | Responsibility                                            | Domain owner                |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| [[domain\|catalog]]        | Book information, pricing, availability, and search       | [[team\|business-strategy]] |
| [[domain\|shopping]]       | Customer basket and pre-order checkout                    | [[team\|business-strategy]] |
| [[domain\|ordering]]       | Accepted orders and their business lifecycle              | [[team\|business-strategy]] |
| [[domain\|reviews]]        | Customer feedback and ratings                             | [[team\|business-strategy]] |
| [[domain\|conversational]] | Customer-facing AI assistance and book discovery          | [[team\|contributor-team]]  |
| [[domain\|platform]]       | Scheduled work and shared email notification capabilities | [[team\|platform-team]]     |

### Team responsibilities

| Team                        | How it contributes                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| [[team\|business-strategy]] | Owns the root BookWorm domain and the Catalog, Shopping, Ordering, and Reviews subdomains in the current catalog.                                      |
| [[team\|contributor-team]]  | Develops and maintains the platform; it is the recorded owner of Conversational and implements systems and services across multiple subdomains.        |
| [[team\|platform-team]]     | Maintains shared infrastructure and is the recorded owner of the Platform subdomain. It also co-owns the Product Catalog System with Contributor Team. |

### Ownership boundaries to keep in mind

- **Catalog** owns book details, prices, and availability. Other areas consume that information through their own agreed models.
- **Shopping** owns the active basket before purchase. Clearing a basket does not prove an order was completed.
- **Ordering** owns the accepted order and its official business status. Financial workflow progress or email delivery does not alter that status.
- **Reviews** owns customer feedback. Displaying a rating summary in Catalog does not transfer ownership of the underlying reviews.
- **Conversational** can answer questions and research options, but Catalog remains the source of book facts; conversational assistance does not directly view or modify live basket or order state.
- **Platform** runs scheduled tasks and delivers notifications, without owning the business records of the areas it supports.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Domain ownership vs. system co-ownership**: A single team may own business direction for a subdomain while different teams build or co-own the underlying systems (for example, [[system|product-catalog-system]] is co-owned by Platform Team and Contributor Team even though Business Strategy owns the Catalog subdomain). Separating business accountability from implementation ownership scales better, provided teams check resource-level ownership before acting.
- **Discipline at boundaries**: Requiring changes to happen inside the owning context and cross boundaries only through explicit agreements adds upfront coordination, but prevents fragile hidden dependencies across teams.

## 5. Technical Reference

### Metadata and ownership conventions

- Domain-level accountability is recorded in each subdomain's EventCatalog `owners` metadata field.
- Exact system and service ownership should always be verified in the `owners` field on the resource itself (such as [[system|product-catalog-system]], co-owned by [[team|platform-team]] and [[team|contributor-team]]).

### Cross-boundary integration rules

- Make data and behavior changes inside the owning bounded context.
- Communicate across bounded contexts only through documented APIs, events, commands, or queries.
- Never access another context's database directly.

### Pre-change checklist

1. Read [[domain|BookWorm]] for strategic classification, business invariants, and the full context map.
2. Open the relevant subdomain page and review its scope and systems.
3. Read the target resource's engineering docs, architecture decisions, and message definitions before modifying a service or contract.
4. Use subdomain guides and runbooks for common tasks and operational troubleshooting.
5. Confirm every change belongs to the right owner, preserves context boundaries, and updates contracts alongside documentation.
