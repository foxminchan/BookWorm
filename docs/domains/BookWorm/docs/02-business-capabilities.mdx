## 1. Context & Problem

An online bookstore is more than inventory counts and order forms—it is a connected experience stretching from discovering a book to deciding to buy, receiving confirmation, and returning later for reviews or support. Organizing BookWorm around individual user interface screens would collapse all of those responsibilities into one tangled layer, making every feature risky and slow to change.

To avoid that trap, BookWorm organizes its capabilities by business domain so each stage of the customer journey has clear ownership while still working together seamlessly.

## 2. Business Value

- **Cohesive customer experience**: Connects discovery, basket building, checkout, fulfillment, reviews, and conversational help into one smooth journey.
- **Independent evolution**: Lets teams improve pricing, checkout, order processing, feedback, or AI guidance without destabilizing unrelated capabilities.
- **Operational resilience**: Ensures supporting activities—such as sending emails or refreshing rating summaries—can lag or retry without corrupting official customer purchase records.

## 3. How It Works

### Capability map

| Capability                      | Primary owning context     | Purpose                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product discovery and metadata  | [[domain\|catalog]]        | Keep titles, authors, categories, pricing, and availability accurate.                         |
| Basket and checkout intent      | [[domain\|shopping]]       | Let customers add books, revise quantities, and prepare a purchase.                           |
| Order lifecycle management      | [[domain\|ordering]]       | Own accepted purchases and their lifecycle transitions.                                       |
| Customer feedback and ratings   | [[domain\|reviews]]        | Capture comments and aggregate ratings without making Reviews the source of book master data. |
| Conversational guidance         | [[domain\|conversational]] | Help customers find books, answer support questions, and interpret store policies.            |
| Notification and scheduled work | [[domain\|platform]]       | Deliver emails and run recurring operational jobs without owning business state.              |

### Customer journeys

1. **Discovery and selection**: The customer starts with search, browsing, or recommendations. [[domain|catalog]] is the single authority for book facts and availability; storefronts and conversational experiences present information drawn from Catalog rather than maintaining competing product records.
2. **Basket and checkout**: When a customer chooses a title, [[domain|shopping]] manages the temporary, editable basket representing intent to buy. At checkout, Shopping hands the confirmed purchase commitment to [[domain|ordering]], marking a clear shift from shopping intent to an official order.
3. **Order confirmation and processing**: Ordering records the accepted purchase and manages its official lifecycle status. Downstream activities such as Finance workflows and customer notifications depend on order progress, but do not control or own order state.
4. **Reviews and trust signals**: After discovering or purchasing a book, customers can submit feedback. [[domain|reviews]] stores those individual reviews and shares summary figures (such as average rating and review count) so Catalog can display trust signals without taking ownership of the underlying feedback.
5. **Support and conversational assistance**: Customers can ask questions through [[domain|conversational]], which helps with book questions, store policies, and straightforward support while respecting each domain's ownership rules.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Eventual alignment for derived views**: Treating Catalog rating summaries as derived views of Reviews means summary numbers may briefly trail newly submitted reviews. That small delay is an intentional trade-off so review activity never blocks or complicates product management.
- **Separating delivery from business outcome**: Treating notifications as supporting operational work rather than proof of an order outcome prevents email delivery issues from being confused with purchase success or failure.
- **Upfront boundary checks**: Blurred lines—such as mixing Catalog and Reviews or letting a screen bypass domain rules—create long-term data drift; checking ownership before scoping work prevents costly rework.

## 5. Technical Reference

BookWorm operates as a system of bounded contexts governed by three architectural rules:

- Each bounded context owns its source of truth.
- Cross-domain state changes occur through explicit contracts rather than shared persistence.
- Derived projections can lag or be rebuilt/corrected without rewriting authoritative business state.

### Pre-implementation validation checklist

Before finalizing a product or platform design across [[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], [[domain|reviews]], [[domain|conversational]], or [[domain|platform]], verify:

1. Is the change placed in the correct bounded context?
2. Does the authoritative source of truth remain unambiguous?
3. Are downstream projections treated as derived rather than authoritative?
4. Does the end-to-end customer experience stay consistent across discovery, basket, order, and feedback?

If any answer is unclear, resolve the domain ownership or contract definition before implementation begins.
