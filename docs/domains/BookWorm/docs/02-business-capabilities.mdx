## What BookWorm delivers

BookWorm is a bookstore platform that turns product discovery into a purchase,
then supports the review and support experience around that journey. The value of
the system is not just inventory and order handling; it is the combined flow from
finding a book to deciding to buy it, receiving a confirmation, and later
revisiting the experience through recommendations, support, and feedback.

The business capabilities are organized by domain rather than by UI screen. That
means the platform can evolve without collapsing the ownership of data and
responsibility into a single monolithic application layer.

## Capability map

| Capability                      | Primary owning context     | Purpose                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product discovery and metadata  | [[domain\|catalog]]        | Keep titles, authors, categories, pricing, and availability accurate.                         |
| Basket and checkout intent      | [[domain\|shopping]]       | Let customers add books, revise quantities, and prepare a purchase.                           |
| Order lifecycle management      | [[domain\|ordering]]       | Own accepted purchases and their lifecycle transitions.                                       |
| Customer feedback and ratings   | [[domain\|reviews]]        | Capture comments and aggregate ratings without making Reviews the source of book master data. |
| Conversational guidance         | [[domain\|conversational]] | Help customers find books, answer support questions, and interpret store policies.            |
| Notification and scheduled work | [[domain\|platform]]       | Deliver emails and run recurring operational jobs without owning business state.              |

## Customer journeys

### 1. Discovery and selection

The customer begins with search, browsing, or recommendation. At this stage,
[[domain|catalog]] is the actor of record for book facts and availability. A user
may discover a title through the product catalog, a recommendation flow, or a
book-related conversational request.

The important boundary is that discovery data is derived from Catalog data, not a
separate system of record. This allows the storefront and conversational systems
to present accurate information while keeping the product domain authoritative.

### 2. Basket and checkout

Once a customer wants a title, the experience enters the [[domain|shopping]]
context. A basket is mutable, temporary, and customer-driven. It represents the
intent to purchase, not a completed order.

When the basket is checked out, Shopping hands the business commitment to
[[domain|ordering]]. This creates a distinct transition from shopping intent to
accepted order business state.

### 3. Order confirmation and processing

Ordering takes responsibility for the accepted order. It persists the purchase,
records the lifecycle status, and exposes the order as the business source of
truth. Related workflows such as Finance, notifications, and downstream side
Effects depend on the order state but do not own it.

### 4. Reviews and trust signals

After purchase or discovery, customers may leave feedback. [[domain|reviews]]
stores this feedback and publishes the information needed for derived catalog
signals such as average rating and review count. The rating summary remains a
projection into Catalog and is not treated as the original review record.

### 5. Support and conversational assistance

Customers may also ask for help through the conversational experience. The
[[domain|conversational]] context routes requests and responds using the right
organizational boundaries. It can answer questions about books, store policies,
and straightforward support scenarios without bypassing domain ownership.

## The product as a system of systems

BookWorm's value emerges when these capabilities work together, but the domain
boundaries still matter. A poor design would blur Catalog and Reviews, or treat a
notification as an order outcome. The operating principle is simple:

- Each domain owns its source of truth.
- Cross-domain changes happen through explicit contracts.
- Derived data can lag or be corrected without rewriting the real business state.

## What to check before changing the domain

Before a product or platform change is considered complete, validate whether it
matches the customer journey and the ownership model:

1. Is the change in the correct bounded context?
2. Is the source of truth still clear?
3. Are downstream projections treated as derived rather than authoritative?
4. Is the customer experience consistent across discovery, basket, order, and
   feedback?

If any answer is unclear, the change likely needs more contract or domain
clarification before implementation.
