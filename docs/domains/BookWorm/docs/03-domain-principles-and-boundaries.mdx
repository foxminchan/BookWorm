## 1. Context & Problem

As a digital commerce platform grows, the greatest risk to maintainability is allowing every capability to share one tangled data model. When multiple areas read and update the same records directly, nobody can tell which part of the business owns the truth, small changes trigger unexpected side effects, and auditing or troubleshooting becomes difficult.

BookWorm solves this by separating its domain into well-defined areas with strict rules on ownership, derived copies, and how changes travel across boundaries.

## 2. Business Value

- **Unambiguous accountability**: Every core business fact—product price, active basket, accepted order, customer review—has a single authoritative home.
- **Safer, faster delivery**: Teams can evolve internal workflows without breaking neighboring business areas as long as shared agreements remain intact.
- **Easy recovery from display or search drift**: Supporting summaries, search indexes, and temporary speedups can be rebuilt cleanly without risking core business records.
- **Clear auditing and troubleshooting**: Separating operational tasks (like retrying an email) from official business decisions makes order history and customer support far easier to verify.

## 3. How It Works

### Guiding principles

1. **One owner for every source of truth**: Product facts live in [[domain|catalog]], temporary shopping intent lives in [[domain|shopping]], accepted purchases live in [[domain|ordering]], and individual customer feedback lives in [[domain|reviews]]. Supporting platform features such as notifications and scheduled work help the business operate without owning business records.
2. **Derived information is clearly marked and rebuildable**: Some information is intentionally copied for convenience or speed—such as rating summaries shown alongside books, search indexes built from catalog details, and temporary performance copies. These are never treated as independent authorities and can be regenerated from the original source if they ever drift.
3. **Areas collaborate through explicit agreements, never shortcuts**: One area never reaches directly into another area's records. Instead, key transitions—such as handing a basket over to create an order, sharing order updates, publishing review summaries, or triggering notifications—happen through formal, documented handoffs.
4. **Operational work stays separate from business ownership**: Background tasks—like refreshing search data or retrying a failed email—must never alter the official business state of an order or a book record.

### Core business rules

- A basket is not an order.
- An accepted checkout creates a single order.
- Ordering owns the order lifecycle after acceptance.
- Catalog owns the canonical product and pricing information.
- Reviews owns the review record; Catalog owns only the derived summary.
- Platform notifications represent delivery work, not business state.
- Conversational assistance helps customers access information without owning the underlying records.

### Ownership model by subdomain

The contexts in this table are [[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], [[domain|reviews]], [[domain|conversational]], and [[domain|platform]].

| Subdomain      | What it owns                                                   | What it does not own                       |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Catalog        | Product metadata, pricing, availability, and search projection | Individual review records or order state   |
| Shopping       | Basket state and pre-order intent                              | Accepted orders or final lifecycle state   |
| Ordering       | Order record and status transitions                            | Basket lifecycle or product master data    |
| Reviews        | Feedback records, ratings, and review history                  | Authoritative book metadata                |
| Conversational | Customer-facing interaction patterns and book support guidance | Booking, billing, and order-of-record data |
| Platform       | Notifications, scheduled jobs, and infrastructure support      | Business state of the domains it serves    |

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Explicit handoffs over shared shortcuts**: Prohibiting direct cross-area data reads takes more upfront design on workflows like basket-to-order handoff or order status propagation, but prevents hidden coupling and accidental cross-context updates.
- **Eventual alignment for projections**: Derived summaries and search data may briefly lag behind primary updates; accepting this minor delay keeps core transactions simple and recoverable.
- **Common anti-patterns to avoid**:
  - Reading order status, review details, or basket state directly from another context's storage.
  - Treating a derived summary or projection as the original source of truth.
  - Mixing operational retry status with official business status.
  - Embedding business rules inside scheduled background jobs or email delivery flows.
  - Interpreting an accepted email send as proof that a purchase or support action succeeded.

## 5. Technical Reference

BookWorm enforces bounded-context boundaries across [[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], [[domain|reviews]], [[domain|conversational]], and [[domain|platform]]:

- **Cross-context contracts**: Communicate exclusively via explicit commands, events, queries, or synchronous service interfaces—never direct reads/writes against another context's primary database.
- **Derived projections & caching**: Catalog rating summaries (projected from Reviews), search vectors (derived from Catalog), and cache entries (temporary optimization layers) are disposable/replayable and never authoritative.
- **Operational separation**: Scheduled jobs (such as vector index updates or email retries) must never mutate authoritative Order or Book domain state.

### Contributor design checklist

1. Identify the owning bounded context before modifying a model.
2. Classify data explicitly as source-of-truth or derived.
3. Prefer explicit event or command boundaries over shared persistence.
4. Confirm the customer-facing product experience still aligns with the domain contract.
5. Update the contract and documentation whenever ownership changes.

### Three-question boundary gate

For any cross-context change, verify before implementation:

1. Which context owns the data?
2. What explicit contract carries the change?
3. Is the downstream side effect derived or authoritative?
