**BookWorm** runs an e-commerce platform that allows users to browse and purchase
[[entity|book]]s online,
providing a seamless shopping experience. The platform is designed to handle various aspects of
the online bookstore, including product catalog management, shopping cart functionality,
checkout processes, and order management.

## Domain-Driven Design

BookWorm is organised as a bounded-context landscape. The core subdomains are
[[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], and
[[domain|conversational]]. [[domain|reviews]] is a supporting subdomain, while
[[domain|platform]] provides generic capabilities such as notifications and
scheduled processing.

Each subdomain owns its model and ubiquitous language. Context boundaries are
crossed through explicit events and contracts rather than by sharing domain
entities:

- **Catalog** is the upstream source for book availability and product
  information. Shopping and Reviews consume catalog concepts through their own
  models.
- **Shopping** coordinates the customer cart and checkout experience, then
  hands an accepted purchase to Ordering.
- **Ordering** owns order lifecycle and fulfilment state. Conversational uses
  an anti-corruption layer when presenting order information to customers.
- **Platform** is a generic-domain supplier for cross-cutting capabilities such
  as email delivery and scheduled jobs.

### Business invariants

- A [[entity|basket]] cannot be checked out without valid [[entity|book]] and
  price information.
- An accepted Checkout creates exactly one Order.
- [[domain|ordering]] owns the Order lifecycle after acceptance.
- Reviews references a [[entity|book]] identity but does not own Catalog data.
- Platform notifications must not change Ordering or payment state.

### Domain boundaries

BookWorm owns the customer-facing book discovery and purchase experience,
including catalog information, baskets, checkout, orders, reviews, and
conversational assistance. It does not own payment-provider settlement,
external shipping execution, email infrastructure internals, AI model hosting,
or identity and authentication infrastructure.

See the [ubiquitous language](/docs/domains/BookWorm/language) for shared
business terms and definitions.
Each bounded context also maintains its own language where terms have a
context-specific meaning.
