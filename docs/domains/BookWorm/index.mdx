BookWorm decomposes its online bookstore platform into six bounded contexts classified by Domain-Driven Design (DDD) strategic importance: four Core domains (`Catalog`, `Shopping`, `Ordering`, `Conversational`), one Supporting domain (`Reviews`), and one Generic domain (`Platform`). Each context owns its authoritative data and communicates across boundaries exclusively through versioned HTTP, gRPC, MCP, A2A, or Kafka contracts.

## Scope and constraints

- **In scope:** Product discovery and catalog management, customer baskets, checkout and order lifecycle management, customer feedback and AI summaries, conversational assistance, and cross-cutting scheduling and notification delivery.
- **Out of scope:** External payment-provider settlement, physical shipping carrier execution, external SMTP delivery internals, AI model hosting internals, and external identity provider infrastructure (Microsoft Entra ID).
- **Key constraints:** Bounded contexts never share databases; derived projections (Catalog rating summaries, Qdrant vectors, Marten order summaries) remain rebuildable from authoritative sources.

### Edge applications

Three Next.js 16 applications run behind the YARP API Gateway (`gateway`): **Storefront** (`@bookworm/storefront`) serves public catalog browsing, AI chat, and customer authentication; **Commerce** (`@bookworm/commerce`) serves authenticated `/basket`, `/checkout/*`, and `/account/*` routes; **Backoffice** (`@bookworm/backoffice`) serves staff administration under `/backoffice`.

## Business invariants

- A basket cannot proceed to checkout without valid Catalog book identities and current pricing.
- Every accepted checkout creates one authoritative `Order` in `Ordering` with status `New`.
- `Ordering` owns the `Order` lifecycle (`New` → `Completed` or `Cancelled`); Finance workflow timeouts and Notification outcomes never alter `Order` status.
- `Reviews` references books strictly by `BookId` UUID and does not own Catalog metadata.
- `Conversational` reads Catalog through MCP tools and Rating through A2A gRPC; it answers Ordering and Shopping policy questions as read-only guidance without modifying live baskets or orders.
- `Platform` triggers jobs and delivers emails without taking ownership of any business domain's records.

## Deployment and cross-cutting concerns

- **Hosting and ingress:** YARP API Gateway is the sole externally exposed entry point; all backend services, frontends, and Presidio sidecars run internally behind it and deploy via Aspire 17 (`aspire deploy`).
- **Security and privacy:** Microsoft Entra ID governs user and service authentication (`admin`, `business_user`, `customer` roles plus Entra Agent Identities). Presidio screens AI prompts and outputs fail-closed.
- **Observability and resilience:** OpenTelemetry propagates W3C trace context across HTTP, gRPC, and Kafka; Wolverine routes exhausted Kafka consumers to per-service dead-letter queues.

## Quality scenarios and known risks

| Quality attribute / risk      | Scenario or exposure                                                   | Current mitigation or gap                                                                      |
| :---------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Modifiability**             | Domain rules evolve without cross-service database coupling            | Enforced by `tests/BookWorm.ArchTests` (`ArchUnitNET.TUnit`) and Pact contract tests           |
| **Eventual consistency**      | Catalog rating summaries and Qdrant search vectors trail source writes | Explicitly modeled as rebuildable projections; see [[domain\|catalog]] and [[domain\|reviews]] |
| **External boundary outages** | External SMTP, Qdrant Cloud, or Kafka interruption                     | MailKit resilience, Wolverine retries + DLQs, and FusionCache fail-safe                        |
