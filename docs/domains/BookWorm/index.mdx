## 1. Context & Problem

**BookWorm** runs an online bookstore enabling customers to browse and purchase [[entity|book]]s through a seamless end-to-end experience—covering product discovery, shopping cart management, checkout, order processing, customer reviews, and conversational support.

Operating all of these capabilities inside a single undifferentiated system would blur business rules and tie differentiating customer features directly to commodity infrastructure. BookWorm therefore structures the domain into distinct areas with clear strategic roles and explicit external boundaries—separating what BookWorm owns from external capabilities like payment settlement, shipping execution, email infrastructure internals, AI model hosting, and identity infrastructure.

## 2. Business Value

- **Focused investment**: Classifying capabilities by strategic importance ensures teams invest deep effort in differentiating bookstore experiences (discovery, shopping, ordering, conversational help) while keeping supporting and commodity utilities modular.
- **Reliable customer transactions**: Clear domain rules protect purchase integrity—ensuring checkouts use valid book and pricing data and each accepted checkout produces a single official order.
- **Cleaner partner integration**: Defining explicit boundaries around external payment, delivery, email, AI, and identity providers prevents third-party details from leaking into core business decisions.

## 3. How It Works

BookWorm coordinates six specialized business areas across the customer journey:

- **[[domain|catalog]]** supplies authoritative book information, pricing, and availability. Other areas consume product facts through their own tailored views.
- **[[domain|shopping]]** manages the customer [[entity|basket]] and checkout experience, then hands off an accepted purchase to Ordering.
- **[[domain|ordering]]** owns the official order lifecycle and fulfillment progress, while Conversational translates order details safely for customers without exposing or altering internal order mechanics.
- **[[domain|conversational]]** delivers AI-assisted customer guidance and support.
- **[[domain|reviews]]** captures customer ratings and feedback as a supporting capability.
- **[[domain|platform]]** provides shared, generic capabilities such as email delivery and scheduled operational tasks.

### Business invariants

- A [[entity|basket]] cannot be checked out without valid [[entity|book]] and price information.
- An accepted checkout creates exactly one Order.
- [[domain|ordering]] owns the Order lifecycle after acceptance.
- Reviews references a [[entity|book]] identity but does not own Catalog data.
- Platform notifications must not change Ordering or payment state.

### Domain boundaries and shared language

BookWorm owns the customer-facing book discovery and purchase experience—catalog information, baskets, checkout, orders, reviews, and conversational assistance—and leaves payment-provider settlement, external shipping execution, email infrastructure internals, AI model hosting, and identity/authentication infrastructure outside its domain. Shared business terms are defined in the [ubiquitous language](/docs/domains/BookWorm/language) alongside the [[diagram|bookworm-event-storming]] board.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Explicit contracts over shared entities**: Preventing subdomains from sharing internal records directly requires translating concepts across boundaries (for instance, Shopping and Reviews maintain their own view of catalog items, and Conversational translates Ordering data rather than reading it raw). This adds translation effort, but keeps every subdomain independent and resilient to change.
- **Externalized commodity infrastructure**: Keeping payment settlement, physical shipping, email transport, AI model hosting, and authentication outside BookWorm narrows internal operational burden while requiring clear boundary safeguards so external outages or delays do not corrupt core order state.

## 5. Technical Reference

- **Bounded-context landscape**: Core ([[domain|catalog]], [[domain|shopping]], [[domain|ordering]], [[domain|conversational]]), Supporting ([[domain|reviews]]), and Generic ([[domain|platform]]).
- **Integration mechanics**: Boundaries are crossed via explicit events and contracts rather than shared domain entities; [[domain|conversational]] uses an anti-corruption layer when presenting [[domain|ordering]] information.
- **Ubiquitous language & modeling**: Each bounded context maintains context-specific terminology alongside the shared [ubiquitous language](/docs/domains/BookWorm/language) and the [[diagram|bookworm-event-storming]] artifact.
