## 1. Context & Problem

BookWorm spans catalog management, pre-purchase shopping, order fulfillment, customer reviews, conversational discovery, and platform messaging. When product managers, business analysts, solution architects, and developers use everyday words like "book", "basket", "buyer", "order", or "checkout" differently across subdomains, requirements drift and service boundaries blur.

This domain-level ubiquitous language establishes one shared set of core concepts and ownership rules across the entire BookWorm domain.

## 2. Business Value

- **Shared cross-team vocabulary:** Aligns business stakeholders and engineering teams on the exact meaning of BookWorm's core domain concepts.
- **Protected service ownership:** Makes explicit which subdomain owns each record—such as books in Catalog, baskets in Shopping, orders in Ordering, and feedback in Reviews—preventing accidental data duplication.
- **Reliable business semantics:** Distinguishes mutable shopping intent from durable purchase records, live catalog prices from recorded order prices, and checkout initiation from completed orders.

## 3. How It Works

The BookWorm domain vocabulary connects six core capability areas:

- **Catalog & Reviews (`Book`, `Feedback`):** A **Book** is a title represented in the bookstore catalog with descriptive, pricing, availability, and classification details owned by Catalog, while other contexts reference it only by identifier. **Feedback** captures a customer's rating and optional comment about a book; Reviews owns individual submissions, whereas Catalog maintains only an aggregate rating summary.
- **Shopping (`Basket`):** A **Basket** is a customer's changeable collection of book references and quantities before purchase, representing mutable shopping intent rather than an order or an inventory reservation.
- **Checkout & Ordering (`Checkout`, `Buyer`, `Order`, `Order Item`):** **Checkout** is the cross-context process that coordinates transitioning a checked-out basket into the order workflow—it is a process, not another name for an order. A **Buyer** is the person associated with an order in Ordering (holding a name and delivery address, and not automatically sharing the same identifier used for a shopping basket). An **Order** is the durable purchase record with a lifecycle status, composed of **Order Items** that record the book identifier, quantity, and price used for that purchase rather than live catalog pricing.
- **Conversational Support & Platform (`Chat Request`, `Idempotency Guard`, `Dead-Letter Queue`, `MJML Template`, `Vector Batch Job`):** A **Chat Request** is a customer question routed or answered by conversational agents that may discuss a book or order without taking ownership of those records. Platform executes stateless notifications using **MJML Template** rendering, **Idempotency Guard** deduplication in Redis, and **Dead-Letter Queue** routing on exhausted retries, while **Vector Batch Job** triggers periodic Catalog vector indexing.

## 4. Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

- **Identifier references over duplicated entities:** Having other contexts reference a **Book** by identifier instead of copying full catalog data keeps Catalog as the single source of truth, while requiring targeted lookups or read projections when enriched details are needed.
- **Recorded order prices vs. live catalog prices:** Storing the agreed purchase price on each **Order Item** guarantees historical financial accuracy when catalog prices change, whereas a **Basket** remains transient and reflects current catalog state until checkout.
- **Aggregated ratings vs. individual reviews:** Keeping detailed **Feedback** records in Reviews while storing only an aggregate summary in Catalog avoids tight runtime coupling between browsing and reviews, at the cost of eventual consistency for rating totals.
