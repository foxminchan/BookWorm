## Context and problem statement

Book discovery, shopping, ordering, reviews, conversations, and platform operations have different goals and change at different times. When they share the same business rules and information, a change in one area can affect several teams or require coordinated updates elsewhere.

BookWorm needs a consistent way to organize work so that every area manages its own rules and outcomes while still sharing reliable common tools.

## Decision drivers

- **Easy to change:** A change should stay within the area responsible for it.
- **Easy to release:** Compatible improvements should reach customers without requiring every area to update at the same time.
- **Clear responsibility:** Each area should clearly own the outcomes it provides.
- **Consistent quality:** Shared tools should remain available everywhere without making one area responsible for another area's decisions.

## Considered options

1. Keep areas organized around the responsibilities they serve
2. Combine everything into one application
3. Divide one application into separate parts
4. Divide work by specialty

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: **Keep areas organized around the responsibilities they serve**.

Each of BookWorm's six business areas will manage its own rules, information, and customer behavior. The areas will communicate through clearly agreed exchanges rather than sharing hidden decisions.

Common tools may help all areas with everyday work, but they will not contain rules that belong to a particular business area. This decision applies to how the whole system is organized, not to how an individual area is built.

### Consequences

- **Positive:** Teams can understand, change, and release their area more independently.
- **Positive:** Customers receive improvements faster because one area does not need to wait for every area to change.
- **Positive:** Responsibility is easier to identify when a result or rule is incorrect.
- **Negative:** Work that crosses several areas needs clear agreements and careful recovery when part of it fails.
- **Negative:** Finding the cause of a problem may require checking more than one area.
- **Follow-up:** Confirm that work remains in the correct business area and review the arrangement when the platform team evaluates the system.

## Pros and cons of the options

### Keep areas organized around the responsibilities they serve

- **Good**, because rules and information stay with the team responsible for them.
- **Good**, because areas can grow and improve independently when their agreements remain compatible.
- **Bad**, because work spanning several areas can take longer to coordinate and diagnose.

### Combine everything into one application

- **Good**, because all parts communicate quickly and some actions are easier to complete.
- **Bad**, because one change can affect every area and slow down release cycles.
- **Bad**, because a failure can spread across the whole application.

### Divide one application into separate parts

- **Good**, because areas remain clearly separated without the cost of separate applications.
- **Bad**, because all parts are released together and depend on the same application being available.
- **Bad**, because this would move away from the way BookWorm is already organized.

### Divide work by specialty

- **Good**, because teams can specialize in particular kinds of work.
- **Bad**, because one customer feature would cross several teams and ownership would become unclear.
