## Context

Catalog, Shopping, Ordering, Reviews, Conversational, and Platform change for different business reasons. A common business model or technical-layer decomposition would make changes cross ownership boundaries and obscure accountability.

## Decision

Retain the capability-aligned bounded-context decomposition. Each context owns its model and behavior; independently deployable services expose explicit contracts to other contexts. Shared infrastructure supplies technical mechanisms, not a common business layer.

This records the whole-system decomposition, not the internal structure or workflow choice of any one system.

## Decision drivers

- Modifiability: keep a business-rule change with its owner.
- Deployability: isolate compatible changes without requiring a platform-wide release.
- Correctness: make responsibility for an accepted business outcome unambiguous.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative                       | Assessment                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Single deployable monolith        | Simpler runtime coordination, but conflicts with the documented independent-service direction.       |
| Modular monolith                  | Preserves logical modules with less distribution cost, but changes the established deployment model. |
| Services split by technical layer | Separates technology while spreading one business change across multiple services.                   |

## Consequences

- Ownership and impact analysis follow business capabilities.
- Cross-context work requires contracts, failure handling, and operational correlation.
- Distribution increases deployment and diagnostic complexity; independence is conditional on compatible interfaces.
