## Context and problem statement

Books, shopping carts, orders, reviews, chat sessions, and scheduled work have different lifecycles and rules. If several areas can freely change the same records, one area may bypass another area's checks and make independent changes difficult.

BookWorm needs one clear owner for each kind of business information.

## Decision drivers

- **Correctness:** One responsible area should enforce every rule and status change.
- **Easy evolution:** Storage details should change without breaking other areas.
- **Clear accountability:** One area should own both the information and the decisions about it.

## Considered options

1. Give each business area ownership of its own information
2. Store every kind of business information in one shared place
3. Route every storage request through one central area
4. Use physically separate storage for every area

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: **Give each business area ownership of its own information**.

Each area will store and protect the information it needs. It may choose the kind of storage that best matches how customers use that information.

Shared equipment may reduce costs, but private information remains separated and owned by the responsible area.

### Consequences

- **Positive:** An area can change how it stores information without changing agreements with its customers or partners.
- **Negative:** Information across areas cannot be combined with a single direct database query.
- **Follow-up:** Document storage choices for each area and check that areas do not bypass ownership boundaries.

## Pros and cons of the options

### Give each business area ownership of its own information

- **Good**, because every change passes through the responsible area's checks.
- **Good**, because each area can choose suitable storage.
- **Bad**, because reports and work that span several areas require additional coordination.

### Store every kind of business information in one shared place

- **Good**, because combined reports are simpler to build.
- **Bad**, because a change for one area can break or bypass another area.

### Route every storage request through one central area

- **Good**, because storage access is concentrated in one place.
- **Bad**, because the central area can become a bottleneck and still must understand many kinds of business work.

### Use physically separate storage for every area

- **Good**, because each area's storage is isolated.
- **Bad**, because this adds unnecessary cost and complexity for many small areas.
