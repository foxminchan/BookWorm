## Context and problem statement

No single test catches every problem in a system with many connected areas. Small checks verify business rules quickly but may miss communication changes. Full browser journeys confirm customer behavior but cannot cover every internal rule.

BookWorm needs several kinds of checks, each aimed at a different risk.

## Decision drivers

- **Business correctness:** Catch rule mistakes quickly.
- **Area compatibility:** Verify that connected areas understand each other's information.
- **Structure:** Prevent areas from bypassing ownership or violating agreed boundaries.

## Considered options

1. Use separate automated checks for business rules, structure, agreements, information formats, and customer journeys
2. Rely on small unit checks alone
3. Rely on full browser journeys alone
4. Rely on manual review alone

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: **Use separate automated checks for business rules, structure, agreements, information formats, and customer journeys**.

BookWorm will combine:

- Fast business-rule checks
- Architecture checks that enforce boundaries
- Producer and consumer agreement checks
- Information-format compatibility checks
- Customer browser journeys
- Documentation quality checks

### Consequences

- **Positive:** Teams receive fast, targeted feedback across all major risk areas.
- **Negative:** Multiple test types require maintenance and discipline.
- **Follow-up:** Add stronger checks where database and emulator behavior are not yet covered.

## Pros and cons of the options

### Use separate automated checks for business rules, structure, agreements, information formats, and customer journeys

- **Good**, because each risk has a suitable automated check.
- **Good**, because connected-area agreements can be tested without starting every live service.
- **Bad**, because contributors must maintain several kinds of checks.

### Rely on small unit checks alone

- **Good**, because checks run quickly.
- **Bad**, because they cannot detect broken agreements or website behavior.

### Rely on full browser journeys alone

- **Good**, because tests exercise real customer interactions.
- **Bad**, because they are slower and do not cover every internal rule.

### Rely on manual review alone

- **Good**, because humans apply judgment.
- **Bad**, because manual inspection misses many subtle communication and repeated-change mistakes.
