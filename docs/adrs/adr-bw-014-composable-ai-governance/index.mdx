## Context and problem statement

Chat assistance, review summaries, and connected AI tools handle customer words and review text. They may use models and external tools, so one bad input or one overly broad permission could cause harm.

BookWorm needs shared safety controls while also requiring each AI capability to declare its own permissions and limits.

## Decision drivers

- **Defensive layers:** Check personal data, instructions, results, and permissions before and after AI activity.
- **Minimum access:** Give each AI capability only the data and actions it needs.
- **Explicit verification:** Require every new AI route to declare and test its safety controls.

## Considered options

1. Use shared safety controls plus specific rules for each AI capability
2. Rely only on instructions given to the model
3. Build separate safety controls for every AI area
4. Assume one shared package protects every AI route

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: **Use shared safety controls plus specific rules for each AI capability**.

BookWorm will combine:

- Shared rules that apply across AI features
- Personal-data protection that removes or masks sensitive values
- Input and output checks that reject unsafe requests or results
- Separate identities and permissions for each AI capability

### Consequences

- **Positive:** AI areas share consistent privacy and safety controls while keeping permissions separate.
- **Negative:** Safety tools add work and may slow a response.
- **Follow-up:** Review rules and permissions whenever a new AI capability or tool is added.

## Pros and cons of the options

### Use shared safety controls plus specific rules for each AI capability

- **Good**, because proven controls are reused while permissions remain narrow.
- **Good**, because one AI capability cannot automatically inherit another's permissions.
- **Bad**, because no safety control can guarantee protection from every new attack.

### Rely only on instructions given to the model

- **Good**, because setup is simple.
- **Bad**, because instructions are not an enforceable security boundary.

### Build separate safety controls for every AI area

- **Good**, because each capability controls its own safeguards.
- **Bad**, because fixes must be repeated and rules may drift apart.

### Assume one shared package protects every AI route

- **Good**, because adding a route appears faster initially.
- **Bad**, because direct AI calls or missed registration can bypass safeguards.
