## Context and problem statement

Book discovery needs fast search results and current rating summaries. The original book information and individual reviews remain stored in their respective owning areas. Search indexes and summaries are helpful copies, not the original source.

BookWorm needs a clear rule that distinguishes original information from copies prepared for faster access.

## Decision drivers

- **Fast reading:** Optimize search and summaries without moving ownership of the original information.
- **Reliable decisions:** Checkout and changes must use the authoritative source, not an outdated copy.
- **Clear updates:** Make delayed updates and recovery steps understandable.

## Considered options

1. Keep fast-access copies separate and allow short update delays
2. Treat search indexes and summaries as the original source
3. Update every copy immediately during every original change
4. Check every original record for every search

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: **Keep fast-access copies separate and allow short update delays**.

BookWorm will prepare search indexes and rating summaries in the background. These copies may be briefly stale, but they can be rebuilt without changing the original information.

The responsible area remains the source for purchase checks and important actions. Search displays and summaries remain supplementary until their updates catch up.

### Consequences

- **Positive:** Search and summaries can be rebuilt independently without changing original records.
- **Negative:** Customers may briefly see outdated rankings or totals.
- **Follow-up:** Monitor update delays and the success of rebuilding tasks.

## Pros and cons of the options

### Keep fast-access copies separate and allow short update delays

- **Good**, because search tools can handle heavy reading without slowing original updates.
- **Good**, because copies can be recreated when they become incorrect.
- **Bad**, because customer views must tolerate temporary delays.

### Treat search indexes and summaries as the original source

- **Good**, because one location serves both reading and writing.
- **Bad**, because a copy may lose history or cannot resolve changes safely.

### Update every copy immediately during every original change

- **Good**, because copies stay current immediately.
- **Bad**, because an outage in one copy can block essential updates.

### Check every original record for every search

- **Good**, because results are always current.
- **Bad**, because checking several areas makes browsing slower and limits advanced search.
