## Context and problem statement

BookWorm describes its structure in one place and records decisions in another. Copying the full explanation into both places can leave them out of date when a choice changes.

BookWorm needs one authoritative record for each decision, with an index that only helps readers find it.

## Decision drivers

- **Traceability:** Give readers one place to review the reason for a decision and any later replacement.
- **No duplication:** Prevent conflicting descriptions and statuses.
- **Clear scope:** Separate whole-system choices from choices that belong to one specific area.

## Considered options

1. Keep one authoritative record per decision and index those records centrally
2. Copy full explanations into the overall design chapters
3. Put every decision in one ungrouped folder
4. Keep decisions only inside individual area folders

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: **Keep one authoritative record per decision and index those records centrally**.

Whole-system decisions remain separate from system-specific decisions. The index shows which decisions are active and which have been replaced.

When an area replaces a previous choice, maintainers add a new record and link it to the old one instead of rewriting history.

### Consequences

- **Positive:** Readers find one clear record with its current status and history.
- **Negative:** Adding or replacing a decision requires updating both the record and the index.
- **Follow-up:** Keep the index and active decisions current and mark reconstructed decisions as proposed until approved.

## Pros and cons of the options

### Keep one authoritative record per decision and index those records centrally

- **Good**, because every decision has one clear location and replacement history.
- **Good**, because whole-system choices are not buried inside one area's records.
- **Bad**, because contributors must maintain both the record and index.

### Copy full explanations into the overall design chapters

- **Good**, because readers can see every decision on one page.
- **Bad**, because duplicate explanations quickly become inconsistent.

### Put every decision in one ungrouped folder

- **Good**, because every decision is easy to locate by folder.
- **Bad**, because whole-system and area-specific decisions lose their clear scope.

### Keep decisions only inside individual area folders

- **Good**, because local teams manage their own decisions.
- **Bad**, because whole-system decisions become fragmented and replacements may be missed.
